Medians over the 39 regions swept, which is fewer than the 40 the rest of the paper uses (\texttt{bicubic} on 38, \texttt{linear} on 38, the rest on all). Each row rebuilds the carrier tile from the same native window with a different kernel and the same reading operator, so differences between rows are the transfer alone. Two library names mislead: \texttt{linear} is an inverse-distance-weighted average over four neighbours, not a linear interpolant, and \texttt{bicubic} is Keys' cubic convolution, whose signed kernel rings outside the local sample range by design; its row is reported for completeness, not as a finding. \emph{Transfer} is the error against an analytic field known at every position, as a percentage of the field's standard deviation; it is the only column that gives a transfer no credit for copying its input samples. \emph{Over} is the percentage of cells placed outside the range of the data the transfer was given, which detects a diverging interpolant that the constant-field check passes. \emph{Floor} is the RMSE of the uncompressed tile read on the native pixels over the two footprints' intersection, as a fraction of the scene's standard deviation, and \emph{rel.} its per-region ratio to \texttt{clough\_tocher}; both reward a transfer for staying close to the identity on the native lattice and are reported for that reason, not as a ranking. \emph{log-PSD} is the RMS distance in $\log_{10}$ between the projected tile's radial power spectrum and the observation's up to the native Nyquist, and \emph{top oct.} the ratio of their power in the highest octave alone: above 1 the transfer leaves more high-frequency power than the observation carries. The last columns are per-region compressed-size ratios to the same reference at three RMSE targets.
