\subsection{The Comparison Under a Fixed Codec}
\label{sec:same_codec}

Tables~\ref{tab:synthesis} and~\ref{tab:error_at_size} let the codec
be chosen for each region, grid and target, and the answer is an
envelope: no archive selects its codec region by region after the fact.
Table~\ref{tab:fixed_codec} holds the codec fixed on both grids, the
controlled and operational form of the comparison, which separates
whether a grid compresses better from whether the codecs that suit it
best differ from those that suit the other. Each cell is the median
per-region HPX/UTM compressed-size ratio at that target with the
\emph{same} codec on both grids, the UTM side carrying
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$, and the number of qualifying regions in
parentheses; a region enters a cell only if that codec brackets the
target with two measured points on both grids. One reporting rule
differs here on purpose: this is a codec-by-codec sensitivity table, so
every cell is shown with its own $n$ and none is withheld, even where
fewer than three quarters of the regions qualify (at the loosest target
JPEG~XL qualifies on 26 regions and JPEG~2000 on 30). The verdicts of
Sections~\ref{sec:rd_main}--\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} rest on the
withheld rule; this table is read beside them.

Two observations follow. First, the codec ranking and the grid comparison
are different results. JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL lead on both grids alike
(Section~\ref{sec:codec_choice}), and at $10^{-2}$ the oracle selects one
of the two on 30 of the 32 qualifying regions on the HEALPix side and on
31 on the native side, so the oracle ratios are close to what either
codec gives on its own: $0.77$ and $0.71$ against $0.72$ for the
envelope. Codec selection does not create the crossover. Second, the
crossover is codec-dependent in a way the envelope hides. Four of the
five codecs reproduce it, JPEG~XL and JPEG~2000 most strongly ($0.71$ and
$0.77$ at $10^{-2}$, $p<10^{-7}$), then DWT+Zstandard ($0.81$) and SZ3
($0.93$, $p=0.02$). ZFP does not: under ZFP the HEALPix tile costs
$1.54$--$1.72$ times the native product at every target, with no decline
as the target loosens. This behaviour is consistent with ZFP's
fixed-accuracy block coding and its different rate-allocation strategy,
and the experiment does not isolate the codec mechanism responsible for
the difference; SZ3, which also reproduces the crossover, is
prediction-based, so the successful family cannot be defined by the
transform alone. An archive that standardised on ZFP would see no regime
in which the carrier is the smaller representation; one that
standardised on any of the other four would see the regime of
Table~\ref{tab:synthesis}. The scope of the crossover is thus a
carrier filled by a transfer as smooth as Clough--Tocher
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}) \emph{and} coded by one of the four,
and the second condition binds as much as the first.
