\subsection{The Comparison Under a Fixed Codec}
\label{sec:same_codec}

Tables~\ref{tab:synthesis} and~\ref{tab:error_at_size} let the codec
be chosen for each region, grid and \term{target}, and the answer is an
envelope: no archive selects its codec region by region after the fact.
Table~\ref{tab:fixed_codec} holds the codec fixed on both grids, the
operational form of the comparison. Each cell is the median per-region
HPX/UTM compressed-\term{size ratio} at that \term{target} with the \emph{same} codec
on both grids, the UTM side carrying $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$, with the
number of qualifying regions in parentheses. A region enters a cell only
if that codec brackets the \term{target} with two measured points on both
grids. This is a codec-by-codec sensitivity table, so every cell is
shown with its own $n$ and none is withheld, even where fewer than three
quarters of the regions qualify. At the loosest \term{target} JPEG~XL qualifies
on 26 regions and JPEG~2000 on 30. The verdicts of
Sections~\ref{sec:rd_main}--\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} rest on the
withheld rule.

Two observations follow. First, the codec ranking and the grid comparison
are different results. JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL lead on both grids alike
(Section~\ref{sec:codec_choice}). At $10^{-2}$ the oracle selects one
of the two on 30 of the 32 qualifying regions on the HEALPix side and on
31 on the native side. The oracle ratios are therefore close to what
either codec gives on its own: $0.77$ and $0.71$ against $0.72$ for the
envelope. Codec selection does not create the crossover. Second, the
crossover is codec-dependent in a way the envelope hides. Four of the
five codecs show the same directional crossover: JPEG~XL and JPEG~2000
most strongly ($0.71$ and $0.77$ at $10^{-2}$, $p<10^{-7}$), then
DWT+Zstandard ($0.81$) and SZ3 ($0.93$, $p=0.02$). These codec-specific
$p$-values are descriptive and uncorrected, since this table is a
sensitivity analysis and not a family of tests the paper draws a verdict
from. Under ZFP the HEALPix tile costs $1.54$--$1.72$ times the native
product at every \term{target}, with no decline as the \term{target} loosens. This is
consistent with ZFP's fixed-accuracy block coding and its rate
allocation, and the experiment does not isolate the mechanism. SZ3,
which reproduces the crossover, is prediction-based, so the successful
family cannot be defined by the transform alone. An archive that
standardised on ZFP would see no regime in which the \term{carrier} is the
smaller representation. The scope of the crossover is thus a \term{carrier}
filled by the Clough--Tocher \term{transfer}, of those tested
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}), \emph{and} coded by one of the other
four, and the second condition binds as much as the first.
