\subsection{Rate--Distortion Comparison: the Overhead-Free Ratio and the Critical Overhead}
\label{sec:rd_main}

Three quantities carry the result, in an order that introduces no
assumption before it is needed. The first is the overhead-free HEALPix/UTM
size ratio at equal reconstruction error ($\gamma=1$), a property of the
two grids and of the transfer that filled one of them. The second is the
same ratio read as a decision threshold: the distortion-dependent
\emph{critical overhead} $\gamma_\star(e)$, the native-product overhead
above which the carrier becomes the smaller representation at error $e$.
This is the general result, and it is a curve. The third is one point on
that curve, the illustrative $30\%$ overhead ($\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$)
of Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative}.

At $\gamma=1$, native UTM is the more compressible representation at four
of the five RMSE targets, by a margin that falls monotonically as the
target loosens: $+106\%$, $+94\%$, $+59\%$ and $+19\%$ from $10^{-4}$ to
$3\times10^{-3}$. At $10^{-2}$ the sign changes: the Clough--Tocher tile is
$7\%$ smaller than the native square with no overhead on either side
(median $0.93$ over the 32 regions whose contrast admits the target,
$p=0.03$ before correction). The $2.58$ of the uncompressed tile is never
seen again once a codec is involved.

Writing this gap as
$\gamma_\star(e)=S_{\mathrm{HPX}}(e)/S_{\mathrm{UTM,crop}}(e)$ is an
accounting identity; the change of sign at $\gamma=\gamma_\star$ explains
nothing about why the gap narrows with distortion, and what the identity
gives is a threshold a product designer can compare with a measured
overhead. Table~\ref{tab:gamma_continuous} reports $\gamma_\star$ at every
target. It falls from $2.06$ to $0.93$ and reaches $1.19$ at
$3\times10^{-3}$, below the illustrative $1.30$, which is why the measured
ratio at that target is $0.92$ and the paired test does not separate the
grids there. At the three tightest targets $\gamma_\star$ runs from $1.59$
to $2.06$, overheads we know of no measurement supporting, and at the
tightest target native UTM stays ahead for three quarters of the regions
even at $\gamma=2$. At $3\times10^{-3}$ a native product organised with
less than $19\%$ of overhead would restore the advantage of UTM. At
$10^{-2}$, $\gamma_\star=0.93$ lies below one, so the carrier is already
the smaller representation at equal layout overhead ($\gamma=1$) and stays
so for any relative overhead $\gamma\ge0.93$; a native product organised
more compactly than the carrier by more than $7\%$ would still be the
smaller of the two. What the result removes at that target is the need to
assume an overhead in the carrier's favour. $\gamma_\star$ has not been
recomputed for the matched-budget protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

In Table~\ref{tab:gamma_continuous}, each region's $\gamma_\star$ is the
value at which the two representations reach equal compressed size for
that region, so HEALPix is the smaller one exactly where
$\gamma \ge \gamma_\star$. The win columns give the fraction of the $n$
regions that qualify at that target --- 40, 40, 39, 37 and 32 as the
target loosens --- satisfying that at three illustrative values, and
\emph{inconclusive} the range of $\gamma$ over which the paired
signed-rank test does not separate the grids at the $5\%$ level.

Because the overhead enters as one multiplicative factor, every statement
about it is a function of one per-region quantity: HEALPix is smaller
exactly where $\gamma\ge\gamma_\star$, and the paired test at overhead
$\gamma$ is the signed-rank test on $\log(\gamma_\star/\gamma)$. Verdict,
significance and spread are all read from the empirical distribution of
$\gamma_\star$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:verdict_vs_gamma}). With an overhead
$\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$ on the carrier and $\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}$ on the
native product, only the ratio $\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$
matters, which is why the figure extends below $1$: there the carrier
carries the heavier container, and at the looser targets a fifth to a
third of the regions already have their critical overhead in that range.
One component of $\gamma$ is measurable: the container format accounts for
at most half a percent on either side (Appendix~\ref{app:container}). A
$30\%$ overhead therefore describes granule geometry (padding, overlap,
per-granule metadata), a property of a product layout, which is why it is
carried as a parameter.

No single summary statement is available. A reader who assumes no
overhead ($\gamma=1$) finds HEALPix smaller for $2\%$ to $69\%$ of the
regions depending on the target; one who accepts the illustrative $1.30$
finds $2\%$ to $94\%$; one who assumes the native layout costs twice what
the carrier does finds $28\%$ to $100\%$. Only at the tightest target is
the answer robust over the whole plausible range in favour of UTM, and
only at the loosest in favour of HEALPix. Each target also has a band of
$\gamma$, narrow at the extremes and $1.35$--$1.73$ at $10^{-3}$, within
which the two grids cannot be separated; a reader whose product falls in
that band should draw no conclusion from this comparison. The eight
regions absent at $10^{-2}$ are those whose scene contrast does not exceed
the target (Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}); keeping them as bounds would
leave the ratio at the illustrative overhead within $[0.69, 0.74]$
(Appendix~\ref{app:censoring}).
