\subsection{Rate--Distortion Comparison: the Overhead-Free Ratio and the Critical Overhead}
\label{sec:rd_main}

Three quantities carry the result of this paper. They are reported in an
order such that no assumption is introduced before it is needed. The first
is the overhead-free HEALPix/UTM size ratio at equal reconstruction error, with
no overhead assigned to either product ($\gamma=1$); it is a property of the
two grids and of the transfer that populated one of them. The second is the
same ratio read as a decision threshold, the distortion-dependent
\emph{critical overhead} $\gamma_\star(e)$, defined as the native-product
overhead above which the carrier becomes the smaller representation at error
$e$. This is the general result, and it is a curve rather than a verdict.
The third is one point on that curve, an illustrative $30\%$ granule
overhead ($\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$), reported in
Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} because a concrete operating point is
useful, and reported after the curve because it is not the result.

At $\gamma=1$, native UTM is the more compressible representation at four
of the five RMSE targets, by a margin that decreases monotonically as the
target loosens: $+106\%$, $+94\%$, $+59\%$ and $+19\%$ from $10^{-4}$ to
$3\times10^{-3}$. At $10^{-2}$ the sign changes: the Clough--Tocher tile
is $7\%$ smaller than the native square with no overhead charged to either
side (median $0.93$ over the 32 regions whose contrast admits the target,
$p=0.03$ before correction). In plain terms, the compressed HEALPix tile is
twice the size of the compressed native square when almost no error is
tolerated, and slightly smaller than it when a quarter of the scene
contrast is; the $2.58$ of the uncompressed tile is never reached again
once a codec is involved, and at the loosest target the oversampling
penalty is more than recovered.

Restating this gap as
$\gamma_\star(e)=S_{\mathrm{HPX}}(e)/S_{\mathrm{UTM,crop}}(e)$ is an
accounting identity rather than an explanation. The change of sign at
$\gamma=\gamma_\star$ is tautological and says nothing about why the gap
narrows with distortion; what the identity provides is a threshold that a
product designer can compare with a measured overhead.
Table~\ref{tab:gamma_continuous} reports $\gamma_\star$ at every target. It
decreases from $2.06$ to $0.93$ and reaches $1.19$ at $3\times10^{-3}$,
below the illustrative $1.30$, which is why the measured ratio at that
target is $0.92$ and the paired test does not separate the grids there. At
the three tightest targets $\gamma_\star$ ranges from $1.59$ to $2.06$,
overheads that we are not aware of any measurement supporting, and at the
tightest target native UTM remains ahead for three quarters of the regions
even at $\gamma=2$. At the two loosest targets $\gamma_\star$ falls to
$1.19$ and $0.93$: at $3\times10^{-3}$ a native product organised with
less than $19\%$ of overhead would restore the advantage of UTM, and at
$10^{-2}$ the carrier is the smaller representation before any overhead is
charged to the native product, so that no layout assumption is needed for
the verdict there, only the tolerated error. The value of $\gamma_\star$
has not been recomputed for the matched-budget protocol, which would
require repeating the budget interpolation at $\gamma=1$
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).


Table~\ref{tab:gamma_continuous} maps that assumption to its consequence.
Each region's critical overhead $\gamma_\star$ is the value at which the
two representations reach equal compressed size for that region, so
HEALPix is the smaller of the two exactly where $\gamma \ge \gamma_\star$;
the win columns give the fraction of the 40 regions satisfying that at
three illustrative values, and \emph{inconclusive} the range of $\gamma$
over which the paired signed-rank test does not separate the grids at the
$5\%$ level. No value of $\gamma$ is privileged here.

Because the overhead enters as a single multiplicative factor, every
statement about it is a function of one per-region quantity. The
overhead-free ratio of a region is its $\gamma_\star$; HEALPix is the smaller
representation exactly where $\gamma\ge\gamma_\star$, and the paired test at
overhead $\gamma$ is the signed-rank test on $\log(\gamma_\star/\gamma)$.
Verdict, significance and spread are therefore all read from the empirical
distribution of $\gamma_\star$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:verdict_vs_gamma}). Reading
$\gamma$ in this way also removes an asymmetry. With an overhead
$\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$ on the carrier and $\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}$ on the
native product, the only parameter that matters is the ratio
$\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$, which is why the figure
extends below $1$: there the carrier carries the heavier container, and a
fifth to a third of the regions already have their critical overhead in
that range at the looser targets. One component of $\gamma$ is measurable,
and Appendix~\ref{app:container} measures it: the container format accounts
for at most half a percent on either side, so that a $30\%$ overhead
describes granule geometry (padding, overlap, per-granule metadata), a
property of a product layout, which is why it is carried as a parameter.

The table shows that no single summary statement is available. A reader
who assumes no overhead ($\gamma=1$) finds HEALPix smaller for
$2\%$ to $69\%$ of the regions depending on the target; one who accepts
the illustrative $1.30$ finds $2\%$ to $94\%$; one who assumes that the
native layout costs twice what the carrier does finds $28\%$ to $100\%$.
Only at the tightest target is the answer robust over the whole plausible
range in favour of UTM, and only at the loosest is it robust in favour of
HEALPix. Each target also has a band of $\gamma$, narrow at the extremes
and $1.35$--$1.73$ at $10^{-3}$, within which the two grids cannot be
separated at all; a reader whose product falls in that band should draw no
conclusion from this comparison rather than read off a ratio. The $n$
column of the table records that the loosest targets are established on
fewer regions than the tightest: the eight regions absent at $10^{-2}$ are
those whose scene contrast does not exceed the target
(Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}), and Appendix~\ref{app:censoring} shows
that keeping them as bounds would leave the ratio at the illustrative
overhead within $[0.69, 0.74]$.
