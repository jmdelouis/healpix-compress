\subsection{Rate--Distortion Comparison: the Overhead-Free Ratio and the Critical Overhead}
\label{sec:rd_main}

Three quantities carry the result. The first is the HEALPix/UTM size
ratio at equal reconstruction error with no \term{overhead} on either side
($\gamma=1$). It is a property of the two grids and of the \term{transfer} that
filled one of them. The second is the same ratio read as a decision
threshold: the \emph{critical overhead} $\gamma_\star(e)$, the
native-product \term{overhead} above which the \term{carrier} becomes the smaller
representation at error $e$. This is the general result, and it is a
curve. The third is one point on that curve, the illustrative $30\%$
\term{overhead} ($\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$) of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative}. The order introduces no assumption
before it is needed.

At $\gamma=1$, native UTM is the more compressible representation at four
of the five \term{RMSE targets}. Its margin falls as the \term{target} loosens:
$+106\%$, $+94\%$, $+59\%$ and $+19\%$ from $10^{-4}$ to
$3\times10^{-3}$. At $10^{-2}$ the sign changes. There the Clough--Tocher
\term{tile} is $7\%$ smaller than the \term{native square} (median $0.93$ over the 32
regions whose contrast admits the \term{target}; $95\%$ confidence interval
$0.89$--$0.99$; $p=0.03$ before correction; 10 of the 32 regions lie
above parity). The advantage is small and statistically marginal: the
confidence interval stops just short of parity. It also depends on the
\term{reference}. Here each grid is scored against its own raw raster. The
Clough--Tocher \term{transfer} has already removed high-frequency noise that a
codec would otherwise spend bits on, while the native side keeps that
noise. Section~\ref{sec:common_reference} charges the smoothing to the
\term{carrier}, and the sign reverses. Section~\ref{sec:support_controls} gives
the native side the same smoothing instead, and the \term{carrier} is then
$1.24$ times the native raster at this \term{target}. A smaller confound runs
the other way at the tight \term{targets}. The native product is distributed
as integers and already sits on the coding lattice; the interpolated
\term{carrier} does not, so it pays a quantisation the native side never pays
(Appendix~\ref{app:codec_audit}). The $2.58$ of the uncompressed \term{tile} is
never seen again once a codec is involved.

Writing this gap as
$\gamma_\star(e)=S_{\mathrm{HPX}}(e)/S_{\mathrm{UTM,crop}}(e)$ is an
accounting identity. The change of sign at $\gamma=\gamma_\star$ explains
nothing about why the gap narrows with distortion. What it gives is a
threshold that a product designer can compare with a measured \term{overhead}.
The \term{overhead} enters as one multiplicative factor, so every statement
about it reduces to one per-region quantity: HEALPix is smaller exactly
where $\gamma\ge\gamma_\star$, and the paired test at \term{overhead} $\gamma$ is
the signed-rank test on $\log(\gamma_\star/\gamma)$. If the \term{carrier}
carries an \term{overhead} $\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$ and the native product
$\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}$, only the ratio
$\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$ matters. This is why
Fig.~\ref{fig:verdict_vs_gamma} extends below $1$: there the \term{carrier}
carries the heavier container, and at the looser targets a fifth to a
third of the regions already have their \term{critical overhead} in that range.
Two components of $\gamma$ are known. The container format is measured
in bytes and accounts for at most half a percent on either side
(Appendix~\ref{app:container}). The $21\%$ of sample area that the
Sentinel-2 tiling duplicates is known as a geometry
(Section~\ref{sec:overhead}). Padding and per-granule metadata depend on
the product layout. That is why $\gamma$ is carried as a parameter and
$30\%$ is an illustration.

Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} reports $\gamma_\star$ at every \term{target}. Each
region's value is the \term{overhead} at which the two representations reach
equal compressed size; the table gives the median over the $n$ regions
that qualify (40, 40, 39, 37 and 32 as the \term{target} loosens). It falls
from $2.06$ to $0.93$. At $3\times10^{-3}$ it reaches $1.19$, below the
illustrative $1.30$; this is why the measured ratio at that \term{target} is
$0.92$ and why the paired test does not separate the grids there. At the
three tightest \term{targets} $\gamma_\star$ runs from $1.59$ to $2.06$,
\term{overheads} we know of no measurement supporting. At the tightest \term{target}
native UTM stays ahead for three quarters of the regions even at
$\gamma=2$. At $3\times10^{-3}$ a native product organised with less
than $19\%$ of \term{overhead} would restore the advantage of UTM. The
Sentinel-2 tiling duplicates about $21\%$ of the nominal sample area
(Section~\ref{sec:overhead}), which is above that value. What the
duplication costs in compressed bytes is not measured here, and the
$19\%$ threshold sits inside the band where the paired test separates
nothing. At $10^{-2}$, $\gamma_\star=0.93$ lies below one. The \term{carrier}
is already the smaller representation at equal layout \term{overhead} and stays
so for any relative \term{overhead} $\gamma\ge0.93$; a native product organised
more compactly than the \term{carrier} by more than $7\%$ would still be the
smaller of the two. What the result removes at that \term{target} is the need
to assume an \term{overhead} in the \term{carrier}'s favour. $\gamma_\star$ has not
been recomputed for the \term{matched-budget} protocol
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The column
$f(e)=(2.58-\gamma_\star(e))/(2.58-1)$ is the share of the raw
oversampling penalty that compression recovers at error $e$: $0.33$ at
the tightest \term{target} and $1.04$ at the loosest, where the \term{carrier} has
passed parity. The win columns give the fraction of the $n$ regions for
which HEALPix is smaller at three illustrative \term{overheads}.
\emph{Inconclusive} is the range of $\gamma$ over which the paired
signed-rank test does not separate the grids at the $5\%$ level.

No single summary statement is available. A reader who assumes no
\term{overhead} ($\gamma=1$) finds HEALPix smaller for $2\%$ to $69\%$ of the
regions, depending on the \term{target}. One who accepts the illustrative
$1.30$ finds $2\%$ to $94\%$. One who assumes the native layout costs
twice what the \term{carrier} does finds $28\%$ to $100\%$. Only at the tightest
\term{target} is the answer robust over the whole range of \term{overheads} examined
here in favour of UTM, and only at the loosest in favour of HEALPix. Each
\term{target} also has a band of $\gamma$ within which the two grids cannot be
separated, narrow at the extremes and $1.35$--$1.73$ at $10^{-3}$. The
eight regions absent at $10^{-2}$ are those whose scene contrast does
not exceed the \term{target} (Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}); keeping them as
bounds would leave the ratio at the illustrative \term{overhead} within
$[0.69, 0.74]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:censoring}).
