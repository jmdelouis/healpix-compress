\section{Conclusion}
This paper asked whether Sentinel-2 imagery stored on the HEALPix level next
finer than its native resolution, which avoids the nominal undersampling
implied by the \SI{12.4}{m} level-19 cell but oversamples the data by a
factor of $\approx2.58$ in cell area, costs more to store once compression
and the storage layout of the native product are both taken into account.
Using only established, independently published compressors (ZFP, SZ3,
JPEG~2000, JPEG~XL and a DWT+Zstandard baseline), no HEALPix-specific
coding, and an equal-area protocol that crops the native UTM raster to a
square of the same nominal ground area as each HEALPix tile, the answer on
40 regions spanning four land-cover classes and both HEALPix cell-shape
regimes separates into four statements at four levels of the chain.

\emph{Geometry.} Level 20 carries a fixed sampling-density penalty of
$2.584\times$ relative to the native \SI{10}{m} raster, exact and identical
for every region, which $30\%$ of native-layout overhead reduces to
$1.99\times$ but does not remove.

\emph{Compression.} That penalty is largely redundancy, and lossy coding
removes most of it (Table~\ref{tab:headline}). With no overhead charged to
either side, the Clough--Tocher tile is $2.06\times$ the native square at an
RMSE of $10^{-4}$ and $0.93\times$ at $10^{-2}$, the class medians lying
within $2.01$--$2.17$ and $0.89$--$1.10$; the gap therefore decreases
monotonically from $+106\%$ to $-7\%$, which is a critical overhead
$\gamma_\star$ falling from $2.06$ to $0.93$. The decrease is a property of
the sampling geometry rather than of scene content: an analytic field with
the spectrum and instrument width fitted to the imagery, sampled by both
lattices with no support mismatch, no content difference and no transfer,
reproduces its decline and its magnitude to within $8$--$27\%$, though the
residual is not attributed to any one of the three. At the illustrative
overhead $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$, native UTM remains smaller at the
three tightest RMSE targets and the two looser budgets (median ratios
$1.23$--$1.59$ and $1.33$--$4.01$), HEALPix is smaller at the loosest RMSE
target ($0.72$) and the tightest budget ($0.83$), and seven of the eight
paired tests survive the Holm correction; the exception is the
$3\times10^{-3}$ target, where the two grids are not separated.

\emph{Processing chain.} The crossover is a result for one transfer
operator and one class of codec, and does not survive the alternatives
tested. The carrier was populated with Clough--Tocher
interpolation, chosen before any storage figure was measured
(Section~\ref{sec:resampler}); rebuilding the same tiles with a PSF-aware or
an inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.4$ times the bytes at the
same coding RMSE against their own uncompressed carriers, and leaves
HEALPix ahead at no target
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}). For an archive the distinction is
concrete: a Clough--Tocher carrier compressed to a quarter of the scene
contrast is about the size of the native product, whereas a PSF-aware
carrier at the same error is about $2.2$ times it, and what the archive
buys for the difference is a sharper product rather than a smaller one.
Only a few percent of this cost is the price of deconvolution; the remainder is the price of leaving a $C^1$
curvature-minimising interpolant, whose output a lossy coder can discard far
more of at equal error, and the observations are consistent with energy
compaction rather than total information content as the mechanism. The
observed crossover is therefore a result for a carrier populated by a
transfer in the smoothness class represented here by Clough--Tocher, and
should not be read as a transfer-independent property of HEALPix: with no
overhead charged to either side the PSF-aware tile stays at $2.2$--$2.7$
times the native square over the whole error range and the
inverse-distance tile at $2.1$--$2.5$, against $2.06$ falling to $0.93$ for
Clough--Tocher. On the
codec side, JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL lead on both grids alike, which we
interpret as a property of these codecs on this imagery rather than of the
quadtree; held fixed on both grids, four of the five reproduce the
regime-dependent verdict and ZFP, whose fixed-accuracy block transform
cannot decline to spend bits on redundancy, does not
(Section~\ref{sec:same_codec}).

The verdict also depends on which reconstruction reference is accepted, and
both are reported. Against the same Sentinel-2 observation, the uncompressed
carrier already differs from the native product by $4.0\times10^{-3}$ in
median under the nearest-cell read, about $10\%$ of the standard deviation
of a scene, and no swept configuration reaches four of the five RMSE targets
against it; the $2\%$-budget ratio changes from $0.83$ to $1.44$ in favour
of native UTM. Much of this floor belongs to the reading operator rather
than to the grid: a read that interpolates within the carrier lattice
removes $66\%$ of its mean squared error on the same tiles
(Table~\ref{tab:reader_floor}), and when the sweep is re-scored through that
read the lead of native UTM at the $2\%$ budget narrows to $1.12$ without
reversing, while the loosest RMSE target, the only one that read makes
reportable, turns in favour of HEALPix ($0.93$ at the illustrative overhead,
$1.20$ overhead-free). The residual floor of $1.9\times10^{-3}$ is what the
transfer and the interpolating read cost together. Read against the latent
scene instead, the reference that applies when the carrier is used as if it
were the scene, HEALPix becomes the smaller representation while the
compression error is still $0.13$ to $0.50$ of the error the product already
carries, in three of the four scene classes
(Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). The practical conclusion is conditional
and should be stated as such: a HEALPix carrier is competitive at aggressive
compression when the HEALPix cube is itself the reference product, and is
not, at any storage budget, when the native product remains the ground truth
and the transfer error is charged to it.

\emph{Product architecture.} Whether the carrier is the smaller archive is
therefore not a property of either grid but a comparison between two
measured quantities: the overhead the native product actually carries, and
$\gamma_\star(e)$ at the distortion the archive tolerates. At $10^{-2}$ that
threshold has fallen below one, so the carrier is smaller before any
overhead is charged; at $10^{-4}$ it stands at $2.06$, an overhead we are
not aware of any measurement supporting.

In summary, oversampling at HEALPix level 20 introduces a large raw storage
penalty, but most of that penalty is redundancy that lossy compression
removes, and whether the carrier becomes operationally competitive is
determined jointly by the tolerated distortion, the transfer that populates
it, the codec and reading operator through which it is used, and the
relative storage overhead of the two layouts. The
critical-overhead formulation expresses this trade-off without privileging
any storage architecture, and it is the form in which we consider the
result should be used. The evidence is from Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m},
one acquisition per region, and should not be transferred to multispectral
products, temporal cubes, model output or flux-conservative fields without
being re-measured there. The most important next step is to decouple the
three parts of the chain that this paper has shown to matter, namely the
transfer, the carrier and the reading operator, and to evaluate the
complete chain with the operational PSF-aware transfer and a reader matched
to it on the same sites, which is what the pipeline shared with the
companion study~\cite{delouis2026resample} was built for.
