\section{Conclusion}
This paper asked whether Sentinel-2 imagery stored on the HEALPix level
next finer than its native resolution costs more to store once
compression and the storage layout of the native product are both
counted. That level oversamples the data by $\approx2.58$ in cell area.
With established compressors only, no HEALPix-specific coding, and an
equal-area protocol on 40 regions in four land-cover classes, the answer
separates into four statements.

\emph{Geometry.} Level 20 carries a fixed sampling-density penalty of
$2.584\times$ relative to the native \SI{10}{m} raster, exact for every
region, which $30\%$ of native-layout overhead reduces to $1.99\times$.

\emph{Compression.} Most of that penalty is redundancy that lossy coding
removes (Table~\ref{tab:synthesis}). With no overhead charged to either
side, the Clough--Tocher tile falls from $2.06\times$ the \term{native square}
at an RMSE of $10^{-4}$ to $0.93\times$ at $10^{-2}$, so the critical
overhead $\gamma_\star$ falls with it. Two controls say where that
decline comes from. An analytic field sampled by both lattices with no
\term{transfer} follows it to within $5$--$24\%$. When the native side is
given a strong Clough--Tocher smoothing control, the \term{carrier} costs
$2.23$ to $1.24$ times the \term{native square}. That is within $7\%$ of the
control.
The controls show that transfer-induced smoothing is sufficient to
account for the remaining crossover under the tested conditions, and in
these controls geometry alone does not cross parity.
The coarser level-19 \term{carrier} is smaller, at $0.67$ to $0.78$ of the
\term{native square}, but carries a transfer-and-read floor about $2.25$ times
that of level 20.

\emph{Processing chain.} The crossover holds for one \term{transfer} and is
codec-dependent: four of the five codecs tested reproduce the
regime-dependent pattern, while ZFP does not. Rebuilding the same tiles
with a PSF-aware or an
inverse-distance \term{transfer} costs $1.2$ to $2.5$ times the bytes at the
same coding RMSE and leaves HEALPix ahead at no \term{target}
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}). This is a price, not a verdict on
the PSF-aware \term{transfer}: its tile holds content the native raster
does not, and the bytes pay for that content. The PSF/IDW differential
is only $3$--$13\%$, so deconvolution alone is unlikely to explain the
two-fold surcharge. The split survives when the codec is held fixed on both grids,
and the four that reproduce the pattern span wavelet, multiscale and
prediction-based designs (Section~\ref{sec:same_codec}).

\emph{Reference.} Against the same Sentinel-2 observation, the
uncompressed \term{carrier} already differs from the native product by
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median under the nearest-cell read, about $10\%$ of
a scene's standard deviation. The $2\%$-budget ratio then moves from
$0.83$ to $1.44$ in favour of native UTM. A \term{read} that interpolates within
the \term{carrier} lattice removes $66\%$ of that floor's mean squared error.
Re-scored through it, the lead of native UTM at the $2\%$ budget narrows
to $1.12$ without reversing. The size advantage of HEALPix at the
$10^{-2}$ \term{target} survives under that read and the illustrative
overhead, at $0.93$ (Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}). The change
of \term{reference} therefore removes the \term{matched-budget} advantage
and keeps the loose-\term{target} size advantage. This
\term{reference} applies to the Clough--Tocher \term{carrier}, which aims at the
observation; the PSF-aware \term{carrier} aims at the latent field, and its
comparison at equal reconstruction error awaits a synthetic scene with a
known latent field. A HEALPix
\term{carrier} is therefore competitive at aggressive compression when the
HEALPix cube is itself the \term{reference} product, while native UTM remains
ahead at all three reportable \term{matched-storage} budgets when the native
Sentinel-2 observation remains the \term{reference} field.

The competitive regime is a conditional result, not a general
recommendation for Sentinel-2 archives. It rests on 32 of the 40 regions
(six lakes and two smooth forests do not admit the \term{target}). It holds at
an error of about a quarter of a typical tile's contrast, and only under
the within-representation \term{reference}.

In summary, whether the \term{carrier} is the smaller archive is a comparison
between the overhead the native product carries and $\gamma_\star(e)$ at
the distortion the archive tolerates. The Sentinel-2 tiling duplicates
$21\%$ in sample area through tile overlap, at an unmeasured cost in
compressed bytes (Section~\ref{sec:overhead}). At $10^{-2}$ the
threshold has fallen just below one. The margin is small and only
marginally significant in the nominal paired test ($p=0.03$, $95\%$
confidence interval $0.89$--$0.99$), and it holds only under a \term{reference}
that charges the \term{transfer}'s smoothing to nobody. At $10^{-4}$ the
threshold stands at $2.06$, an overhead we know of no measurement
supporting. The evidence is from
Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m}, one acquisition and one tile per region.
The ratios should be re-measured before being applied to other bands,
sensors, temporal stacks, model output, flux-conservative fields or a
full-sphere product. Two measurements would sharpen the framework: the
compressed-byte overhead of a real tiled product, and a non-deconvolving
\term{transfer} as smooth as Clough--Tocher, which would separate smoothness
from interpolation class. The larger gain lies beyond this framework.
Each tile here was compressed alone, as the native product is. A
shared grid puts every date, band and sensor on the same cells, and the
nested index makes any spatial, temporal or spectral block a contiguous
chunk shaped to the read pattern. Coding that coherence, first on global
model output where no \term{transfer} error enters, is the next step; the
present result is the floor it starts from.
