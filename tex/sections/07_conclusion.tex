\section{Conclusion}
This paper asked whether Sentinel-2 imagery stored on the HEALPix level
next finer than its native resolution, which oversamples the data by
$\approx2.58$ in cell area, costs more to store once compression and the
storage layout of the native product are both counted. With established
compressors only, no HEALPix-specific coding, and an equal-area protocol
on 40 regions in four land-cover classes and both HEALPix cell-shape
regimes, the answer separates into four statements.

\emph{Geometry.} Level 20 carries a fixed sampling-density penalty of
$2.584\times$ relative to the native \SI{10}{m} raster, exact for every
region, which $30\%$ of native-layout overhead reduces to $1.99\times$.

\emph{Compression.} That penalty is largely redundancy, and lossy coding
removes most of it (Table~\ref{tab:headline}). With no overhead charged to
either side, the Clough--Tocher tile is $2.06\times$ the native square at
an RMSE of $10^{-4}$ and $0.93\times$ at $10^{-2}$, the class medians
within $2.01$--$2.17$ and $0.89$--$1.10$; the gap falls monotonically
from $+106\%$ to $-7\%$, a critical overhead $\gamma_\star$ falling from
$2.06$ to $0.93$. An analytic field with the spectrum and instrument width
fitted to the imagery, sampled by both lattices with no support mismatch,
no content difference and no transfer, follows that decline to within
$8$--$27\%$, so sampling geometry reproduces rate--distortion behaviour of
this form and approximate magnitude; whether it accounts for it in full is
open. At the illustrative $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$,
native UTM stays smaller at the three tightest RMSE targets and the two
looser budgets (median ratios $1.23$--$1.59$ and $1.33$--$4.01$), HEALPix
is smaller at the loosest RMSE target ($0.72$) and the tightest budget
($0.83$), and seven of the eight paired tests survive the Holm correction,
the exception being the $3\times10^{-3}$ target.

\emph{Processing chain.} The crossover is a result for one transfer and
one class of codec. Rebuilding the same tiles with a PSF-aware or an
inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.4$ times the bytes at the same
coding RMSE against their own uncompressed carriers and leaves HEALPix
ahead at no target (Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}): with no overhead
charged, the PSF-aware tile stays at $2.3$--$2.6$ times the native square
over the whole error range and the inverse-distance tile at $2.1$--$2.5$,
against $2.06$ falling to $0.93$ for Clough--Tocher. The extra bytes go
with a transfer designed to recover a sharper latent-field estimand; what
it buys in reconstruction quality is established by the companion study
under its own conditions, and this paper measures its storage cost. The
PSF/IDW differential is only $4$--$9\%$, so deconvolution alone is
unlikely to explain the two-fold surcharge, and the observations are
consistent with energy compaction as the mechanism. On the codec side,
JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL lead on both grids alike; held fixed on both grids,
four of the five codecs reproduce the regime-dependent verdict, while ZFP
does not (Section~\ref{sec:same_codec}).

The verdict also depends on which reconstruction reference is accepted.
Against the same Sentinel-2 observation, the uncompressed carrier already
differs from the native product by $4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median under the
nearest-cell read, about $10\%$ of a scene's standard deviation; no swept
configuration reaches four of the five RMSE targets against it, and the
$2\%$-budget ratio moves from $0.83$ to $1.44$ in favour of native UTM. A
read that interpolates within the carrier lattice removes $66\%$ of that
floor's mean squared error (Table~\ref{tab:reader_floor}); re-scored
through it, the lead of native UTM at the $2\%$ budget narrows to $1.12$
without reversing, and the loosest RMSE target turns in favour of HEALPix
($0.93$ at the illustrative overhead, $1.20$ overhead-free). Against the
latent scene, the reference that applies when the carrier is used as if
it were the scene, HEALPix becomes the smaller representation while the
compression error is still $0.13$ to $0.50$ of the error the product
already carries, in all four scene classes, with a storage gain that is
substantial in three of them and about $2\%$ over water
(Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). The practical conclusion is
conditional: a HEALPix carrier is competitive at aggressive compression
when the HEALPix cube is itself the reference product, and at no storage
budget when the native product remains the ground truth and the transfer
error is charged to it.

\emph{Product architecture.} Whether the carrier is the smaller archive is
a comparison between two measured quantities: the overhead the native
product carries, and $\gamma_\star(e)$ at the distortion the archive
tolerates. At $10^{-2}$ that threshold has fallen just below one, though
by a margin the paired test does not resolve (Section~\ref{sec:rd_main});
at $10^{-4}$ it stands at $2.06$, an overhead we know of no measurement
supporting.

In summary, oversampling at HEALPix level 20 brings a large raw storage
penalty, most of it is redundancy that lossy compression removes, and
whether the carrier becomes competitive is set jointly by the tolerated
distortion, the transfer that fills it, the codec and reading operator
through which it is used, and the relative storage overhead of the two
layouts. The critical-overhead formulation expresses this trade-off
without privileging any storage architecture. The evidence is from
Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m}, one acquisition and one tile per region,
and the ratios should be re-measured before being applied to other bands,
sensors, multispectral cubes, temporal stacks, model output,
flux-conservative fields or a full-sphere product. The most important
next step is to evaluate the complete chain with the operational PSF-aware
transfer and a reader matched to it on the same sites, which is what the
pipeline shared with the companion study~\cite{delouis2026resample} was
built for.
