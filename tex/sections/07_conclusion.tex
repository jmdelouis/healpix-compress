\section{Conclusion}
This paper asked whether Sentinel-2 imagery stored on the HEALPix level
next finer than its native resolution, which oversamples the data by
$\approx2.58$ in cell area, costs more to store once compression and the
storage layout of the native product are both counted. With established
compressors only, no HEALPix-specific coding, and an equal-area protocol
on 40 regions in four land-cover classes, the answer separates into four
statements.

\emph{Geometry.} Level 20 carries a fixed sampling-density penalty of
$2.584\times$ relative to the native \SI{10}{m} raster, exact for every
region, which $30\%$ of native-layout overhead reduces to $1.99\times$.

\emph{Compression.} Most of that penalty is redundancy that lossy coding
removes (Table~\ref{tab:synthesis}). With no overhead charged to either
side, the Clough--Tocher tile falls from $2.06\times$ the native square
at an RMSE of $10^{-4}$ to $0.93\times$ at $10^{-2}$, so the critical
overhead $\gamma_\star$ falls with it. Two controls say where that
decline comes from. An analytic field sampled by both lattices with no
transfer follows it to within $5$--$24\%$; and when the native side is
given a strong Clough--Tocher smoothing control the carrier costs $2.23$
to $1.24$ times the native square, within $7\%$ of that control. The
controls show that transfer-induced smoothing is sufficient to account
for the remaining crossover under the tested conditions, and the grid
alone never crosses parity.
The coarser level-19 carrier is smaller, at $0.67$ to $0.78$ of the
native square, but carries a transfer-and-read floor about $2.25$ times
that of level 20.

\emph{Processing chain.} The crossover holds for one transfer and is
codec-dependent: four of the five codecs tested reproduce the
regime-dependent pattern, while ZFP does not. Rebuilding the same tiles
with a PSF-aware or an
inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.5$ times the bytes at the
same coding RMSE and leaves HEALPix ahead at no target
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}). The PSF/IDW differential is only
$3$--$13\%$, so deconvolution alone is unlikely to explain the two-fold
surcharge. The split survives when the codec is held fixed on both grids,
and the four that reproduce the pattern span wavelet, multiscale and
prediction-based designs (Section~\ref{sec:same_codec}).

\emph{Reference.} Against the same Sentinel-2 observation, the
uncompressed carrier already differs from the native product by
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median under the nearest-cell read, about $10\%$ of
a scene's standard deviation, and the $2\%$-budget ratio moves from
$0.83$ to $1.44$ in favour of native UTM. A read that interpolates within
the carrier lattice removes $66\%$ of that floor's mean squared error;
re-scored through it, the lead of native UTM at the $2\%$ budget narrows
to $1.12$ without reversing. The operational chain answers the same way:
the PSF-aware carrier read through the forward operator it was built for
needs $1.34$, $4.58$ and $43.5$ times the native product's error at the
$2\%$, $5\%$ and $10\%$ budgets, and reaches no RMSE target on three
quarters of the regions (Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}). A HEALPix
carrier is therefore competitive at aggressive compression when the
HEALPix cube is itself the reference product, while native UTM remains
ahead at all three reportable matched-storage budgets when the native
product remains the ground truth.

In summary, whether the carrier is the smaller archive is a comparison
between the overhead the native product carries and $\gamma_\star(e)$ at
the distortion the archive tolerates. The Sentinel-2 tiling duplicates
$21\%$ in sample area through tile overlap, at an unmeasured cost in
compressed bytes (Section~\ref{sec:overhead}). At $10^{-2}$ the threshold
has fallen just below one, by a margin that is small and only marginally
significant in the nominal paired test ($p=0.03$, $95\%$ confidence
interval $0.89$--$0.99$), and only under a reference that charges the
transfer's smoothing to nobody; at $10^{-4}$ it stands at $2.06$, an
overhead we know of no measurement supporting. The evidence is from
Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m}, one acquisition and one tile per region,
and the ratios should be re-measured before being applied to other bands,
sensors, temporal stacks, model output, flux-conservative fields or a
full-sphere product. Two measurements would sharpen the framework: the
compressed-byte overhead of a real tiled product, and a non-deconvolving
transfer as smooth as Clough--Tocher, which would separate smoothness
from interpolation class.
