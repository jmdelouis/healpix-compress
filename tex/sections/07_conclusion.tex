\section{Conclusion}
This paper asked whether Sentinel-2 imagery stored on the HEALPix level
next finer than its native resolution, which oversamples the data by
$\approx2.58$ in cell area, costs more to store once compression and the
storage layout of the native product are both counted. With established
compressors only, no HEALPix-specific coding, and an equal-area protocol
on 40 regions in four land-cover classes and both HEALPix cell-shape
regimes, the answer separates into four statements.

\emph{Geometry.} Level 20 carries a fixed sampling-density penalty of
$2.584\times$ relative to the native \SI{10}{m} raster, exact for every
region, which $30\%$ of native-layout overhead reduces to $1.99\times$.

\emph{Compression.} Most of that penalty is redundancy that lossy coding
removes (Table~\ref{tab:synthesis}). With no overhead charged to either
side, the Clough--Tocher tile is $2.06\times$ the native square at an
RMSE of $10^{-4}$ and $0.93\times$ at $10^{-2}$; the critical overhead
$\gamma_\star$ falls from $2.06$ to $0.93$. An analytic field with the
spectrum and instrument width fitted to the imagery, sampled by both
lattices with no transfer, follows that decline to within $5$--$24\%$;
with both sides equally smooth the carrier costs $2.23$ to $1.24$ times
the native square, within $7\%$ of that control at every target, so the
remainder is the smoothing the transfer gives the carrier. The coarser
level-19 carrier stays at $0.67$ to $0.78$ of the native square: the
finer carrier's cost is the cost of its density, large where the codec
keeps every sample and small where it keeps few. At the illustrative
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$, native UTM stays smaller at the three
tightest RMSE targets and the two looser budgets, HEALPix is smaller at
the loosest RMSE target ($0.72$) and the tightest budget ($0.83$), and
seven of the eight paired tests survive the Holm correction.

\emph{Processing chain.} The crossover is a result for one transfer and
one class of codec. Rebuilding the same tiles with a PSF-aware or an
inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.4$ times the bytes at the same
coding RMSE and leaves HEALPix ahead at no target
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}): the PSF-aware tile stays at
$2.3$--$2.6$ times the native square over the whole error range. The
extra bytes go with a transfer designed to recover a sharper latent-field
estimand, whose reconstruction benefit the companion study establishes
under its own conditions. The PSF/IDW differential is only $3$--$13\%$,
so deconvolution alone is unlikely to explain the two-fold surcharge, and
the observations are consistent with energy compaction playing an
important role. JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL lead on both grids alike; held
fixed on both grids, four of the five codecs reproduce the
regime-dependent verdict, while ZFP does not
(Section~\ref{sec:same_codec}).

\emph{Reference.} Against the same Sentinel-2 observation, the
uncompressed carrier already differs from the native product by
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median under the nearest-cell read, about $10\%$ of
a scene's standard deviation, and the $2\%$-budget ratio moves from
$0.83$ to $1.44$ in favour of native UTM. A read that interpolates within
the carrier lattice removes $66\%$ of that floor's mean squared error
(Table~\ref{tab:floors}); re-scored through it, the lead of native UTM
at the $2\%$ budget narrows to $1.12$ without reversing, and the loosest
RMSE target turns in favour of HEALPix ($0.93$ at the illustrative
overhead, $1.20$ overhead-free). Against the latent scene, HEALPix
becomes the smaller representation while the compression error is still
$0.13$ to $0.50$ of the error the product already carries
(Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). A HEALPix carrier is therefore
competitive at aggressive compression when the HEALPix cube is itself
the reference product, and at no storage budget when the native product
remains the ground truth and the transfer error is charged to it.

In summary, whether the carrier is the smaller archive is a comparison
between two measured quantities: the overhead the native product
carries, of which the Sentinel-2 tiling alone imposes $21\%$ through tile
overlap (Section~\ref{sec:overhead}), and $\gamma_\star(e)$ at the
distortion the archive tolerates, itself set by the transfer that fills
the carrier and the codec and reading operator through which it is used.
At $10^{-2}$ the threshold has fallen just below one, by a margin the
paired test does not resolve and only under a reference that charges the
transfer's smoothing to nobody; at $10^{-4}$ it stands at $2.06$, an
overhead we know of no measurement supporting. The evidence is from
Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m}, one acquisition and one tile per region,
and the ratios should be re-measured before being applied to other bands,
sensors, temporal stacks, model output, flux-conservative fields or a
full-sphere product. The most important next step is to evaluate the
complete chain with the operational PSF-aware transfer and a reader
matched to it on the same sites, which is what the pipeline shared with
the companion study~\cite{delouis2026resample} was built for.
