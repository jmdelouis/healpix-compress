\subsection{The Verdict Depends on Which Reference Is Accepted}
\label{sec:common_reference}

Every ratio above compares each grid with its own raw representation,
which charges the \term{carrier} nothing for the \term{transfer}. This section scores
both grids against the same Sentinel-2 observation. The decoded HEALPix
field is read on the native UTM pixels and compared with the observed
crop over the intersection of the two footprints. Over the 40 regions
the intersection is $74$--$83\%$ of the crop ($81\%$ median), and the
comparison is made on the $73$--$81\%$ that survives a three-pixel
erosion of its boundary. Projecting a constant field returns exactly one
everywhere on this area, the check required before any region is
accepted. Everything here depends on how the decoded \term{carrier} is read.
Section~\ref{sec:operator} shows that changing the \term{read} alone removes
two thirds of the floor's mean squared error. The sweep was therefore
scored twice: through the nearest-cell read used throughout the paper,
and through the interpolating \term{read} of that section. The UTM side is
identical under both.

The first consequence is a floor, which belongs to the transfer-and-read
pair. The first block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports the RMSE of the
\emph{uncompressed} level-20 \term{carrier} against the observation, read on
the native pixels of the intersection with the nearest \term{read}. It is
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median, about $10\%$ of the standard deviation of
the scene. It rises to $7.9\times10^{-3}$ over urban scenes and falls to
$8.1\times10^{-4}$ over water, following the high-frequency content as a
\term{transfer} step should. In the sweeps no codec setting on the HEALPix side
went more than $0.4\%$ below it under either \term{read}; it is the
error of the uncompressed chain, not a bound, since a coding error that
partly cancels the \term{transfer} error could go lower
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term}). The \term{read} alone accounts for an error of that size
(Section~\ref{sec:operator}), so the floor is an upper bound on what
the representation costs.

Under the nearest \term{read} four of the five \term{RMSE targets} lie beneath this
floor, and only the loosest qualifies on the $75\%$ of regions required
by Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}: $10^{-2}$ reaches $78\%$;
$3\times10^{-3}$, $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$ reach $30\%$, $18\%$ and
$5\%$; $10^{-4}$ is reachable nowhere. At $10^{-2}$ under this read the
median ratio is $1.09$ under the illustrative overhead ($1.42$
overhead-free) and the paired test does not separate the grids
($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.18$). Under the interpolating read the median
floor falls to $2.0\times10^{-3}$, and $10^{-2}$ is reported on $80\%$
of the regions. (The $66\%$ of Section~\ref{sec:operator} is a
paired per-region figure, a median RMSE ratio of $0.584$; the ratio of
the two medians, $0.49$, is a different statistic.) $3\times10^{-3}$ and $10^{-3}$ rise to $57\%$ and $25\%$ and
stay withheld, since the regions that would remain are the smoothest.

The second consequence is a reversal, the most important caveat of this
paper. Under the \term{common reference} and the nearest \term{read}, native UTM is
ahead at all three budgets and is not separated from HEALPix at the one
evaluable \term{RMSE target}. The
median RMSE ratios are $1.50$, $4.05$ and $32.5$ at the $2\%$, $5\%$ and
$10\%$ budgets, over the 40, 39 and 38 regions that qualify
(Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$ throughout), against $0.82$, $1.33$ and $4.02$
under the within-representation measure. The one HEALPix advantage at a budget, which
Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} already owes to the illustrative
overhead, reverses under the change
of \term{reference} for an arithmetic reason. At a $2\%$ budget the compression
error and the \term{transfer} error are of the same order, so a measure that
charges the second to HEALPix moves the balance by more than the codec
does. The interpolating \term{read} changes the size of this reversal, and at
one \term{target} its sign. The ratio at the $2\%$ budget falls from $1.50$ to
$1.12$ over all 40 regions, still in favour of native UTM (Holm-adjusted
$p<10^{-4}$). The $5\%$ and $10\%$ ratios fall from $4.05$ and $32.5$
to $2.50$ and $19.3$. At the one reportable \term{RMSE target}, $10^{-2}$,
HEALPix becomes the smaller representation under the illustrative
overhead: a median ratio of $0.93$ over the 32 qualifying regions
(Holm-adjusted $p=3\times10^{-3}$), where the nearest \term{read} gave $1.09$
and no separation. The overhead-free ratio there is
$1.20$, a \term{critical overhead} below the illustrative $1.30$. That median
coincides by chance with the overhead-free within-representation value
of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}; the two come from different sweeps,
\term{references} and overheads. The within-representation ratio at this
\term{target} ($0.72$) is never recovered, and the three budgets stay with
native UTM. Of the gap between the two \term{references} at the $2\%$ budget
($1.50$ against $0.82$ under the nearest \term{read}, $1.12$ against $0.82$
under the interpolating one), about half was the reader.

This \term{reference} is one-sided by construction. The observation is
the native raster itself, noise included, so the native side has no
floor, while the \term{carrier}'s is $2$ to $4\times10^{-3}$. At the
$5\%$ and $10\%$ budgets the native error lies far below that floor, and
the ratios there measure the floor more than the codecs; the $2\%$
budget and the $10^{-2}$ \term{target} are where both grids are limited
by coding.

Two results survive the switch of \term{reference}. JPEG~2000 leads by a wide
margin under both \term{references} and both \term{reads}, while JPEG~XL and SZ3
exchange second place. The latitude effect keeps its
direction: the equatorial belt stays more favourable to HEALPix than the
caps at the tightest budget (overhead-free ratios:
$1.63$ against $2.15$ under the nearest read, $1.30$ against $1.60$ under the interpolating one).
It passes the Holm correction of its family under the interpolating
read ($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.003$ at the tightest budget) and not under the
nearest ($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.11$), as expected if the jitter of the
nearest \term{read}, largest where cells are most anisotropic, was
masking it.

Both measures are therefore reported and neither is elected. If the
HEALPix cube is itself the product, the case a DGGS archive is built for,
the within-representation measure is the operative one and the
regime-dependent verdict of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main} stands. If the
native Sentinel-2 observation remains the \term{reference} product and the
HEALPix copy is a derived
convenience, the \term{transfer} error belongs in the accounting. What the two measures jointly
exclude is the unconditional claim that a HEALPix \term{carrier} is cheaper.
The Clough--Tocher \term{transfer} keeps the observation's own
resolution, so the observation is the field it aims at. The PSF-aware
\term{carrier} of Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost} aims at the latent
field before the instrument blur and is not scored here
(Section~\ref{sec:operator}); ranking the two \term{transfers} against
that latent field needs a synthetic scene in which it is known, and is
left to future work.

Two further questions about this \term{reference} are answered in
Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}: how large the compression error is at
the crossings of Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} as a fraction
$\kappa$ of the \term{transfer} error ($\kappa=0.23$ to $0.76$ against
the latent scene, in all four classes), and why the two errors do not
add in quadrature (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term}).
