\subsection{The Verdict Depends on Which Reference Is Accepted}
\label{sec:common_reference}

Every ratio above compares each grid with its own raw representation,
which charges the carrier nothing for the transfer. This section scores
both grids against the same Sentinel-2 observation. The decoded HEALPix
field is read on the native UTM pixels and compared with the observed
crop over the intersection of the two footprints: over the 40 regions
the intersection is $74$--$83\%$ of the crop ($81\%$ median), and the
comparison is made on the $73$--$81\%$ that survives a three-pixel
erosion of its boundary. Projecting a constant field returns exactly one
everywhere on this area, the check required before any region is
accepted. Everything here depends on how the decoded carrier is read,
and Section~\ref{sec:operator} shows that changing the read alone
removes two thirds of the floor's mean squared error, so the sweep was
scored twice, through the nearest-cell read used throughout the paper
and through the interpolating read of that section; the UTM side is
identical under both.

The first consequence is a floor, which belongs to the transfer-and-read
pair. The first block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports the RMSE of the
\emph{uncompressed} level-20 carrier, read on the native pixels of the
intersection with the nearest read, against the observation:
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median, about $10\%$ of the standard deviation of
the scene, rising to $7.9\times10^{-3}$ over urban scenes and falling to
$8.1\times10^{-4}$ over water, following the high-frequency content as a
transfer step should. No codec setting on the HEALPix side can go below it, and the read
alone accounts for an error of that size (Section~\ref{sec:operator}),
so the floor is an upper bound on what the representation costs.

The second consequence is a reversal, the most important caveat of this
paper. Under the common reference and the nearest read, native UTM is
ahead at \emph{every} evaluable target, matched-budget included: the
median RMSE ratios are $1.44$, $3.86$ and $32.2$ at the $2\%$, $5\%$ and
$10\%$ budgets, over the 37, 36 and 34 regions that qualify
(Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$ throughout), against $0.83$, $1.33$ and $4.01$
under the within-representation measure. The one clear HEALPix advantage of Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative}
reverses under the change of reference for an arithmetic reason: at a
$2\%$ budget the compression error and the transfer error are of the
same order, so a measure that charges the second to HEALPix moves the
balance by more than the codec does. The interpolating read changes the size of this reversal, and at
one target its sign. The ratio at the $2\%$ budget falls from $1.44$ to
$1.12$ over all 40 regions, still in favour of native UTM (Holm-adjusted
$p<10^{-4}$), and the $5\%$ and $10\%$ ratios fall from $3.86$ and $32.2$
to $2.50$ and $19.0$. At the one RMSE target this read makes reportable,
$10^{-2}$, HEALPix is the smaller representation under the illustrative
overhead: a median ratio of $0.93$ over the 32 qualifying regions
(Holm-adjusted $p=1\times10^{-3}$), with an overhead-free ratio of
$1.20$, a critical overhead below the illustrative $1.30$. That median coincides by chance with the overhead-free
within-representation value of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}; the two come
from different sweeps, references and overheads. The
within-representation ratio at this target ($0.72$) is never recovered,
and the three budgets stay with native UTM; of the gap between the two
references at the $2\%$ budget ($1.44$ against $0.83$ under the nearest
read, $1.12$ against $0.83$ under the interpolating one), about half was
the reader.

Two results survive the switch of reference. JPEG~2000 leads by a wide
margin under both references and both reads, while JPEG~XL and SZ3
exchange second place (under the nearest read JPEG~XL takes more outright
wins, $49$ against $33$, and SZ3 the better mean rank, $2.54$ against
$3.01$; under the interpolating read $48$ against $44$ and $2.54$ against
$2.96$). The latitude effect keeps its direction, the equatorial belt
staying more favourable to HEALPix than the caps at the tightest budget
($1.63$ against $2.06$ under the nearest read, $1.31$ against $1.60$ under
the interpolating one), and the reader decides its significance: under
the nearest read the contrast fails the Holm correction of its family
($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.18$); under the interpolating read it passes at all
three budgets ($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.003$ at the tightest, $0.003$ and
$0.002$ at the other two) and fails at the one reportable RMSE target,
as expected if the jitter of the nearest read, largest where cells are
most anisotropic, was masking it.

Both measures are therefore reported and neither is elected. If the
HEALPix cube is itself the product, the case a DGGS archive is built for,
the within-representation measure is the operative one and the
regime-dependent verdict of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main} stands. If the
native product remains the ground truth and the HEALPix copy is a derived
convenience, the transfer error belongs in the accounting, and on that
basis the native grid is more efficient at every storage budget and at
every target, the single exception being the loosest RMSE target read
through the interpolating operator. What the two measures jointly
exclude is the unconditional claim that a HEALPix carrier is cheaper.
Two further questions about this reference are answered in the
appendices. Writing the compression error as a fraction
$\kappa$ of the transfer error (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}), the
crossings of Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} occur at $\kappa=0.13$ to
$0.50$ against the latent scene in all four classes and inflate the total
error of the product by a measured $2.5$ to $16.0\%$; the saving in bytes
is substantial in three of them, $12$ to $30\%$, and about $2\%$ over
water. Against the observation the same crossings cost $37$ to $115\%$. The two errors do not add in quadrature
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term}): the cross term is negative against the
observation, where quadrature overstates the total by up to $24$ points,
and positive against the latent scene, where it understates it by up to
$6.6$ points, with JPEG~2000 carrying the whole effect and ZFP orthogonal
within $|\rho|\le0.03$.
