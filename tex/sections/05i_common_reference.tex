\subsection{The Verdict Depends on Which Reference Is Accepted}
\label{sec:common_reference}

Every ratio above compares each grid with its own raw representation,
which charges the \term{carrier} nothing for the \term{transfer}. This section scores
both grids against the same Sentinel-2 observation. The decoded HEALPix
field is read on the native UTM pixels and compared with the observed
crop over the intersection of the two footprints. Over the 40 regions
the intersection is $74$--$83\%$ of the crop ($81\%$ median), and the
comparison is made on the $73$--$81\%$ that survives a three-pixel
erosion of its boundary. Projecting a constant field returns exactly one
everywhere on this area, the check required before any region is
accepted. Everything here depends on how the decoded \term{carrier} is read.
Section~\ref{sec:operator} shows that changing the \term{read} alone removes
two thirds of the floor's mean squared error, so the sweep was scored
twice: through the nearest-cell read used throughout the paper, and
through the interpolating \term{read} of that section. The UTM side is
identical under both.

The first consequence is a floor, which belongs to the transfer-and-read
pair. The first block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports the RMSE of the
\emph{uncompressed} level-20 \term{carrier} against the observation, read on
the native pixels of the intersection with the nearest \term{read}. It is
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median, about $10\%$ of the standard deviation of
the scene. It rises to $7.9\times10^{-3}$ over urban scenes and falls to
$8.1\times10^{-4}$ over water, following the high-frequency content as a
\term{transfer} step should. No codec setting on the HEALPix side can go below
it. The \term{read} alone accounts for an error of that size
(Section~\ref{sec:operator}), so the floor is an upper bound on what
the representation costs.

Under the nearest \term{read} four of the five \term{RMSE targets} lie beneath this
floor, and no \term{RMSE target} qualifies on the $75\%$ of regions required by
Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}. $10^{-2}$ reaches $70\%$;
$3\times10^{-3}$, $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$ reach $30\%$, $15\%$ and
$7\%$; $10^{-4}$ is reachable nowhere. Under the interpolating read the
floor halves, to $1.9\times10^{-3}$, and $10^{-2}$ becomes reportable
($80\%$). $3\times10^{-3}$ and $10^{-3}$ rise to $60\%$ and $25\%$ and
stay withheld, since the regions that would remain are the smoothest.

The second consequence is a reversal, the most important caveat of this
paper. Under the \term{common reference} and the nearest \term{read}, native UTM is
ahead at \emph{every} evaluable \term{target}, \term{matched-budget} included. The
median RMSE ratios are $1.44$, $3.86$ and $32.2$ at the $2\%$, $5\%$ and
$10\%$ budgets, over the 37, 36 and 34 regions that qualify
(Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$ throughout), against $0.83$, $1.33$ and $4.01$
under the within-representation measure. The one clear HEALPix
advantage of Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} reverses under the change
of \term{reference} for an arithmetic reason. At a $2\%$ budget the compression
error and the \term{transfer} error are of the same order, so a measure that
charges the second to HEALPix moves the balance by more than the codec
does. The interpolating \term{read} changes the size of this reversal, and at
one \term{target} its sign. The ratio at the $2\%$ budget falls from $1.44$ to
$1.12$ over all 40 regions, still in favour of native UTM (Holm-adjusted
$p<10^{-4}$). The $5\%$ and $10\%$ ratios fall from $3.86$ and $32.2$
to $2.50$ and $19.0$. At the one \term{RMSE target} this \term{read} makes reportable,
$10^{-2}$, HEALPix is the smaller representation under the illustrative
\term{overhead}: a median ratio of $0.93$ over the 32 qualifying regions
(Holm-adjusted $p=1\times10^{-3}$), with an overhead-free ratio of
$1.20$, a \term{critical overhead} below the illustrative $1.30$. That median
coincides by chance with the overhead-free within-representation value
of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}; the two come from different sweeps,
\term{references} and \term{overheads}. The within-representation ratio at this
\term{target} ($0.72$) is never recovered, and the three budgets stay with
native UTM. Of the gap between the two \term{references} at the $2\%$ budget
($1.44$ against $0.83$ under the nearest \term{read}, $1.12$ against $0.83$
under the interpolating one), about half was the reader.

Two results survive the switch of \term{reference}. JPEG~2000 leads by a wide
margin under both \term{references} and both \term{reads}, while JPEG~XL and SZ3
exchange second place. Under the nearest \term{read} JPEG~XL takes more
outright wins, $49$ against $33$, and SZ3 the better mean rank, $2.54$
against $3.01$; under the interpolating read the figures are $48$
against $44$ and $2.54$ against $2.96$. The latitude effect keeps its
direction: the equatorial belt stays more favourable to HEALPix than the
caps at the tightest budget ($1.63$ against $2.06$ under the nearest
read, $1.31$ against $1.60$ under the interpolating one). The reader
decides its significance. Under the nearest read the contrast fails the
Holm correction of its family ($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.18$). Under the
interpolating read it passes at all three budgets
($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.003$ at the tightest, $0.003$ and $0.002$ at the
other two) and fails at the one reportable \term{RMSE target}. This is what
one expects if the jitter of the nearest \term{read}, largest where cells are
most anisotropic, was masking it.

Both measures are therefore reported and neither is elected. If the
HEALPix cube is itself the product, the case a DGGS archive is built for,
the within-representation measure is the operative one and the
regime-dependent verdict of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main} stands. If the
native Sentinel-2 observation remains the \term{reference} product and the
HEALPix copy is a derived
convenience, the \term{transfer} error belongs in the accounting. On that basis
the native grid is more efficient at all three reportable \term{matched-storage}
budgets. Among the \term{RMSE targets}, only $10^{-2}$ becomes reportable under
the interpolating \term{read}, and there the \term{carrier} is smaller under the
illustrative \term{overhead}. What the two measures jointly
exclude is the unconditional claim that a HEALPix \term{carrier} is cheaper.
This \term{reference} applies to the Clough--Tocher \term{carrier} only. That \term{transfer}
keeps the observation's own resolution, so the observation is the field
it aims at. The PSF-aware \term{carrier} of Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}
aims at the latent field before the instrument blur. Scored against the
observation, it would be charged for the small-scale content it was
built to restore, and the comparison would rank the two \term{transfers} on a
field only one of them \term{targets}. Such a comparison needs a synthetic
scene whose latent field is known, and it is left to future work
(Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}).

Two further questions about this \term{reference} are answered in the
appendices. The compression error can be written as a fraction $\kappa$
of the \term{transfer} error (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). On that scale
the crossings of Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} occur at
$\kappa=0.13$ to $0.50$ against the latent scene, in all four classes,
and inflate the product's total error by a measured $2.5$ to $16.0\%$.
The saving in bytes is $12$ to $30\%$ in three classes and about $2\%$
over water. Against the observation the same crossings cost $37$ to
$115\%$. The two errors do not add in quadrature
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term}). The cross term is negative against the
observation, where quadrature overstates the total by up to $24$ points,
and positive against the latent scene, where it understates it by up to
$6.6$ points. JPEG~2000 carries the whole effect, and ZFP is orthogonal
within $|\rho|\le0.03$.
