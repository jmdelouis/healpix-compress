\subsection{The Verdict Depends on Which Reference Is Accepted}
\label{sec:common_reference}

Every ratio reported above compares each grid with its own raw
representation: the HEALPix side with its resampled carrier tile, the native
side with the raw UTM crop. This is the natural measure for an archive whose
reference product \emph{is} the HEALPix cube, but it charges the carrier
nothing for the transfer that placed the data on it. This section reports
the complementary measure, in which both grids are scored against the same
Sentinel-2 observation. Everything in it depends on how the decoded carrier
is read on the native pixels, and Section~\ref{sec:operator} has shown that
changing the read alone removes two thirds of the floor's mean squared
error. The sweep was therefore
scored twice, through the nearest-cell read used throughout the paper and
through the interpolating read of Table~\ref{tab:reader_floor}. The UTM side
is identical under both, since it depends only on the intersection mask.

The decoded HEALPix field is read on the native UTM pixels and compared
with the observed crop over the intersection of the two footprints. This
restriction is necessary: the carrier tile is a square rotated with respect
to UTM, the equal-area crop is axis-aligned, and although the two enclose
the same area they do not cover the same ground. Over the 40 regions the
intersection is $74$--$83\%$ of the crop ($81\%$ median), and the comparison
is made on the $73$--$81\%$ that survives a three-pixel erosion of its
boundary. Projecting a constant field returns exactly one everywhere on this
area, which is the check required before any region is accepted.

The first consequence is a floor, attributable to the transfer-and-read pair
rather than to the grid alone. Table~\ref{tab:representation_floor} reports
the RMSE of the \emph{uncompressed} carrier against the observation:
$4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median under the nearest read, about $10\%$ of the
standard deviation of the scene, rising to $7.9\times10^{-3}$ over urban
scenes and falling to $8.1\times10^{-4}$ over water, i.e.\ following the
high-frequency content as a transfer step should. No codec setting can fall
below this floor, and under the nearest read four of the five RMSE targets
lie beneath it. The consequence is censoring: no RMSE target qualifies on
the $75\%$ of regions required by the criterion of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}. The $10^{-2}$ target reaches $70\%$;
$3\times10^{-3}$, $10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$ reach $30\%$, $15\%$ and
$7\%$; $10^{-4}$ is reachable nowhere. Under the interpolating read the
floor halves, to $1.9\times10^{-3}$, and $10^{-2}$ becomes reportable
($80\%$), while $3\times10^{-3}$ and $10^{-3}$ rise to $60\%$ and $25\%$
and remain withheld. The regions that would remain at the withheld targets are the
smoothest ones, so that reporting them would describe a selected subsample.

The second consequence is a reversal, which is the most important caveat of
this paper. Under the common reference and the nearest read, native UTM is
ahead at \emph{every} evaluable target, matched-budget included: the median
RMSE ratios are $1.44$, $3.86$ and $32.2$ at the $2\%$, $5\%$ and $10\%$
budgets, over the 37, 36 and 34 regions that qualify at each
(Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$ throughout), against $0.83$, $1.33$ and $4.01$
under the within-representation measure. The one clear HEALPix advantage of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} does not survive the change of reference,
for an arithmetic reason: at a $2\%$ budget the compression error and the
transfer error are of the same order, so that a measure charging the second
to HEALPix moves the balance by more than the codec does.

The interpolating read changes the magnitude of this reversal, and at one
target its sign (Table~\ref{tab:common_two_readers}). The ratio at the
$2\%$ budget falls from $1.44$ to $1.12$ over all 40 regions, still in
favour of native UTM (Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$), and the $5\%$ and $10\%$
ratios fall from $3.86$ and $32.2$ to $2.50$ and $19.0$. At the one RMSE
target that this read makes reportable, $10^{-2}$, HEALPix is the smaller
representation under the illustrative overhead: a median ratio of $0.93$
over the 32 qualifying regions (Holm-adjusted $p=1\times10^{-3}$), with an
overhead-free ratio of $1.20$, a critical overhead below the illustrative
$1.30$ rather than above it. The within-representation ratio at this target
($0.72$) is not recovered, and the three storage budgets stay with native
UTM, so the change of reference still costs HEALPix the advantage it holds
at generous budgets; what the two reads bracket is how much of that cost
belongs to the reading operator. Of the gap between the two references at
the $2\%$ budget, $1.44$ against $0.83$ under the nearest read and $1.12$
against $0.83$ under the interpolating one, about half was the reader.

Two results are unchanged by the switch of reference. JPEG~2000 leads by a
wide margin under both references and both reads; JPEG~XL and SZ3 exchange
second place without either approaching it (under the nearest read JPEG~XL
takes more outright wins, $49$ against $33$, and SZ3 the better mean rank,
$2.54$ against $3.01$; under the interpolating read the figures are $48$
against $44$ and $2.54$ against $2.96$), so that the leading codec is a
property of the data and not of the measure. The latitude effect keeps its
direction: the equatorial belt remains more favourable to HEALPix than the
caps at the tightest budget, with median ratios of $1.63$ against $2.06$
under the nearest read and $1.31$ against $1.60$ under the interpolating
one. Here the reader determines the significance. Under the nearest read the
contrast does not pass the Holm correction of its family
($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.18$); under the interpolating read it does at all
three storage budgets ($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.003$ at the tightest, $0.003$
and $0.002$ at the other two), though not at the one reportable RMSE
target, as expected if the jitter of the nearest read,
largest where the cells are most anisotropic, was masking it.

Both measures are therefore reported, and neither is elected. If the HEALPix
cube is itself the product, which is the case a DGGS archive is built for,
the within-representation measure is the operative one and the
regime-dependent verdict of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main} stands. If the native
product remains the ground truth and the HEALPix copy is a derived
convenience, the transfer error belongs in the accounting, and on that basis
the native grid is more efficient at every storage budget and under the
nearest read at every target, the single exception being the loosest RMSE
target read through the interpolating operator. What the
two measures jointly exclude is the unconditional claim that a HEALPix
carrier is cheaper.

Two further questions concerning this reference are answered in the
appendices and summarised here because Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}
relies on them. Writing the compression error as a fraction $\kappa$ of the
transfer error (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}), the crossings of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} occur at $\kappa=0.13$ to $0.50$ against
the latent scene and inflate the total error of the product by a measured
$2.5$ to $16.0\%$ for savings of $12$ to $30\%$ in three of the four scene
classes, whereas against the observation the same crossings cost $37$ to
$115\%$. The two errors do not add in quadrature
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term}): the sign of the cross term depends on the
reference, negative against the observation, where quadrature overstates
the total by up to $24$ points, and positive against the latent scene,
where it understates the total by up to $6.6$ points, with JPEG~2000
carrying the whole effect and ZFP orthogonal within $|\rho|\le0.03$.
