\subsection{The Two Level-19 Carriers Cost the Same}
\label{sec:level19_direct}

A carrier built by Clough--Tocher at level 19 holds the interpolant
evaluated at the level-19 cell centres; the aggregate holds the mean of
the interpolant at the four level-20 centres inside each cell. The two
coincide only for a function that is affine over a cell, so the gap
between them is the curvature of the interpolant at the \SI{12.4}{m}
scale. Both objects were therefore built. The direct carrier uses the
same native sub-window each stored tile was transferred from, the same
parent, the same Clough--Tocher operator and the same checks; only the
output level differs. The two are read identically, at the nearest
containing cell of their own level, against the same observation over
the intersection mask of Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}. Rebuilding
from the window makes this the one measurement in the paper that depends
on the window still holding the product its tile was built from;
Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry} tests that for all 40 regions and
finds 39 sound, so the comparison runs on 39. The last block of
Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports both carriers; its last column counts the
regions on which the direct carrier has the lower floor.

The two carriers are different arrays: they differ by $6.0\%$ of a scene's
standard deviation in RMS, $0.27$ times the level-19 floor itself, so the
curvature term is real and of the predicted order. Their floors against
the observation are nevertheless the same: $8.72\times10^{-3}$ for the
direct carrier against $8.69\times10^{-3}$ for the aggregate, a median
ratio of $1.002$, within $5\%$ on 38 of the 39 regions, with the direct
carrier lower on 15 of them and a widest departure of $1.051$. The
difference between the two carriers moves cell values without moving the
error against the scene; we have not measured an inner product between
that difference and the observation, so we do not call it orthogonal.
Against the level-20 floor the two coarser carriers cost $2.25$ and
$2.24$, so the conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:level_choice} holds for
either reading of ``the level-19 carrier'', and the figure quoted there is
the aggregate's.

The excluded region is Lake Geneva. Rebuilt at level 19 its carrier
misses the aggregate by twice the scene's standard deviation, which looks
like a failure of the transfer at the coarser level. The cause lies
upstream: asked for level 20, where the rebuild must return the stored
tile, the same operator on the same samples misses it by the same amount,
an RMS of $1.97$ standard deviations at a correlation of $0.585$. The
window and the tile are related by a gain and an offset of their own, a
radiometric difference between two products
(Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry}). Kept, the region would leave the
median ratio at $1.002$; it is excluded because its input is no longer
the input the comparison assumes. One safeguard follows for anyone
rebuilding a carrier at a new level: a geometric check that the window
still covers the tile does not catch a window that has been re-acquired
(it passes on all 40 regions, this one included), while a transfer asked
for the level its tile is already stored at must return that tile.
