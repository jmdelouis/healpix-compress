\subsection{The Two Level-19 Carriers Cost the Same}
\label{sec:level19_direct}

A carrier built by Clough--Tocher at level 19 holds the interpolant
evaluated at the level-19 cell centres; the aggregate holds the mean of
the interpolant at the four level-20 centres inside each cell. The two
coincide only for a function that is affine over a cell, so the gap
between them is the curvature of the interpolant at the \SI{12.4}{m}
scale, which need not be negligible. Both objects were therefore built.
The direct carrier uses the same native sub-window each stored tile was
transferred from, the same parent, the same Clough--Tocher operator and
the same constant-field check and missing-cell completion; only the output
level differs. The two are read identically, at the nearest containing
cell of their own level, against the same observation over the same
intersection mask. Rebuilding from the window makes this the one
measurement in the paper that depends on the window still holding the
product its tile was built from; Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry}
tests that for all 40 regions and finds 39 sound, so the comparison runs
on 39.

The last block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} sets the two level-19 carriers
against the same observation as the level-20 tile, on the intersection
mask of Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}.
\emph{Direct} evaluates the Clough--Tocher interpolant of the native
samples at the level-19 cell centres; \emph{aggregate} averages the four
level-20 children, the object Section~\ref{sec:level_choice} measures. The
last column counts the regions on which that carrier has the lower floor.


The two carriers are different arrays: they differ by $6.0\%$ of a scene's
standard deviation in RMS, $0.27$ times the level-19 floor itself, so the
curvature term is real and of the predicted order. Their floors against
the observation are nevertheless the same: $8.72\times10^{-3}$ for the
direct carrier against $8.69\times10^{-3}$ for the aggregate, a median
ratio of $1.002$, within $5\%$ on 38 of the 39 regions, with the direct
carrier lower on 15 of them and a widest departure of $1.051$. The
difference between the two carriers moves cell values without moving the
error against the scene; we have not measured an inner product between
that difference and the observation, so we do not call it orthogonal.
Against the level-20 floor the two coarser carriers cost $2.25$ and
$2.24$, so the conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:level_choice} holds for
either reading of ``the level-19 carrier'', and the figure quoted there is
the aggregate's.

The excluded region is Lake Geneva. Rebuilt at level 19 its carrier
misses the aggregate by twice the scene's standard deviation, which looks
like a failure of the transfer at the coarser level. It is not: asked for
level 20, where the rebuild must return the stored tile, the same operator
on the same samples misses it by the same amount, an RMS of $1.97$
standard deviations at a correlation of $0.585$.
Level 19 inherits the discrepancy: the window and the tile are related
by a gain and an offset of their own, a radiometric difference between two
products, as Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry} establishes for all 40
regions. Kept, the region would move the median ratio from $1.002$ to
$1.002$; it is excluded because its input is no longer the input the
comparison assumes. Two safeguards follow for anyone rebuilding a
carrier at a new level. A tile and the window it came from are separate
files that can drift apart, and a geometric check that the window still
covers the tile does not catch a window that has been re-acquired (the
check passes on all 40 regions, this one included). And the cheap control
is the decisive one: a transfer asked for the level its tile is already
stored at must return that tile.
