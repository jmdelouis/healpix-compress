\subsection{The Two Level-19 Carriers Cost the Same}
\label{sec:level19_direct}

The objection to the previous subsection is concrete and was worth
answering rather than conceding. A carrier built by Clough--Tocher at level
19 holds the interpolant evaluated at the level-19 cell centres; the
aggregate holds the mean of the interpolant at the four level-20 centres
inside each of those cells. The two coincide only for a function that is
affine over a cell, so the gap between them is the curvature of the
interpolant at the \SI{12.4}{m} scale, which is not obviously negligible
compared with the quantity the level choice turns on.

Both objects were therefore built. The direct carrier uses the same native
sub-window each stored tile was transferred from, the same parent, the same
Clough--Tocher operator and the same constant-field check and missing-cell
completion; only the output level differs. The two are then read
identically, at the nearest containing cell of their own level, against the
same observation over the same intersection mask, so that nothing but the
cell values can separate them.

Rebuilding from the window makes this the one measurement in the paper that
depends on the window still holding the product its tile was built from.
Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry} tests that for all 40 regions and finds
39 of them sound and one not, so the comparison runs on 39.

Table~\ref{tab:level19_direct} sets the three carriers against the same
Sentinel-2 observation, on the intersection mask of
Section~\ref{sec:common_reference} and each read at the nearest containing
cell of its own level. \emph{Direct} evaluates the Clough--Tocher
interpolant of the native samples at the level-19 cell centres;
\emph{aggregate} averages the four level-20 children, which is the exact
cell-average of the stored carrier and the object
Section~\ref{sec:level_choice} measures. The last column counts the regions
on which that carrier has the lower floor.

The two carriers are genuinely different arrays. They differ by $6.0\%$ of
a scene's standard deviation in RMS, which is $0.27$ times the level-19
floor itself, so the curvature term the objection identifies is real and of
the order it predicted. Their floors against the observation are
nevertheless the same: $8.72\times10^{-3}$ for the direct carrier against
$8.69\times10^{-3}$ for the aggregate, a median ratio of $1.002$, within
$5\%$ on 38 of the 39 regions, and the direct carrier has the lower floor
on 15 of them. The widest departure anywhere is $1.051$. The difference
between the two carriers therefore has no material effect on the floor: it
moves cell values without moving the error against the scene. We have not
measured an inner product between that difference and the observation, so we
do not call it orthogonal. Against the level-20 floor the two coarser carriers cost
$2.25$ and $2.24$, so the conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:level_choice}
holds for either reading of ``the level-19 carrier'', and the figure quoted
there is the aggregate's.

The excluded region is Lake Geneva, and what it turned out to be is worth
stating, because our first reading of it was wrong. Rebuilt at level 19 its
carrier missed the aggregate by twice the standard deviation of the scene,
which we took for a failure of the transfer at the coarser level. It is
not. Asked for level 20 --- the level the tile is stored at, where the
rebuild must return the stored tile ---
the same operator on the same samples misses it by the same amount, an RMS
of $1.97$ standard deviations at a correlation of $0.585$. Nothing at
level 19 produced the discrepancy; level 19 inherited it. The window and
the tile are related by a gain and an offset of their own, which is a
radiometric difference between two products and not a resampling error,
and the audit that establishes this for all 40 regions is
Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry}. Kept, the region would move the
median ratio from $1.002$ to $1.002$; it is excluded because its input is
not the input the comparison assumes, not because its result is
inconvenient.

Two things follow for anyone rebuilding a carrier at a new level. A tile and
the window it came from are separate files that can drift apart, and a
geometric check that the window still covers the tile does not catch a
window that has been re-acquired --- ours passed on all 40 regions,
including this one. And the cheap control is the one this section needed: a
transfer asked for the level its tile is already stored at must return that
tile, and if it does not, no number built on it means what it appears to
mean.
