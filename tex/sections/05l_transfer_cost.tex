\subsection{What the Transfer Costs to Store}
\label{sec:transfer_cost}
This section prices the \term{transfer} itself. The three \term{transfers} are not
alternatives for the same purpose. Clough--Tocher keeps the resolution
and the instrument response of the product, so its tile carries the
same information as the native raster and the comparison isolates what
the grid costs. The PSF-aware \term{transfer} is designed to restore
small-scale structure, so its tile carries content the native raster
does not, and its bytes pay for that content. The inverse-distance
average over the four nearest cells of Section~\ref{sec:resampler} (the
library's \texttt{linear} kernel) restores none and serves as the
control. The control is needed because a deconvolved tile costing more
than a Clough--Tocher tile would establish nothing on its own: every
property of a deconvolving \term{transfer} is confounded with its not being
Clough--Tocher. The same rate--distortion protocol is run on \term{carrier}
tiles rebuilt from the same native windows by the two further
\term{transfers}. The PSF tiles are built at the damping at which the
reconstruction error was measured ($\lambda=10^{-3}$) and at the nominal
\SI{12.5}{m} response width. Coverage is 40 regions for the PSF \term{transfer}
and 39 for the inverse-distance average.

Table~\ref{tab:transfer} uses short names: C--T for Clough--Tocher, PSF
for the PSF-aware \term{transfer}, IDW for the inverse-distance average. Every
ratio at a given \term{target} is a paired median over the same regions. A
region is admitted under the rule of Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}
applied to all four sweeps: the codec the oracle selects must bracket
the \term{target} on the C--T, PSF and IDW \term{carriers} and on the \term{native crop}. The
extra sweeps remove one region at the tight \term{targets} and two at the
loosest, where $n$ falls to 30. In block (a), the three columns under
$\gamma=1$ give each \term{carrier} against the equal-area \term{native crop} with no
overhead, composed per region, so a value below one favours HEALPix. The
three columns under ``relative to C--T'' give the compressed size
relative to the Clough--Tocher tile of the same region at the same
absolute RMSE; the last of them sets the deconvolving \term{transfer} against
the non-deconvolving one. Under the illustrative overhead every value of
the first three columns divides by $1.3$.

The control is informative. At the same coding RMSE, each measured
against that \term{transfer}'s own uncompressed \term{carrier}, the deconvolved tile
costs $1.26$ to $2.45$ times the Clough--Tocher tile of the same region,
increasing with the \term{target}. The inverse-distance average costs
$1.20$ to $2.15$, very nearly the same. Relative to each other the two exceed
parity by only $1.03$ to $1.14$, separable at the three tightest \term{targets}
and not at the two loosest. This PSF/IDW differential of $3$--$14\%$
suggests that deconvolution alone is unlikely to explain the roughly
two-fold surcharge, since the \term{transfer} that restores no detail pays
nearly all of it too. The two \term{transfers} are not matched in smoothness or
interpolation class, so this experiment does not isolate or bound the
contribution of deconvolution: the two chains differ in kernel,
smoothness, frequency response and behaviour under noise. Isolating it
would need a non-deconvolving \term{transfer} of the same smoothness class,
which we do not have.

Block (b) of Table~\ref{tab:transfer} separates content from
codability. Its second column is each raster's own lossless size. The
other columns give the compressed size at each \term{target} as a fraction of
that lossless size, so the entropy difference between rasters divides
out. The lossless sizes are within $9\%$ of each other: \SI{1.31}{MB}, \SI{1.37}{MB} and \SI{1.43}{MB} for Clough--Tocher, PSF and
inverse-distance. The rate--distortion curves have very different
slopes. At the $10^{-2}$ \term{target} the Clough--Tocher tile compresses to
$1.1\%$ of its own lossless size, the deconvolved tile to $2.6\%$, the
inverse-distance tile to $3.3\%$ and the native raster to $3.8\%$. Scene
complexity does not explain the surcharge. Correlating each region's
lossless size on the Clough--Tocher raster with the surcharge it pays
(the median over the \term{targets} both sweeps bracket) gives a Spearman
$\rho$ of $-0.12$ for the PSF \term{transfer} ($p=0.456$, $n=40$) and $-0.08$
for the inverse-distance average ($p=0.615$, $n=39$). This tests whether
more complex scenes pay more; it does not test whether the content the
other \term{transfers} add is what costs the bytes. The spectral observations
are consistent with energy compaction playing an important role, and
the mechanism is not isolated. The deconvolution puts power back into
the small scales directly, $2.80$ times the observation's in the top
octave against $0.89$ for Clough--Tocher, and a coder asked for a given
RMSE must encode it. The inverse-distance average adds no such power
($0.92$) and still pays. What is established is that its output lies
twice as far from an analytic truth as that of Clough--Tocher, and that
content which is neither the scene nor suppressed must be coded as
content.

The consequence for the headline result is one of the largest
sensitivities measured in this study. Under Clough--Tocher the HEALPix
\term{carrier} is the cheaper representation at the two loosest \term{targets}
($0.94$ and $0.72$ against the native product under the illustrative
overhead).
Under either other \term{transfer} it is cheaper at none: $2.00$ and $1.78$ for
the PSF \term{transfer}, $1.88$ and $1.57$ for the inverse-distance average. A
factor of two in the storage cost of the \term{transfer} exceeds the whole
margin, so the regime-dependent verdict of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main} is
a statement about a \term{carrier} filled by this \term{transfer}. In final sizes with
no overhead (Tables~\ref{tab:synthesis} and~\ref{tab:transfer}), the
Clough--Tocher tile compresses to between $2.07$ and $0.93$ times the
\term{native square} as the tolerated error grows. The PSF-aware tile
compresses to between $2.62$ and $2.32$ times, and the inverse-distance
tile to between $2.49$ and $2.05$ times. The last two barely move with the tolerated
error. Their content resists lossy coding, while the content of the
Clough--Tocher tile is mostly the smooth redundancy a codec is built to
remove.

Block (c) of Table~\ref{tab:transfer} rebuilds the \term{carrier} with five
kernels from the same native window (bicubic is a radial
nearest-neighbour adaptation of Keys' cubic convolution kernel) and reads
each with the same operator, so any difference
between rows is the \term{transfer} alone. \emph{Transfer} is the error against
the analytic field, as a percentage of that field's standard deviation;
it is the only measure in the table that gives a \term{transfer} no credit for
copying its input samples. \emph{Over} counts the cells a \term{transfer}
places outside the range of the data it was given, how a diverging
interpolant shows itself to a check that a constant field passes.
\emph{Floor} is the error of the uncompressed tile read back on the
native pixels, as a fraction of the scene's standard deviation, and
\emph{rel.} its per-region ratio to Clough--Tocher; both reward a
\term{transfer} for staying close to the identity on the native lattice.
\emph{log-PSD} is the RMS distance in $\log_{10}$ between the tile's
radial power spectrum and the observation's up to the native Nyquist
frequency, and \emph{top oct.} the ratio of their power in the highest
octave. The last three columns are compressed-\term{size ratios} to
Clough--Tocher at three \term{targets}.

Equal bytes are not equal value. Among the \term{transfers} that aim to
reproduce the analytic field, Clough--Tocher reaches $7.97\%$ of its
standard deviation, against $18.30\%$ for nearest and $16.20\%$ for the
inverse-distance average, on the 40 regions (39 for the inverse-distance
average and for bicubic, which do not cover Lake Taupo). The field carries the spectrum and response
fitted to the imagery, and the Clough--Tocher figure is consistent with
the $3.9\%$ to $10.3\%$ by class that the noiseless control of
Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget} finds on the same construction. The bicubic row is kept for completeness and
is not a valid measurement of bicubic interpolation. A diagnostic on four
regions, one per class, shows that the radial variant used here fails to
reproduce a constant field on about $1.6\%$ of the cells. On those cells
it returns values scaled by up to three orders of magnitude, and they
carry almost all of its \term{transfer} error. A separable bicubic
interpolation of the same field on the source raster gives $5.8\%$ of
the standard deviation with no such tail. The defect belongs to that
operator, not to the
kernel, and the row is marked rather than removed so that the omission
is explicit. The $24.46\%$ of the PSF \term{transfer} is no
ranking entry, since its estimand is the latent field and the analytic
field here is the observation-resolution one. The extra bytes of the
inverse-distance average buy \term{transfer} error, twofold.

Nothing in this section measures the quality of the PSF-aware
\term{transfer}. Its purpose is to estimate a field the observation does
not contain, and a comparison at the observation's resolution can only
charge it for that content, never credit it. What this section
establishes is the storage price of that content. The extra bytes of the
deconvolved \term{carrier} are of a different kind by design, and what they
buy is established by the companion study under its own conditions. On a known pre-degradation texture the matched
reconstruction recovers $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$ of the imposed blur error.
On real reduced-resolution Sentinel-2 imagery it has the smallest RMSE
in all 40 regions, improving on the best of nearest, bilinear, bicubic
and Richardson--Lucy by $11.0\%$ to $17.9\%$, with the restored power at
low and intermediate frequencies~\cite{delouis2026resample}. Our own
synthetic harness reproduces that recovery at the nominal response width
without noise, and loses most of it at the noise level measured on the
imagery (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). Whether the particular tiles
whose bytes are counted here realise it is not established. With this
reservation, the two-fold surcharge has two meanings. On the deconvolved \term{carrier} it
is the price of small-scale content placed there by a validated method.
On the inverse-distance \term{carrier} it is the price of content twice as
far from the truth as the \term{transfer} it replaced. Among the
\term{transfers} that reproduce the product, Clough--Tocher is both the most
accurate and the most compressible. The observations are consistent
with the two sharing a cause. A \term{transfer} that represents a band-limited
field faithfully does not invent fine-scale content, and content it
does not invent is content the codec need not pay for. This is a
statement about reproducing the product, not a ranking of the PSF-aware
\term{transfer}, whose purpose is to go beyond it. A level-20 \term{carrier} becomes
competitive at aggressive compression when it is filled by the
Clough--Tocher \term{transfer}, of the three tested. The other two cost about a
factor of two at the loosest \term{targets} and remove the advantage, a cost
set by how compactly the \term{transfer}'s output can be coded more than by how
much detail it restores. Whether the result extends to other \term{transfers}
of comparable smoothness remains untested.
