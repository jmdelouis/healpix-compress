\subsection{What the Transfer Costs to Store}
\label{sec:transfer_cost}
This section prices the transfer itself. The same rate--distortion
protocol is run on carrier tiles rebuilt from the same native windows by
two further transfers: the PSF-aware one, which restores small-scale
structure by construction, and the inverse-distance average over the four
nearest cells that Section~\ref{sec:resampler} abandoned, which restores
none and serves as the control. The library calls the latter
\texttt{linear}; it is a Shepard-type weighted average whose kernel width
defaults to the \emph{output} cell scale, and Table~\ref{tab:kernel_ablation}
finds it $9.5$ times less accurate than Clough--Tocher against an analytic
field while leaving no excess top-octave power. The control makes the
question falsifiable: a deconvolved tile costing more than a
Clough--Tocher tile would establish nothing on its own, since every
property of a deconvolving transfer is confounded with its not being
Clough--Tocher. The PSF tiles are built at the damping at which the
reconstruction error was measured ($\lambda=10^{-3}$) and at the nominal
\SI{12.5}{m} response width. Coverage is 40 regions for the PSF transfer
and 39 for the inverse-distance average.

Table~\ref{tab:transfer_cost} reports the three carriers under short
names: C--T for Clough--Tocher, PSF for the PSF-aware transfer, IDW for
the inverse-distance average. Every ratio at a given target is a paired
median over the regions bracketed on all three sweeps. The left block
gives the compressed size relative to the Clough--Tocher tile of the same
region at the same absolute RMSE, its last column setting the deconvolving
transfer against the non-deconvolving one; the right block gives each
product against the equal-area native crop at
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.3$, composed per region, so a value below one
favours HEALPix.

The control is informative. At the same coding RMSE, each measured against
that transfer's own uncompressed carrier, the deconvolved tile costs
$1.27$ to $2.41$ times the Clough--Tocher tile of the same region,
increasing with the target; the inverse-distance average costs $1.20$ to
$2.01$, very nearly the same. Relative to each other the two exceed parity
by only $1.04$ to $1.09$, separable at the three tightest targets and not
at the two loosest. Whatever the roughly two-fold cost at loose targets
represents, restoring spatial detail is only a small part of it, because
the transfer that restores none pays it as well. The ratio between the two
bounds the cost of deconvolution, since the two chains differ by more than
the presence of a deconvolution; read as a bound, it says this surcharge
is small next to the surcharge of leaving Clough--Tocher. Isolating it
would need a non-deconvolving transfer of the same smoothness class, which
we do not have.

Table~\ref{tab:compactability} separates content from codability. Its
second column is each raster's own lossless size; the other columns give
the compressed size at each target as a fraction of that lossless size,
so the entropy difference between rasters divides out and what remains is
how much of the content a lossy coder can discard.

What separates the transfers is how compactly each raster can be coded.
Their lossless sizes are within $9\%$ of each other (\SI{1.31}{MB},
\SI{1.36}{MB} and \SI{1.41}{MB} for Clough--Tocher, PSF and
inverse-distance), while their rate--distortion curves have very
different slopes: at the $10^{-2}$ target the Clough--Tocher tile
compresses to $0.8\%$ of its own lossless size, the deconvolved tile to
$1.8\%$, the inverse-distance tile to $2.0\%$ and the native raster to
$3.0\%$ of its own. Across regions, how much a raster carries does not
explain its surcharge: correlating each region's own lossless size on the
Clough--Tocher raster with the surcharge it pays (the median over the
targets both sweeps bracket of that region's entry in the left block of
Table~\ref{tab:transfer_cost}) gives a Spearman $\rho$ of $-0.38$ for the
PSF transfer ($p=0.017$, $n=40$) and $-0.27$ for the inverse-distance
average ($p=0.100$, $n=39$), so the regions whose rasters hold the most
information pay, if anything, the \emph{smallest} surcharge. An
added-content explanation predicts the opposite sign. This is evidence
against total information content as the mechanism, short of a
refutation, since one of the two correlations misses the conventional
threshold. The observations fit energy compaction as the dominant
mechanism, reached by different routes: the deconvolution puts power back
into the small scales directly, $2.80$ times the observation's in the top
octave against $0.89$ for Clough--Tocher, and a coder asked for a given
RMSE must encode it; the inverse-distance average adds no such power
($0.92$) and still pays. The cause has not been isolated at the subband
level. What is established is that its output lies ten times further from
an analytic truth than that of Clough--Tocher, and that content which is
neither the scene nor suppressed must be coded as content.

The consequence for the headline result is the largest single sensitivity
measured in this study. Under Clough--Tocher the HEALPix carrier is the
cheaper representation at the two loosest targets ($0.93$ and $0.73$
against the native product under the illustrative overhead). Under either
other transfer it is cheaper at none: $2.00$ and $1.72$ for the PSF
transfer, $1.90$ and $1.60$ for the inverse-distance average. A factor of
two in the storage cost of the transfer exceeds the whole margin, so the
regime-dependent verdict of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main} is a statement
about a carrier filled by this transfer. In final sizes with no overhead
(Table~\ref{tab:headline}), the Clough--Tocher tile compresses to between
$2.06$ and $0.93$ times the native square as the tolerated error grows,
the PSF-aware tile to between $2.62$ and $2.24$ times, and the
inverse-distance tile to between $2.49$ and $2.07$ times. The last two
barely move with the tolerated error: their content resists lossy coding,
while the content of the Clough--Tocher tile is mostly the smooth
redundancy a codec is built to remove.

Table~\ref{tab:kernel_ablation} rebuilds the carrier with five kernels
from the same native window and reads each with the same operator, so any
difference between rows is the transfer alone (\texttt{linear} is the
inverse-distance average and \texttt{bicubic} Keys' cubic convolution).
\emph{Transfer} is the error against the analytic field, as a percentage
of that field's standard deviation. \emph{Over} counts the cells a
transfer places outside the range of the data it was given, which is how
a diverging interpolant shows itself to a check that a constant field
passes. \emph{Floor} is the error of the uncompressed tile read back on
the native pixels, as a fraction of the scene's standard deviation, and
\emph{rel.} its per-region ratio to Clough--Tocher; both reward a transfer
for staying close to the identity on the native lattice and are reported
without ranking. \emph{log-PSD} is the RMS distance in $\log_{10}$ between
the tile's radial power spectrum and the observation's up to the native
Nyquist frequency, and \emph{top oct.} the ratio of their power in the
highest octave, above one when the transfer leaves more high-frequency
power than the observation carries. The last three columns are
compressed-size ratios to the same reference at three targets.

Equal bytes are not equal value. The accuracy column of
Table~\ref{tab:kernel_ablation} scores each transfer against an analytic
field known at every position, the only measure in that table that gives
a transfer no credit for copying its input samples. Among the transfers
that aim to reproduce that field, Clough--Tocher reaches $1.20\%$ of its
standard deviation, against $10.04\%$ for nearest and $11.48\%$ for the
inverse-distance average, on the 39 regions the window audit of
Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry} admits (38 for the inverse-distance
average and for bicubic). The bicubic row is there for completeness: the
signed kernel of Keys rings outside the local sample range by design. The
$8.15\%$ of the PSF transfer is no ranking entry, since its estimand is
the latent field and departing from the observed one is its purpose. The
extra bytes of the inverse-distance average buy transfer error, tenfold.

The extra bytes of the deconvolved carrier are of the opposite kind, and
the companion study establishes this. On a known pre-degradation texture
the matched reconstruction recovers $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$ of the imposed
blur error, and on real reduced-resolution Sentinel-2 imagery it has the
smallest RMSE in all 40 regions, improving on the best of nearest,
bilinear, bicubic and Richardson--Lucy by $11.0\%$ to $17.9\%$, with the
restored power at low and intermediate frequencies~\cite{delouis2026resample}.
This describes the method under the conditions of that study; whether the
particular tiles whose bytes are counted here realise it is not
established, and our own harness does not reproduce the magnitude of the
recovery (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). With this reservation, the
two-fold surcharge has two meanings: on the deconvolved carrier it is the
price of small-scale content placed there by a validated method; on the
inverse-distance carrier it is the price of content that the ablation
shows to lie ten times further from the truth than the transfer it
replaced. Clough--Tocher remains both the most accurate of the reproducing
transfers and the most compressible, and we argue the two share a cause:
a transfer that represents a band-limited field faithfully does not invent
fine-scale content, and content it does not invent is content the codec
need not pay for. A HEALPix level-20 carrier becomes competitive at
aggressive compression when it is filled by a transfer in the smoothness
class of Clough--Tocher; leaving that class costs about a factor of two at
the loosest targets and removes the advantage, and this cost is a property
of how compactly the transfer's output can be coded, more than of how much
detail it restores.
