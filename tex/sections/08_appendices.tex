\section{Supplementary Methodology}
\label{app:method}

\subsection{Two Literatures Behind the Expectation}
\label{app:literatures}
Approximation theory says the following. The $L_2$ error of
representing a continuous field by samples on a grid of step $T$ is
set, to leading order, by an error kernel whose decay follows the
representation's approximation order. Refining the step therefore buys
a predictable and bounded amount of
accuracy~\cite{blu1999fourier,blu1999approximation,unser2000sampling}.
On a field the instrument has already band-limited, the added samples
are almost determined by their neighbours. Coding theory adds the
second half. Wavelet coefficient magnitudes persist across scales, and
zerotree coders~\cite{shapiro1993ezw,said1996spiht}, hidden Markov
trees~\cite{crouse1998hmt} and parent-conditioned
coding~\cite{buccigrossi1999joint} turn that persistence into bits
saved. An added octave that carries almost no variance is the case
those models handle best, which is why the oversampling penalty is
expected to be much smaller than the factor of~\eqref{eq:alpha}. The
native product shows only part of that octave. A median of $0.21\%$ of
each scene's variance lies above half the native Nyquist
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), while the octave level~20 adds
over level~19 lies mostly above the native Nyquist. On the \term{carrier} that
octave holds $3.07\%$ of the tile's variance
(Section~\ref{sec:level_choice}), two orders of magnitude more than a
spectral extrapolation of the $0.21\%$ suggests.

\subsection{A Direct Test of a Quadtree-Native Coder}
\label{app:quadtree}
A pyramid was built on the quadtree itself and coded with a
parent-conditioned context model in the spirit
of~\cite{buccigrossi1999joint}. Its ideal-entropy rate is $1.08\times$
the bytes the best generic codec spends at equal distortion on the same
40 tiles with the exact quadtree Haar, and $0.93\times$ with a
quadtree-native CDF~5/3 lifting.
Both are bounds that charge nothing for coding, and both lie within what
an implementation loses to its own bound. The link between scales is real
(conditioning on the parent saves $5.9\%$ of an order-0 rate, twice the
$3.0\%$ an intra-band neighbour saves) and small, and the generic codecs
already run a better wavelet on this very quadtree.

\subsection{What the Container Costs}
\label{app:container}
The coverage and datacube model behind a chunked archive sets what a
container must hold beyond the samples~\cite{baumann2025datacube}. One
level-20 chunk in a Zarr~v3 store~\cite{moore2023zarr} costs
\SI{737}{\byte} of group and array metadata, $0.018\%$ of the raw tile;
the cell identifiers of a contiguous NESTED block are implicit. A
single-tile deflate Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF~\cite{ogc_cog2023} of the
\term{equal-area crop} costs \SI{8461}{\byte} of header, tag directory and
near-empty payload, $0.52\%$ of its raw size. Container format therefore
accounts for at most half a percent on either side; a major remaining
contribution to a layout overhead is granule geometry, whose sample-area
effect Section~\ref{sec:overhead} constrains.

\section{Supplementary Controls}
\label{app:controls}

\subsection{Common-Support and Shifted-Crop Controls}
\label{app:support}
Each grid is restricted to the largest block of its own lattice whose
ground lies inside the intersection: an axis-aligned pixel rectangle on
the UTM side, a Morton-aligned cell block on the HEALPix side. Sizes are
normalised by the ground area each covers. Over ten regions this gives
ratios of $2.13$, $1.98$, $1.62$, $1.38$ and $1.35$ bytes per square
kilometre, against $2.06$, $1.94$, $1.59$, $1.19$ and $0.93$ over the
full footprints (Table~\ref{tab:support_controls}). Agreement is close
at the three tightest \term{targets} and drifts at the two loosest, as the
construction predicts. Morton alignment is restrictive, so the HEALPix
block is $256\times256$ cells (\SI{2.5}{\kilo\metre\squared}) against
a $405\times401$ pixel rectangle (\SI{17.6}{\kilo\metre\squared}).
Where files fall to a few kilobytes the fixed container cost weighs more
on the smaller raster, so this control is biased against HEALPix at the
loose end. Re-cutting the \term{UTM crop} at eight offsets of up to 16 native
pixels, with the HEALPix side fixed, changes each region's content by
about as much as the footprint mismatch does. Over four regions and
nine crops each, the median spread of the ratio across offsets is
$0.008$ at the tightest \term{target} and $0.030$ at the loosest, between
$1\%$ and $13\%$ of the effect measured.

\paragraph{Caveats on the spectral fit of Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}.}
Slope and response width trade against each other over a band of this
length: urban regions return the flattest spectra and the widest
responses ($\beta\approx1.2$, FWHM $\approx\SI{13.7}{m}$), forest regions
the steepest and narrowest ($2.35$, \SI{10.1}{m}), so the control uses
the joint median. Over the calmest water the fitted spectrum is nearly
flat ($\beta=-0.19$ over Lake Victoria, with $23\%$ of its variance
above half-Nyquist): those crops are dominated by sensor noise, which is
part of why the per-region range in Table~\ref{tab:support_controls} is
wide where the median is stable.

\subsection{The Reading Operator on Single-Frequency Fields}
\label{app:operator}
A constant field, the only test the nearest-cell read had passed, is also
passed by a badly registered, blurring or jittering operator. We
therefore place analytic single-frequency waves on the level-20 cell
centres of three regions, read them on the native UTM pixels, and
regress each against the same wave at those pixels; nothing is
resampled, so the residual is the operator alone. The operator is registered to well under a
cell: the largest fitted displacement over ten wavelengths and four
directions is \SI{2.1}{\milli\metre}. Its amplitude response is
unremarkable ($0.99$ and above beyond \SI{100}{m}, $0.94$ at \SI{37}{m},
$0.82$ near the native Nyquist and $0.53$ at the level-20 Nyquist) and
directional, by up to $2.29\times$ between the $45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$
directions at the finest wavelength, a measurement of the cell-shape
anisotropy. The residual that the amplitude-and-phase model does not
describe is \emph{almost the whole error}, $95\%$ of the RMSE at
\SI{21}{m} and essentially $100\%$ above \SI{60}{m}: reading the
containing cell acts as a sub-pixel position jitter, reproducing a
linear field with an RMS error of $0.34$ cells of gradient (against
$0.289$ for a uniform read anywhere within $\pm$ half a cell), about
\SI{2.1}{m} on a \SI{10}{m} native pixel. A single cell lights exactly
one native pixel in two of the three regions and \emph{none} in the
third, since a \SI{6.2}{m} cell need not contain any \SI{10}{m} pixel
centre: this operator can drop a stored cell entirely. On a band-limited
field with the spectrum of the imagery ($0.19\%$ of its variance above
half the native Nyquist frequency, against the $0.21\%$ measured on the
crops), the \term{read} alone produces an RMSE of $14.0$--$14.4\%$ of the
field's standard deviation, against a floor of about $10\%$ on the real
tiles; the two agree, because the Clough--Tocher smoothing of the tile
removes the gradients a jitter turns into error.

What the kernel of that \term{read} costs in rate is the other half of
Section~\ref{sec:operator}. With a uniform dead-zone quantiser in an
orthonormal wavelet domain as error source, whose error is close to white
in cell space, $68.9\%$ of the error energy lies in cells the product
never reads. The product error is no smaller for it: the RMS of the
transmitted error is $1.00$ times its RMS over all cells, because a
gather leaves the error per read pixel unchanged, so a coarser step gains
nothing ($0.1\%$ of the rate). What the kernel reveals is allocation,
bits spent on cells nothing depends on, with a bound of
$1/0.308\approx3.2\times$ that a uniform quantiser cannot reach. Seen
from the sweep, $54.0\%$ of the $2\,800$ lossy operating points of the
extended ladders reach a cell-space RMSE below the floor of
Table~\ref{tab:floors}. A \term{read} whose kernel attenuates coding error, such
as the forward operator of the PSF-aware \term{transfer}, would let a codec
target the product's distortion instead of the \term{carrier}'s and save bytes.
That gain would belong to the reader, whose kernel is large precisely
because the \term{carrier} is oversampled relative to it, and would hold only
for a product consumed through that operator, since noise shaped into
its stop-band stays visible to anyone who reads the \term{carrier} as an image.
It is not measured here, because scoring the PSF-aware \term{carrier} against
the observation is not meaningful (Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}).

\subsection{Codec Audit}
\label{app:codec_audit}
The JPEG~XL sweep extends beyond its quality-level ladder, which
saturates near RMSE $10^{-3}$ on Sentinel-2 B04, with Butteraugli-distance
operating points; without them JPEG~XL is absent from the high-fidelity
\term{targets}. Every operating point was written to a file, closed, re-opened
and decoded by a reader written independently of the encoder. Over 424 audited points
(two regions, both grids, both input scenarios, the full sweeps) the
re-read bytes were identical to those written, and the independently
decoded array was identical to the encoder's to the last bit. Three
qualifications follow. Only ZFP writes a self-contained file. The image
codecs need a float32 minimum and span carried out of band, eight bytes
the reported sizes do not charge, at most $0.17\%$ of the smallest size
reported here; the DWT baseline charges four bytes for a scale it never
writes. ``Lossless'' JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL are bit-identical to the
16-bit renormalization they are handed. That renormalization imposes a
floor of $5.7\times10^{-6}$ in median, equal on both grids and more
than an order of magnitude under the tightest \term{target}, which is why we
write \emph{lossless after requantization}. Byte counts depend on the input
type: the image codecs are indifferent within $0.3\%$, lossless JPEG~XL
is $34\%$ \emph{smaller} on integer digital numbers, and the two
absolute-error codecs move by two orders of magnitude, because an error
bound of $10^{-5}$ means one thing on reflectance in $[0,1]$ and another
on digital numbers in $[0,10^{4}]$; both grids are fed identically
everywhere, so no comparison is affected. A fourth observation belongs
to the grid comparison: converting to integer DN costs the \term{native crop}
essentially nothing (its values already sit on the DN lattice, within
$4\times10^{-9}$ to $9\times10^{-8}$ RMSE) and costs the HEALPix \term{carrier}
$2.9\times10^{-5}$, the uniform quantisation error of the $10^{-4}$ step,
because interpolation places the \term{carrier}'s values off that lattice. An
archive that stores integer DN must therefore keep the \term{carrier} in
floating point or accept a quantisation the native product never pays,
$29\%$ of the tightest \term{target}.

\subsection{What the Verdict Owes to Censored Sweep Points}
\label{app:censoring}
A \term{target} that a codec's coarsest setting already satisfies is met
without being measured: the size of that setting bounds what the codec
could have reached with a coarser one. Kept in the paired tests as if
they were estimates, such bounds can decide a verdict: with the original
ladders, the oracle codec was censored on 16 of the 40 regions at the
loosest \term{RMSE target}. Every ladder is therefore extended at its coarse end
(Section~\ref{sec:codecs}) and the rule of Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}
applies. Table~\ref{tab:censoring} reports the verdict under three
readings of the extended sweeps. The first keeps every censored pair as
a bound. The second uses the qualifying pairs alone; these are the
values reported in the paper. The third is the interval that bounds the
median of all 40 regions once each censored value is taken in the only
direction it can move: a censored HEALPix value can only fall, a
censored UTM value only rise. At
every reported \term{target} the three readings agree in sign, the two medians
differ by at most $0.01$, and the interval lies on one side of parity
except at $3\times10^{-3}$, where the paper reports no verdict. The
\emph{Sure} columns count the regions whose side of parity holds under
any reading of the bounds; the sign test in the last column assigns every
undetermined region to the side least favourable to the reported verdict
and separates the grids at every reported \term{target} but $3\times10^{-3}$.
The UTM side carries $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.3$; \term{RMSE targets} compare
sizes, budget \term{targets} compare errors.

\subsection{Is Each Window Still the Product Its Tile Came From?}
\label{app:window_radiometry}
A stored tile and the native window it was transferred from are two
files, and two files drift apart. Three measurements re-run a \term{transfer}
from the window, and for those the window must be the product the tile
came from. The geometric check that the window still \emph{covers} the
tile passes whatever the pixels contain, so a radiometric test is needed
(per-region values in the repository of
Section~\ref{sec:reproducibility}). Each stored tile is compared with
its own window sampled at the tile's cell centres by nearest pixel. That
sampling is cruder than the \term{transfer} which built the tile, so the two
differ even when the window is the right product, and the difference is
additive noise on the regressor. For noise uncorrelated with the signal,
the classical errors-in-variables case, it attenuates the regression
slope by exactly the squared correlation,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{slope} = \frac{\sigma_t^2}{\sigma_t^2 + \sigma_n^2}
                 = \mathrm{corr}^2 ,
\end{equation}
so that $\mathrm{slope}/\mathrm{corr}^2$ is one whatever the noise level
and the contrast of the scene, while a window that is a different
acquisition carries a gain and an offset of its own and breaks the
identity. Across the 40 regions the ratio has median $1.0032$, and 39 of
them lie in $[0.982, 1.011]$ over correlations from $0.910$ to $0.999$ and
scene contrasts spanning more than two orders of magnitude. Lake Geneva
lies at $1.884$. Fixed thresholds on the correlation, the slope and the
offset would also flag the two lowest-contrast lakes, because in a scene
of standard deviation $1.4\times10^{-3}$ any small offset is many
standard deviations; the identity above has neither failure mode.

A second test, exact and free of statistics, confirms it. Each tile's
file also holds the equal-area \term{UTM crop} it is compared against, written
in the same call, so that crop and the window's own pixels over the same
ground must agree to the bit. Over the 198 stored tiles 197 do, and the
one that does not is the Clough--Tocher tile of Lake Geneva, where
$405\,764$ of the $405\,769$ pixels differ. It carries a warning about
how the comparison must be made: the crop box recorded in each file is a pair of
pixel indices into the window the tile was built from, and
36 of the 40 windows have since been re-acquired at a smaller size, hence
with a different pixel origin. Comparing at the recorded indices
reports 39 mismatched tiles, an artefact of the indexing; recomputing
the box from the tile's centroid, a position on the Earth that survives
any re-cutting of the window, leaves one.


\subsection{The Two Level-19 Carriers}
\label{app:level19_direct}
A \term{carrier} built by Clough--Tocher at level 19 holds the interpolant
evaluated at the level-19 cell centres; the aggregate holds the mean of
the interpolant at the four level-20 centres inside each cell. The two
coincide only for a function that is affine over a cell, so the gap
between them is the curvature of the interpolant at the \SI{12.4}{m}
scale. The direct \term{carrier} uses the same native sub-window each stored
tile was transferred from, the same parent, the same Clough--Tocher
operator and the same checks; only the output level differs. Rebuilding
from the window makes this the one measurement in the paper that depends
on the window still holding the product its tile was built from, which
Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry} tests.

The two \term{carriers} are different arrays: they differ by $6.0\%$ of a
scene's standard deviation in RMS, $0.27$ times the level-19 floor
itself, so the curvature term is real and of the predicted order. Their
floors against the observation are nevertheless the same,
$8.72\times10^{-3}$ for the direct \term{carrier} against $8.69\times10^{-3}$
for the aggregate, a median ratio of $1.002$, within $5\%$ on 38 of the
39 regions, with the direct \term{carrier} lower on 15 of them and a widest
departure of $1.051$. The difference between the two \term{carriers} moves cell
values without moving the error against the scene; we have not measured
an inner product between that difference and the observation, so we do
not call it orthogonal. Against the level-20 floor the two coarser
\term{carriers} cost $2.25$ and $2.24$.

The excluded region is Lake Geneva. Rebuilt at level 19 its \term{carrier}
misses the aggregate by twice the scene's standard deviation, which looks
like a failure of the \term{transfer} at the coarser level. The cause lies
upstream: asked for level 20, where the rebuild must return the stored
tile, the same operator on the same samples misses it by the same amount,
an RMS of $1.97$ standard deviations at a correlation of $0.585$. The
window and the tile are related by a gain and an offset of their own, a
radiometric difference between two products
(Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry}). Kept, the region would leave the
median ratio at $1.002$; it is excluded because its input is no longer
the input the comparison assumes. One safeguard follows for anyone
rebuilding a \term{carrier} at a new level: a geometric check that the window
still covers the tile does not catch a window that has been re-acquired
(it passes on all 40 regions, this one included), while a \term{transfer} asked
for the level its tile is already stored at must return that tile.

\section{Reading a Target Against the Error the Product Already Carries}
\label{app:error_budget}\label{sec:error_budget}
Whether an \term{RMSE target} is an aggressive or a conservative place to
compress depends on how far the stored product already sits from what it
claims to represent. Clough--Tocher reproduces the \emph{observed} field,
the scene as the instrument's response left it; a PSF-aware
\term{transfer}~\cite{delouis2026resample} represents the \emph{latent} field,
the scene before the blur, and hands the codec more high-frequency
content to pay for. This appendix measures each \term{transfer} against the
\term{reference} it answers to, then re-expresses the results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} in units of that error.

The measurement is synthetic, because the latent field is not
observable. For each region an analytic random field is built from that
region's own fitted spectral slope and response width
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), convolved exactly with that
response and sampled at the native pixel positions to give the
observation. The truth at each \term{carrier} cell is the average of the field
over that cell's sixteen level-22 children, the cell-average estimand
the companion study reconstructs; in that study a reconstruction agrees
$2.36$ to $2.52$ times better with a cell-average \term{reference} than with a
point-sample one. Averaging four level-21 children instead moves the
blur by $2\%$ and the recovery fraction by less than a point. White
noise is added at the level Immerkaer's $3\times3$ estimator reads off
each region's own imagery, $6.2$--$13.4\%$ of the field's standard
deviation by class, because a deconvolving \term{transfer} amplifies noise as
it amplifies structure. The damping is $\lambda=10^{-3}$. In
Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}, \emph{Blur} is the RMSE between the
observed and latent fields; Clough--Tocher is scored against the observed
field and the PSF-aware \term{transfer} against the latent field; the last two
columns give the recovery fraction $1 - e_{\mathrm{psf}}/e_{\mathrm{blur}}$
at the measured noise level and in the noiseless control.

\input{tables/table_resampling_error}

Clough--Tocher's error against the observed field is small, $7.1\%$ to
$14.8\%$ of a scene's standard deviation by class (median
$3.0\times10^{-3}$ in reflectance), and largely the noise it faithfully
\term{transfers}: the noiseless control puts the interpolation itself at
$3.9\%$ to $10.3\%$. Its error against the \emph{latent} field is
several times larger, $1.1\times10^{-2}$ in median, because the whole
instrument blur stays in place: $12.5\%$ of the latent standard
deviation over forest and $53.8\%$ over urban scenes. These are the two
denominators. The strict reading is the first: Clough--Tocher promised
the observed field and delivered it to $3.0\times10^{-3}$. The
operative reading for a DGGS
archive is often the second, because a reader who treats the HEALPix
cube as ``the data'' compares it to the scene and already carries the
blur.

Table~\ref{tab:alpha_budget} reports both readings. $\kappa$ is the
compression \term{target} as a fraction of the denominator, at the tightest
reported \term{target} where HEALPix is the smaller representation; the
inflation columns give the \emph{measured} growth of the product's total
error, with the orthogonal value $\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-1$ in parentheses.
The choice of denominator moves $\kappa$ at the crossing by a factor of
three to five ($0.22$ against $0.70$ over water, $0.30$ against $1.07$
over agriculture). In general
$D_{\mathrm{total}}/D_0=\sqrt{1+\kappa^2+2\rho\kappa}$ with $\rho$ the
normalised inner product of the two errors, and
Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term} finds $\rho$ neither zero nor of one sign.
Against the latent scene, HEALPix becomes the smaller representation at
$\kappa=0.23$ over urban scenes, $0.30$ over agriculture, $0.22$ over
water and $0.76$ over forest, where the orthogonal formula puts the
inflation of the total error at $2.6\%$, $4.3\%$, $2.4\%$ and
$25.5\%$; the measured values, where the cross term of
Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term} is available, are $6.9\%$, $10.5\%$ and
$6.4\%$, about twice the orthogonal ones. Against the own estimand the
same crossings cost $\kappa=1.23$, $1.07$, $0.70$ and $1.57$: three of
the four lie above one, so on that yardstick the compression error at the
crossing exceeds the \term{transfer} error itself, and no crossing is cheap.
The choice between the two readings is a statement about the intended
use of the archive.

The deconvolving \term{transfer} is measured on the same scenes. Without
noise, the PSF-aware reconstruction recovers $+13.8\%$ of the blur over
agriculture, $+15.6\%$ over urban scenes and $+13.6\%$ over water
(class medians of the per-region fraction,
Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}), the range the companion study
reports, $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$, under the same recovery definition and
damping. Over forest the median is $-3.5\%$. The split is not one of
land cover but of response width. The synthetic scene of each region
carries the width fitted to that region's imagery, and the fit returns
narrow responses over the smoothest scenes, down to \SI{7.4}{m} FWHM.
On the 28 regions whose fitted width is \SI{11}{m} or more, every
reconstruction recovers, by $8\%$ to $20\%$; on the 11 regions below
\SI{11}{m}, where the blur is a few $10^{-4}$ and the inversion's own
error exceeds it, the best recovers $+3\%$ and the rest lose
(Spearman $\rho=0.87$ between fitted width and recovery). The companion
fixes the width at the nominal \SI{12.5}{m} on every scene, which is
why its figure is uniform across classes and ours is not. With the
measured noise added, recovery is positive on 18 of the 39 regions
against 30 of the 39 without it, and the class medians fall to between
$-52.3\%$ over forest and $+3.5\%$ over urban scenes: a deconvolution
amplifies noise at the frequencies it restores, and the companion's
headline figure is a noise-free one. Two differences with the companion's
protocol remain untested here, its \SI{10}{m} square detector aperture,
which the scalar Gaussian inverse does not model, and its high-resolution
image textures in place of a power-law field; at the nominal width and
without noise the two harnesses agree without them.

\section{Is the Compression Error Orthogonal to the Transfer Error?}
\label{app:cross_term}\label{sec:cross_term}
Writing $e_0 = x - \text{truth}$ for the \term{transfer} error on the \term{carrier}
cells and $e_c = \tilde{x} - x$ for the compression error, the exact
relation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{D_{\mathrm{total}}}{D_0} = \sqrt{1 + \kappa^2 + 2\rho\kappa},
\qquad \rho = \frac{\langle e_0, e_c\rangle}{D_0 D_c},
\label{eq:cross_term}
\end{equation}
of which quadrature is the $\rho = 0$ case. We measure $\rho$ on the
synthetic scene of Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}, where both errors are
available exactly, on the same level-20 cells and in the same norm. In
Table~\ref{tab:cross_term}, operating points are grouped around each
reported $\kappa$ within a factor of $1.5$; $n$ counts the operating
points in each window (region, codec and coding setting together, over
the same 40 regions), and a window holding fewer than five is not
reported. The last two columns give the growth of the product's total
error as measured and as quadrature predicts it.

\input{tables/table_cross_term}

The sign depends on the \term{reference}. Against the \emph{observed} field,
$\rho$ is negative and grows in magnitude with $\kappa$, reaching $-0.33$
at $\kappa=1$: there the \term{transfer} error is dominated by the sensor noise
the \term{transfer} faithfully carried (what takes Clough--Tocher from
$0.8$--$1.5\%$ of a scene's standard deviation without noise to
$5.0$--$11.0\%$ with it), and a lossy coder discards part of that noise
with everything else it discards at high frequency. The two errors partly
cancel, and quadrature \emph{overstates} the damage, by $11$ percentage
points at $\kappa=0.5$ and $24$ at $\kappa=1$. Against the \emph{latent}
scene the sign reverses, $\rho$ rising to $+0.17$ at $\kappa=0.2$: there
the \term{transfer} error is the blur the \term{transfer} did not remove, a
\emph{deficit} of high-frequency content, and a wavelet coder's error is
another such deficit. The two align, and the orthogonal formula
\emph{understates} the inflation, by $2.5$ percentage points at
$\kappa=0.2$ and $6.6$ at $\kappa=0.5$, roughly a factor of two on the
figures Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget} reports. Split by codec, the
wavelet coder carries the entire effect ($\rho = -0.18$ against the
observed field and $+0.16$ to $+0.29$ against the latent scene at the
crossing values of $\kappa$), while ZFP, whose block-floating-point
quantisation does not sort by spatial frequency, stays orthogonal under
both \term{references} ($|\rho| \le 0.03$). This is consistent with the
frequency-selective nature of the wavelet coder; two codecs do not
establish a rule for coders in general. The sign of $\rho$ does not
depend on the synthetic field; its magnitude does, because the blur here
is an exact Gaussian attenuation of a power law. The sampled range of
$\kappa$ covers every class's crossing against both denominators, so
Table~\ref{tab:alpha_budget} reports a measured inflation in each cell.

\section{Detailed Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The verdict is conditional on the transfer, by about a factor of two.} Rebuilding the \term{carrier} with a PSF-aware or an inverse-distance \term{transfer} costs $1.2$ to $2.5$ times the bytes at the same coding RMSE (Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}), more than the margin by which HEALPix leads at the loosest \term{targets}. The argument does not extend to \term{transfers} we have not swept, and a non-deconvolving \term{transfer} as smooth as Clough--Tocher, which would isolate deconvolution from smoothness, has not been tested;
    \item \textbf{The equal-area UTM square matches the tile's nominal projected area}, neither its exact geodesic area (UTM is conformal, and $636.7$ pixels were rounded to $637$) nor its shape (Section~\ref{sec:utm_crop}); the fixed $2.58\times$ oversampling would not change under a footprint-exact crop;
    \item \textbf{The overhead is a parameter because only part of it is measured.} The container cost of each side is measured at under half a percent and the overlap of the Sentinel-2 tiling at $21\%$ from its published geometry, while padding and metadata are assumed. $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ has not been measured from complete tiled product files, nor swept over $[1.0,1.5]$ for the \term{matched-budget} protocol, and for compressed storage the overhead is assumed to apply proportionally to the compressed byte count;
    \item \textbf{The level-19 comparison was made on a cell-averaged carrier; the directly evaluated one agrees.} Appendix~\ref{app:level19_direct} finds the two within $0.2\%$ in floor against the observation, with the widest departure at $1.051$, on 39 regions; including Lake Geneva moves the median ratio by less than $0.001$. The kernel ablation and the resampling-error study, which also re-run a \term{transfer}, rest on the same 39; no measurement read from a stored tile is affected;
    \item \textbf{The loosest targets rest on fewer regions.} The failing pairs are the lowest-contrast scenes: none at the two tightest \term{RMSE targets}, one at $10^{-3}$, three at $3\times10^{-3}$ and eight at $10^{-2}$, with no urban scene among the absent. The $40\%$ and $20\%$ budgets qualify on $0\%$ and $65\%$ of the regions and are withheld;
    \item \textbf{Neither reconstruction reference is the whole story.} The floor of Table~\ref{tab:floors} is an upper bound on what the representation costs, since the \term{read} alone can account for it (Section~\ref{sec:operator}). What stays open is the $3\times10^{-3}$ \term{target}, bracketed for $57\%$ of regions under the interpolating \term{read}. The PSF-aware \term{carrier} is not scored against the observation at all: its estimand is the latent field, so such a score would charge it for the content it was built to restore. A comparison of the two \term{transfers} at equal reconstruction error needs a synthetic scene with a known latent field, degraded by the instrument response, transferred by both, compressed, and scored against that field; it is the first experiment a follow-up should run;
    \item \textbf{The super-resolution surcharge is measured; the recovery it buys is reproduced only without noise.} The deconvolved \term{carrier} costs $1.27$ to $2.45$ times the Clough--Tocher one, and the PSF/IDW differential of $3$--$13\%$ suggests that deconvolution alone is unlikely to account for that surcharge; because the two \term{transfers} are not matched in smoothness or interpolation class, the differential does not isolate or bound the deconvolution contribution. In our synthetic harness the PSF-aware reconstruction recovers the companion's $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$ of the blur on the regions whose fitted response width is at or above \SI{11}{m}, loses on the narrower ones, and keeps little of it once noise is added at the level measured on the imagery (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). The companion's square detector aperture and image textures are not modelled here. The storage price is reportable; the quality it buys on real tiles is established only by the companion study;
    \item \textbf{The oracle-best-codec comparison is an envelope, and the crossover is codec-dependent.} Four of the five codecs held fixed on both grids reproduce the regime-dependent pattern; ZFP does not (Section~\ref{sec:same_codec});
    \item \textbf{The byte and type audit covers two regions.} Over 424 points it found no gap between reported and on-disk sizes and no disagreement with an independent decoder; it tests the adapters, which do not vary by region. The image codecs' eight-byte scale-and-offset sidecar is not charged to their sizes, and the DWT baseline charges four bytes for a scale it never writes;
    \item \textbf{The footprint mismatch is constrained, not bounded in the strict sense}: the three controls that address it run on 40, ten and four regions, so they suggest that the mismatch is unlikely to explain the main trend rather than bounding it mathematically, and they do not isolate what it contributes to the crossover at $10^{-2}$. The intersection fractions come from the convex hull of the cell centres, and geodesic areas have not been computed;
    \item \textbf{Codecs are swept over their own quality parameter}, so residual convention effects cannot be excluded;
    \item \textbf{Single band, single sensor, one epoch.} Only Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m} is evaluated, with no derived product: how the measured errors propagate into an index such as NDVI needs B08 and a common mask, and lossy compression is documented to change downstream land-cover classification at ratios inside the range studied here~\cite{zabala2011lossy}. Gridded model output is a different problem: it is already a cell average, so there is no \term{transfer} error to charge; its dominant redundancy is temporal, which none of the codecs favoured here exploits; its HEALPix level is a free design parameter; and conservation adds a constraint an RMSE budget does not enforce;
    \item \textbf{The HEALPix side uses a deliberately simple resampling.} Clough--Tocher is PSF-agnostic; the operational PSF-aware resampler~\cite{delouis2026resample} places more high-frequency detail in the \term{carrier}, so the HEALPix-side ratios here are those of a tile that holds no more than the product; a PSF-aware tile holds more and costs more (Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}). Low power in the observed field bounds nothing about the latent one: the fitted spectra put $2.3\%$ (forest) to $31.1\%$ (urban) of the latent variance above the native Nyquist (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget});
    \item \textbf{Forty regions, one tile each.} Independent replicate tiles within a region would be needed to separate scene-to-scene from tile-to-tile variability. The image codecs receive a 16-bit requantization with an RMSE floor of $5.7\times10^{-6}$ in median, so the reported lossless points are bit-identical after that step only;
    \item \textbf{Ratios over near-constant scenes are fragile.} Over deep water at the loosest \term{targets}, compressed files fall below \SI{10}{kB} and individual ratios range from $0.07$ to $6.1$, though the class medians behave like the others;
    \item \textbf{Learned compression is not evaluated.} Comparing two grids with a neural codec~\cite{balle2017endtoend,valsesia2019onboard} needs models trained symmetrically on both, a study of its own;
    \item \textbf{Codec choice was evaluated on compression efficiency alone.} JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL support partial, range-based reads of a chunk, ZFP at the block level, SZ3 and the DWT baseline neither; where partial access is required, the strongest compressor may be the wrong choice;
    \item \textbf{Global-sphere behaviour is not measured} (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
\end{itemize}
