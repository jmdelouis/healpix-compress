\section{Supplementary Methodology}
\label{app:method}

\subsection{Two Literatures Behind the Expectation}
\label{app:literatures}
Two literatures make the expectation precise. Approximation theory: the
$L_2$ error of representing a continuous field by samples on a grid of
step $T$ is governed, to leading order, by an error kernel whose decay is
set by the representation's approximation order, so refining the step
buys a predictable and bounded amount of
accuracy~\cite{blu1999fourier,blu1999approximation,unser2000sampling}; on
a field the instrument has already band-limited, refining the grid past
the response width adds samples whose values are very nearly determined
by their neighbours. Coding: wavelet coefficient magnitudes persist across
scales, and zerotree coders exploit that persistence
implicitly~\cite{shapiro1993ezw,said1996spiht}; modelling it as a hidden
Markov tree over the scale hierarchy~\cite{crouse1998hmt}, or coding each
coefficient conditioned on its parent~\cite{buccigrossi1999joint}, turns
the same structure into bits saved. An added octave that carries almost
no variance is exactly the case those models handle best, which is why
the oversampling penalty is expected to be much smaller than the factor
of~\eqref{eq:alpha}. How little that octave carries is only partly
visible in the native product: a median of $0.21\%$ of each scene's
variance lies above half the native Nyquist
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), and that band,
$0.025$--$\SI{0.05}{m^{-1}}$, differs from the octave level~20 adds over
level~19, $0.040$--$\SI{0.080}{m^{-1}}$, which lies mostly above the
native product's own Nyquist. The carrier can show it, and
Section~\ref{sec:level_choice} does: the octave holds $3.07\%$ of the
tile's variance, two orders of magnitude more than a spectral
extrapolation of the $0.21\%$ suggests.

\subsection{A Direct Test of a Quadtree-Native Coder}
\label{app:quadtree}
The ideal-entropy rate of a pyramid built on the quadtree itself, coded
with a parent-conditioned context model in the spirit
of~\cite{buccigrossi1999joint}, is $1.08\times$ the bytes the best generic
codec spends at equal distortion on the same 40 tiles with the exact
quadtree Haar, and $0.93\times$ with a quadtree-native CDF~5/3 lifting.
Both are bounds that charge nothing for coding, and both lie within what
an implementation loses to its own bound. The link between scales is real
(conditioning on the parent saves $5.9\%$ of an order-0 rate, twice the
$3.0\%$ an intra-band neighbour saves) and small, and the generic codecs
already run a better wavelet on this very quadtree.

\subsection{What the Container Costs}
\label{app:container}
The chunked archive this accounting describes is itself standardised, and
the coverage and datacube model behind it sets what a container must hold
beyond the samples~\cite{baumann2025datacube}. One level-20 chunk in a
Zarr~v3 store~\cite{moore2023zarr} costs \SI{737}{\byte} of group and
array metadata, $0.018\%$ of the raw tile, and the cell identifiers of a
contiguous NESTED block are implicit. A complete single-tile deflate
Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF~\cite{ogc_cog2023} of the equal-area crop costs
\SI{8461}{\byte} of header, tag directory and near-empty payload, $0.52\%$
of its raw size. Container format therefore accounts for at most half a
percent on either side. What a $30\%$ overhead describes is granule
geometry (swath padding, inter-granule overlap, per-granule metadata), a
property of one product layout, which is why it is carried as a
parameter.

\section{Supplementary Controls}
\label{app:controls}

\subsection{Common-Support and Shifted-Crop Controls}
\label{app:support}
Restricting each grid to the largest region of its own lattice whose
ground lies inside the intersection (the largest axis-aligned pixel
rectangle on the UTM side, the largest Morton-aligned cell block on the
HEALPix side) and normalising by the ground area each covers gives, over
ten regions, ratios of $2.13$, $1.98$, $1.62$, $1.38$ and $1.35$ bytes per
square kilometre, against $2.06$, $1.94$, $1.59$, $1.19$ and $0.93$ over
the full footprints. Agreement is close at the three tightest targets and
drifts at the two loosest, as the construction predicts: Morton alignment
is restrictive, so the HEALPix block is $256\times256$ cells
(\SI{2.5}{\kilo\metre\squared}) against a $405\times401$ pixel rectangle
(\SI{17.6}{\kilo\metre\squared}) on the native side, and where files fall
to a few kilobytes the fixed container cost is a larger share of the
smaller raster's total. This control confirms the tight end and is biased
against HEALPix at the loose end. The cheapest control bounds the mismatch
most directly: re-cutting the UTM crop at eight offsets of up to 16 native
pixels, with the HEALPix side fixed, changes each region's content by
about as much as the footprint mismatch does, and over four regions and
nine crops each the median spread of the ratio across offsets is $0.008$
at the tightest target and $0.030$ at the loosest, between $1\%$ and
$13\%$ of the effect measured.

\paragraph{Caveats on the spectral fit of Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}.}
Slope and response width trade against each other over a band of this
length: urban regions return the flattest spectra and the widest
responses ($\beta\approx1.2$, FWHM $\approx\SI{13.7}{m}$), forest regions
the steepest and narrowest ($2.35$, \SI{10.1}{m}), so neither parameter is
separately well determined and the control uses the joint median. Over
the calmest water the fitted spectrum is nearly flat ($\beta=-0.19$ over
Lake Victoria, with $23\%$ of its variance above half-Nyquist): those
crops are dominated by sensor noise, which is part of why the per-region
range in Table~\ref{tab:support_controls} is wide where the median is
stable.

\subsection{The Reading Operator on Single-Frequency Fields}
\label{app:operator}
The nearest-cell read had been verified only on a constant field, which a
badly registered, blurring or jittering operator all pass. We therefore
place analytic single-frequency waves on the level-20 cell centres, read
them on the native UTM pixels, and regress each against the same wave at
those pixels; nothing is resampled, so the residual is the operator plus
the unavoidable discretisation of reading a cell centre.
Fig.~\ref{fig:operator_response} shows the amplitude and phase of the
response, and Table~\ref{tab:operator_tests} the recovered amplitude by
wave direction in the projected frame and the largest phase shift in
metres.

Three things come out of it, on three regions. The operator is registered
to well under a cell: the largest fitted displacement over ten wavelengths
and four directions is \SI{2.1}{\milli\metre}. Its amplitude response is
unremarkable ($0.99$ and above beyond \SI{100}{m}, $0.94$ at \SI{37}{m},
$0.82$ near the native Nyquist and $0.53$ at the level-20 Nyquist) and
directional, by up to $2.29\times$ between the $45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$
directions at the finest wavelength, the first quantitative measurement
here of the cell-shape anisotropy. The third matters most: the residual
that the amplitude-and-phase model does not describe is \emph{almost the
whole error}, $95\%$ of the RMSE at \SI{21}{m} and essentially $100\%$ at
every wavelength above \SI{60}{m}. Reading the containing cell acts as a
sub-pixel position jitter. A linear field is reproduced with an RMS error
of $0.34$ cells of gradient (against $0.289$ for a uniform read anywhere
within $\pm$ half a cell), a positional jitter of about \SI{2.1}{m} on a
\SI{10}{m} native pixel. A single cell lights exactly one native pixel in
two of the three regions and \emph{none} in the third, since a \SI{6.2}{m}
cell need not contain any \SI{10}{m} pixel centre: this operator can drop
a stored cell entirely.

\subsection{Codec Audit}
\label{app:codec_audit}
Reaching the codec ranking of Section~\ref{sec:codec_choice} required
extending the JPEG~XL sweep beyond its quality-level ladder, which
saturates near RMSE $10^{-3}$ on Sentinel-2 B04; without extra
Butteraugli-distance operating points, JPEG~XL is absent from the
high-fidelity targets. The same shift towards JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL at
aggressive compression appears on the native UTM side, a grid with no
quadtree structure, so we read it as a general property of these codecs
on natural imagery. In Table~\ref{tab:byte_audit}, \emph{self-contained}
asks whether the file alone suffices to decode it; \emph{sidecar} counts
the bytes a decoder needs that the reported size does not charge; the
16-bit floor is the RMSE that renormalisation costs before any
compression.

\input{tables/table_byte_audit}

Every operating point was written to a file, closed, re-opened and decoded
by a reader written independently of the encoder. Over 424 audited points
(two regions, both grids, both input scenarios, the full sweeps) the
re-read bytes were identical to those written, and the independently
decoded array was identical to the encoder's to the last bit. Three
qualifications follow. Only ZFP writes a self-contained file: the image
codecs need a float32 minimum and span carried out of band (eight bytes
the reported sizes do not charge, at most $0.17\%$ of the smallest size
reported here), and the DWT baseline charges four bytes for a scale it
never writes. ``Lossless'' JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL are bit-identical to the
16-bit renormalization they are handed and to nothing else; that
renormalization imposes a floor of $5.7\times10^{-6}$ in median, equal on
both grids and more than an order of magnitude under the tightest target,
which is why we write \emph{lossless after requantization}. Byte counts
depend on the input type: the image codecs are indifferent within $0.3\%$,
lossless JPEG~XL is $34\%$ \emph{smaller} on integer digital numbers, and
the two absolute-error codecs move by two orders of magnitude, because an
error bound of $10^{-5}$ means one thing on reflectance in $[0,1]$ and
another on digital numbers in $[0,10^{4}]$; both grids are fed identically
everywhere, so no comparison is affected, and a reader re-running on
integer input must rescale the ZFP and SZ3 error bounds. A fourth
observation belongs to the grid comparison: converting to integer DN
costs the native crop essentially nothing (its values already sit on the
DN lattice, within $4\times10^{-9}$ to $9\times10^{-8}$ RMSE) and costs
the HEALPix carrier $2.9\times10^{-5}$, the uniform quantisation error of
the $10^{-4}$ step, because interpolation places the carrier's values off
that lattice. An archive that stores integer DN must therefore keep the
carrier in floating point or accept a quantisation the native product
never pays; at $29\%$ of the tightest target this is a cost of the
transfer step, not decisive and not negligible.

\subsection{What the Verdict Owes to Censored Sweep Points}
\label{app:censoring}
A target that a codec's coarsest setting already satisfies is met and not
measured: the size of that setting bounds what the codec could have
reached with a coarser one. An earlier version of this study kept such
bounds in the paired tests as if they were estimates, and at the loosest
RMSE target the oracle codec was censored in this way on 16 of the 40
regions, enough for the sign of the verdict there to rest on how the
bounds were read. Every ladder was therefore extended at its coarse end
(Section~\ref{sec:codecs}) and the rule of Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}
adopted. Table~\ref{tab:censoring} reports the verdict under three
readings of the extended sweeps: the ratios with every censored pair kept
as a bound, the ratios on the qualifying pairs alone (the values reported
in the paper), and the interval that bounds the median of all 40 regions
once each censored value is taken in the only direction it can move (a
censored HEALPix value can only fall, a censored UTM value only rise). At
every reported target the three readings agree in sign, the two medians
differ by at most $0.01$, and the interval lies on one side of parity

\emph{Sure} columns count the regions whose side of parity holds under any
reading of the bounds, for HEALPix, for UTM, and undetermined; the sign
test in the last column assigns every undetermined region to the side
least favourable to the reported verdict and separates the grids at every
reported target but $3\times10^{-3}$. The regions still censored at
$10^{-2}$ are those whose contrast does not exceed the target: no codec
setting can be coarser than a constant, and for those regions the
comparison has no content. The UTM side carries
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.3$; RMSE targets compare sizes, budget targets
compare errors.

\subsection{Is Each Window Still the Product Its Tile Came From?}
\label{app:window_radiometry}
A stored tile and the native window it was transferred from are two
files, and two files drift apart. The compression sweeps, the storage
accounting, the level choice and the reference floors all read the stored
tile and never reopen the window. Three measurements reopen it because
they re-run a transfer --- the kernel ablation, the resampling-error study
and the level-19 comparison --- and for those the window must be the
product the tile came from. The pipeline already checks that the window
still \emph{covers} the tile; that test is geometric and passes whatever
the pixels contain.

The radiometric test is Table~\ref{tab:window_radiometry}. Each stored
tile is compared with its own window sampled at the tile's cell centres by
nearest pixel. Nearest-pixel sampling is cruder than the transfer that
built the tile, so the two differ even when the window is the right
product; that difference is additive noise on the regressor, which
attenuates the regression slope by exactly the squared correlation,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{slope} = \frac{\sigma_t^2}{\sigma_t^2 + \sigma_n^2}
                 = \mathrm{corr}^2 ,
\end{equation}
so that $\mathrm{slope}/\mathrm{corr}^2$ is one whatever the noise level
and whatever the contrast of the scene, while a window that is a different
acquisition carries a gain and an offset of its own and breaks the
identity. Across the 40 regions the ratio has median $1.0032$, and 39 of
them lie in $[0.982, 1.011]$ over correlations from $0.910$ to $0.999$ and
scene contrasts spanning more than two orders of magnitude. Lake Geneva
lies at $1.884$. A first version of this audit tested the correlation, the
slope and the offset against fixed thresholds instead, and flagged two
more regions, the two lowest-contrast lakes, whose absolute residuals are
among the smallest of the forty: in a scene of standard deviation
$1.4\times10^{-3}$, any small offset is many standard deviations. The
identity above has neither failure mode.

A second test, exact and free of statistics, confirms it and carries a
warning. Each tile's file also holds the equal-area UTM crop it is
compared against, written in the same call, so that crop and the window's
own pixels over the same ground must agree to the bit. Over the 198 stored
tiles --- five transfers on each region, three on one --- 197 do, and the
one that does not is the Clough--Tocher tile of Lake Geneva, where
$405\,764$ of the $405\,769$ pixels differ. The warning is in how the
comparison must be made: the crop box recorded in each file is a pair of
pixel indices into the window the tile was built from, and
36 of the 40 windows have since been re-acquired at a smaller size, hence
with a different pixel origin. Comparing at the recorded indices
reports 39 mismatched tiles that are an artefact of the indexing; recomputing the box
from the tile's centroid, a position on the Earth that survives any
re-cutting of the window, leaves one.

\section{Reading a Target Against the Error the Product Already Carries}
\label{app:error_budget}\label{sec:error_budget}
An RMSE target means little on its own: whether $3\times10^{-3}$ is an
aggressive or a conservative place to compress depends on how far the
stored product already sits from what it claims to represent, and that
distance differs between transfers. Clough--Tocher interpolation is a
smooth interpolant meant to reproduce the \emph{observed} field, the scene
as the instrument's response left it. A PSF-aware transfer of the kind the
companion study proposes~\cite{delouis2026resample} represents the
\emph{latent} field, the scene before the response blurred it, and so
hands the codec a product with more high-frequency content to pay for.
Scoring the two at the same absolute RMSE compares errors on two different
quantities. This section measures each transfer against the reference it
answers to, then re-expresses the results of Section~\ref{sec:results} in
units of that error.

The measurement must be synthetic, because the latent field is not
observable. For each region we build an analytic random field from that
region's own fitted spectral slope and response width
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), convolve it exactly with that
response, and sample the result at the native pixel positions to obtain
the observation. The width is an effective parameter of a spectral model,
fitted jointly with the slope and partly degenerate with it; it makes the
synthetic field resemble the imagery, which is all the argument needs. The
truth at each carrier cell is the average of the field over that cell's
sixteen level-22 children, the cell-average estimand the companion study
reconstructs (a reconstruction agrees $2.36$ to $2.52$ times better with a
cell-average reference than with a point-sample one in that study);
averaging four level-21 children instead moves the blur by $2\%$ and the
recovery fraction by less than a point. Sensor noise is essential: a
deconvolving transfer amplifies noise as it amplifies structure, at the
frequencies a codec pays most for, so a noiseless field favours it twice.
We add white noise at the level Immerkaer's $3\times3$ estimator reads off
each region's own imagery, $6.2$--$13.4\%$ of the field's standard
deviation by class; the damping is $\lambda=10^{-3}$, the value the
companion study reports at.

In Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}, \emph{Blur} is the RMSE between the
observed and latent fields, what a non-deconvolving transfer leaves on the
table by construction; Clough--Tocher is scored against the observed field
and the PSF-aware transfer against the latent field; the last two columns
give the recovery fraction $1 - e_{\mathrm{psf}}/e_{\mathrm{blur}}$ at
the measured noise level and in the noiseless control.

\input{tables/table_resampling_error}

Clough--Tocher's error against the observed field is small ($5.0\%$ to
$11.0\%$ of a scene's standard deviation, median $1.9\times10^{-3}$ in
reflectance) and dominated by the noise it faithfully transfers; the
noiseless control puts the interpolation itself at $0.8\%$ to $1.5\%$.
Its error against the \emph{latent} field is an order of magnitude
larger, $1.7\times10^{-2}$ in median, because a transfer that does not
deconvolve leaves the whole instrument blur in place: that blur is
$22.1\%$ of the latent standard deviation over forest and $73.8\%$ over
urban scenes, the spread following the fitted slope. These are the two
denominators, an order of magnitude apart, so which applies is a question
the paper puts to the reader. The strict reading is the first:
Clough--Tocher promised the observed field and delivered it to
$1.9\times10^{-3}$. The operative reading for a DGGS archive is often the
second, because a reader who treats the HEALPix cube as ``the data''
compares it to the scene and already carries the blur.

Table~\ref{tab:alpha_budget} reports both readings for the Clough--Tocher
transfer. \emph{Own estimand} is its RMSE against the observed field;
\emph{latent scene} its RMSE against the unblurred scene, the yardstick
that applies when the carrier is read as if it were the scene. $\kappa$ is
the compression target as a fraction of that denominator. The two errors
add in quadrature only if their cross term vanishes, and it does not, so
the inflation columns give the \emph{measured} growth of the product's
total error with the orthogonal value $\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-1$ in
parentheses. The target shown is the tightest reported one at which
HEALPix is the smaller representation, set by the ratios alone and so
common to both denominators. The choice of denominator moves $\kappa$ at
the crossing by a factor of five at the most expensive class ($0.50$
against $2.28$), which is why both are reported.

Table~\ref{tab:alpha_budget} and Fig.~\ref{fig:error_budget} restate the
crossovers of Section~\ref{sec:results} in these units. In general
$D_{\mathrm{total}}/D_0=\sqrt{1+\kappa^2+2\rho\kappa}$ with $\rho$ the
normalised inner product of the two errors, and $\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-1$ is
the $\rho=0$ case; Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term} measures $\rho$ and finds
it neither zero nor of one sign. Against the latent scene, HEALPix becomes
the smaller representation at $\kappa=0.13$ over urban scenes and
$\kappa=0.19$ over agriculture (total error inflated by a measured $2.5\%$
and $5.1\%$ for a $30\%$ and a $12\%$ saving in bytes), at $\kappa=0.50$
over forest, for $16.0\%$ inflation and a $17\%$ saving, and at
$\kappa=0.14$ over water, for $3.0\%$ inflation and only a $2\%$ saving.
Each is about twice what the orthogonal formula would give ($0.9\%$,
$1.8\%$, $11.9\%$, $1.0\%$). All four classes therefore cross while the
compression error is a fraction of the error the product already carries;
what separates them is what the crossing buys, from $30\%$ of the bytes
over urban scenes down to $2\%$ over water. Against the own estimand the
same four crossings cost $\kappa=1.54$, $2.28$, $1.59$ and $1.22$,
measured inflations of $37\%$, $115\%$, $40\%$ and $19\%$ ($84\%$,
$149\%$, $88\%$ and $58\%$ under the orthogonal formula, which overstates
them here): on that yardstick no crossing is cheap. That asymmetry
sharpens the conditional conclusion of Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}.
For a product used as an information-preserving copy of the scene, the
regime where HEALPix becomes the cheaper representation is reached while
the compression error is still a small fraction of the error the product
carries anyway. All four classes cross under the latent-scene reference,
although the storage gain is substantial in only three of them; over water
the crossover yields only about a $2\%$ saving. For a product held to the
observation it was made from, no crossing is cheap. The choice between the two
readings is a statement about the intended use of the archive.

The deconvolving transfer does not yet support the same accounting. In
this harness the PSF-aware reconstruction recovers no blur consistently at
the measured noise level: by class the recovery runs from $-5.9\%$ over
forest to $+6.0\%$ over water (Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}), positive
on 16 of the 39 regions; in a noiseless control it is a small but
systematic $+3.0\%$ to $+6.5\%$, positive on 38 of the 39. The companion
study reports $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$, noise-free, under the same recovery
definition and damping, on a synthetic observation averaged over a
\SI{10}{m} square detector aperture that the scalar Gaussian inverse does
not model, which makes their recovery conservative. On our side we can
name three candidates. The noise is the largest term: a deconvolution
amplifies it at the frequencies it tries to restore, which is why our own
noiseless control moves from about zero to $+5.4\%$ in median. The latent
field is the second: a power law extrapolated to the level-20 Nyquist
places $2.3\%$ (forest) to $31.1\%$ (urban) of the latent variance above
the \emph{native} Nyquist, in a band the fitted response attenuates to a
few percent of its amplitude, so most of the blur we charge is
unrecoverable by any damping; this is also why the blur is $22$ to $74\%$
of the latent standard deviation here, and why urban scenes recover
least. The depth of the cell average, the third, we tested and exclude. A
residual gap remains and we do not attribute it. The super-resolution
surcharge (what a deconvolved product costs to store at equal $\kappa$)
therefore cannot be quantified from the present measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Is the Compression Error Orthogonal to the Transfer Error?}
\label{app:cross_term}\label{sec:cross_term}
Converting a compression target into a growth of the product's total
error needs to know how the two errors combine. Writing
$e_0 = x - \text{truth}$ for the transfer error on the carrier cells and
$e_c = \tilde{x} - x$ for the compression error, the exact relation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{D_{\mathrm{total}}}{D_0} = \sqrt{1 + \kappa^2 + 2\rho\kappa},
\qquad \rho = \frac{\langle e_0, e_c\rangle}{D_0 D_c},
\label{eq:cross_term}
\end{equation}
of which quadrature is the $\rho = 0$ case. At $\kappa=0.5$, $\rho=-1$
would \emph{halve} the product's error and $\rho=+1$ would grow it by
half. We therefore measure $\rho$, on the synthetic scene of
Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget} so that both errors are available exactly,
on the same level-20 cells and in the same norm. In
Table~\ref{tab:cross_term}, operating points are grouped around each
reported $\kappa$ within a factor of $1.5$; $n$ counts the operating
points in each window (region, codec and coding setting together, over
the same 40 regions), and a window holding fewer than five is not
reported. The last two columns give the growth of the product's total
error as measured and as quadrature predicts it.

The sign depends on the reference. Against the \emph{observed} field,
$\rho$ is negative and grows in magnitude with $\kappa$, reaching $-0.33$
at $\kappa=1$: there the transfer error is dominated by the sensor noise
the transfer faithfully carried (what takes Clough--Tocher from
$0.8$--$1.5\%$ of a scene's standard deviation without noise to
$5.0$--$11.0\%$ with it, Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}), and a lossy
coder discards part of that noise with everything else it discards at
high frequency. The two errors partly cancel, and quadrature
\emph{overstates} the damage, by $11$ percentage points at $\kappa=0.5$
and $24$ at $\kappa=1$. Against the \emph{latent} scene the sign reverses,
$\rho$ rising to $+0.17$ at $\kappa=0.2$: there the transfer error is the
blur the transfer did not remove, a \emph{deficit} of high-frequency
content, and a wavelet coder's error is another such deficit. The two
align, and the orthogonal formula \emph{understates} the inflation, by
$2.5$ percentage points at $\kappa=0.2$ and $6.6$ at $\kappa=0.5$, roughly
a factor of two on the figures Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget} reports.

The codec that does not take part makes this a mechanism. Splitting
Fig.~\ref{fig:cross_term} by codec, the wavelet coder carries the entire
effect ($\rho = -0.18$ against the observed field and $+0.16$ to $+0.29$
against the latent scene at the crossing values of $\kappa$), while ZFP,
whose block-floating-point quantisation does not sort by spatial
frequency, stays orthogonal under both references ($|\rho| \le 0.03$) and
reproduces $\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-1$ to a few tenths of a percentage point.
This behaviour is consistent with the frequency-selective nature of the
wavelet coder, whose error shares the spectral shape of a non-deconvolving
transfer's error at either reference, whereas ZFP remains close to
orthogonal in this experiment; two codecs do not establish a rule for
coders in general. Two limits apply: the sign of $\rho$ follows from
both errors being high-frequency deficits, or from one being noise the
other removes, and does not depend on the synthetic field; its magnitude
does, because the blur here is an exact Gaussian attenuation of a power
law. The sampled range of $\kappa$ covers every class's crossing against
both denominators, so Table~\ref{tab:alpha_budget} reports a measured
inflation in each cell. The practical conclusion stands: the inflation
figures must be measured, and the direction of the error one makes by
computing them depends on the reference the product answers to.

\section{Detailed Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The verdict is conditional on the transfer, by about a factor of two.} Rebuilding the carrier with a PSF-aware or an inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.4$ times the bytes at the same coding RMSE against each transfer's own uncompressed carrier (Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}), more than the margin by which HEALPix leads at the loosest targets. Clough--Tocher is also the most accurate transfer tested against an analytic field, and we argue the two properties share a cause; the argument does not extend to transfers we have not swept, and a non-deconvolving transfer of Clough--Tocher's smoothness class, which would isolate deconvolution from smoothness, has not been tested;
    \item \textbf{An earlier out-of-range figure for the inverse-distance transfer was a diagnostic artefact.} A previous version reported $0.95\%$ of cells outside the range of the input and read this as overshoot; the range had been taken over the footprints' intersection while the values were taken over the whole tile. The diagnostic now takes both over the same ground, and the argument rests on the transfer error of Table~\ref{tab:kernel_ablation}, which is unaffected;
    \item \textbf{The equal-area UTM square is not pixel-for-pixel the HEALPix footprint.} It matches the tile's nominal projected area by construction and neither its exact geodesic area (UTM is conformal, and $636.7$ pixels were rounded to $637$) nor its shape (Section~\ref{sec:utm_crop}). A footprint-exact crop is a possible refinement; the fixed $2.58\times$ oversampling follows from the equal-area property and would not change;
    \item \textbf{The overhead is a parameter because only its container component is measured.} The verdict is reported over a continuous range of $\gamma=\gamma_{\mathrm{UTM}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{HPX}}$, the container cost of each side is measured at under half a percent, and granule geometry is assumed. UTM is a coordinate reference system; padding, overlap and per-tile metadata belong to a product organisation, and a native UTM COG or Zarr archive need not carry them. $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ has not been measured from complete tiled product files, nor swept over $[1.0,1.5]$ for the matched-budget protocol, and for compressed storage the overhead is assumed to apply proportionally to the compressed byte count;
    \item \textbf{The level-19 comparison was made on a cell-averaged carrier; the directly evaluated one agrees.} Section~\ref{sec:level19_direct} finds the two within $0.2\%$ in floor against the observation, with the widest departure at $1.051$, on 39 regions: for Lake Geneva the window is no longer the product the stored tile was built from (Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry}), so the rebuild returns neither carrier. Including it moves the median ratio by less than $0.001$. The kernel ablation and the resampling-error study, which also re-run a transfer, rest on the same 39; no measurement read from a stored tile is affected;
    \item \textbf{The loosest targets rest on fewer regions.} After the ladders were extended, the failing pairs are the lowest-contrast scenes: none at the two tightest RMSE targets, one at $10^{-3}$, three at $3\times10^{-3}$ and eight at $10^{-2}$, with no urban scene among the absent. Seven of eight paired tests survive the Holm correction; the $3\times10^{-3}$ target is the crossover point itself and is reported as inconclusive. The $40\%$ and $20\%$ budgets qualify on $0\%$ and $65\%$ of the regions and are withheld;
    \item \textbf{Neither reconstruction reference is the whole story.} The floor of Table~\ref{tab:representation_floor} is an upper bound on what the representation costs, since the read alone can account for it (Section~\ref{sec:operator}); an interpolating read removes two thirds of its mean squared error, the common-reference sweep has been re-scored through it (Table~\ref{tab:common_two_readers}), and the PSF-aware transfer has been run through the same protocol. Still open are the $3\times10^{-3}$ target, bracketed for $57\%$ of regions under the better read, and a read matched to the PSF transfer on the PSF tiles, which Table~\ref{tab:reader_floor} shows to be within $4\%$ of the interpolating one;
    \item \textbf{The super-resolution surcharge is measured, and our deconvolution control does not reproduce the companion's recovery.} The deconvolved carrier costs $1.27$ to $2.41$ times the Clough--Tocher one, of which at most nine percent is attributable to deconvolving; that is a measurement on real imagery. In our harness the PSF-aware reconstruction recovers $+3.0\%$ to $+6.5\%$ of the blur without noise and nothing at the measured noise level, against the $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$ the companion study reports noise-free; the added noise and a latent spectrum extrapolated to the level-20 Nyquist contribute, the depth of the cell average is excluded by measurement, and a residual gap remains unattributed. The storage price is reportable and the quality it buys is not;
    \item \textbf{The oracle-best-codec comparison is an envelope, and the crossover is codec-dependent.} Four of the five codecs held fixed on both grids reproduce the regime-dependent pattern and ZFP does not (Section~\ref{sec:same_codec}); the same-codec comparison is the operational result and the oracle protocol an upper bound on what codec selection could add;
    \item \textbf{The byte and type audit covers two regions.} Over 424 points it found no gap between reported and on-disk sizes and no disagreement with an independent decoder; it tests the adapters, which do not vary by region. The image codecs' eight-byte scale-and-offset sidecar is not charged to their sizes, and the DWT baseline charges four bytes for a scale it never writes;
    \item \textbf{The footprint mismatch is bounded, and its geometry is measured from cell centres.} The analytic control reproduces the decline of the measured gap to within $8$--$27\%$ without attributing the residual; the intersection fractions come from the convex hull of the cell centres, and each footprint's geodesic area has not been computed;
    \item \textbf{Codecs are swept over their own quality parameter}, so residual convention effects cannot be fully excluded;
    \item \textbf{Single band, single sensor, one epoch.} Only Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m} is evaluated, with no derived product: how the measured errors propagate into an index such as NDVI needs B08 and a common mask, and lossy compression is documented to change downstream land-cover classification at ratios inside the range studied here~\cite{zabala2011lossy}. Gridded model output is a different problem: it is already a cell average, so there is no transfer error to charge and the denominator of $\kappa$ would be the model's own numerical error; its dominant redundancy is temporal, which none of the codecs favoured here exploits; its HEALPix level is a free design parameter, which turns $\gamma_\star$ into a design curve; and conservation adds a constraint an RMSE budget does not enforce;
    \item \textbf{The HEALPix side uses a deliberately simple resampling.} Clough--Tocher is PSF-agnostic; the operational PSF-aware resampler~\cite{delouis2026resample} is expected to place more genuine high-frequency detail in the carrier, so the HEALPix-side ratios here are optimistic relative to it. We previously argued the margin was bounded because the imagery carries only $0.21\%$ of its variance above half the native Nyquist, and withdraw that argument: low power in the observed field bounds nothing about the latent one, and the fitted spectra put $2.3\%$ (forest) to $31.1\%$ (urban) of the latent variance above the native Nyquist (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget});
    \item \textbf{Forty regions, one tile each.} Independent replicate tiles within a region would be needed to separate scene-to-scene from tile-to-tile variability. Window widths differ between regions and are recorded per region; nothing measured depends on them. The image codecs receive a 16-bit requantization with an RMSE floor of $5.7\times10^{-6}$ in median, so the reported lossless points are bit-identical after that step only;
    \item \textbf{Ratios over near-constant scenes are fragile.} Over deep water at the loosest targets, compressed files fall below \SI{10}{kB}; individual water-region ratios there range from $0.07$ to $6.1$ and are uninformative, though the class medians behave like the others;
    \item \textbf{Learned compression is not evaluated.} Neural codecs~\cite{balle2017endtoend,valsesia2019onboard} lead the rate--distortion frontier on natural imagery; comparing two grids with one needs models trained symmetrically on both, a study of its own. The ratios here characterise generic codecs;
    \item \textbf{Codec choice was evaluated on compression efficiency alone.} JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL support partial, range-based reads of a chunk, ZFP at the block level, and SZ3 and the DWT baseline do not; if partial access is an operational requirement, the strongest compressor is not automatically the right choice;
    \item \textbf{Global-sphere behaviour is not measured} (Appendix~\ref{app:global_sphere}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Towards Global-Sphere Compression}
\label{app:global}
\label{app:global_sphere}
All measurements use one dominant level-10 parent tile per scene
($1024\times1024$ level-20 cells), large enough to amortise per-chunk
codec overhead and small enough to remain a practical chunking unit. The
coarsest HEALPix parent is level 0: with $N_{\mathrm{pix}}(\ell)=12\cdot4^{\ell}$,
level 0 has exactly the 12 base faces and level 1 already has 48. A single
level-0 face carried down to level 20 would hold $4^{20}\approx10^{12}$
cells, far too large to compress as one array.

A full-sphere product would therefore need a multi-chunk hierarchy --- for
example many level-10-rooted tiles per base face, each handled as in this
paper --- together with explicit treatment of the 12 base-face boundaries,
which are not simple raster seams. We have not measured full-sphere
behaviour and do not claim that the regional pattern extends to it
unchanged; whether it does, and how base-face boundaries should be handled,
is a question for future work. A full-sphere treatment would also open a
representation the tile-local codecs used here cannot exploit: multiscale
bases defined on the sphere itself, such as needlets~\cite{marinucci2008needlets},
and the sphere-adapted scattering representations already used to model
non-Gaussian structure in full-sky HEALPix data~\cite{delouis2022scattering},
model exactly the cross-scale dependency that Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}
identifies as the source of the compressibility, on a support no chunk
boundary interrupts.
