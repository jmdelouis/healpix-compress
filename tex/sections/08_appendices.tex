\section{Supplementary Methodology}
\label{app:method}

\subsection{Two Literatures Behind the Expectation}
\label{app:literatures}
Approximation theory says the following. The $L_2$ error of
representing a continuous field by samples on a grid of step $T$ is
set, to leading order, by an error kernel whose decay follows the
representation's approximation order. Refining the step therefore buys
a predictable and bounded amount of
accuracy~\cite{blu1999fourier,blu1999approximation,unser2000sampling}.
On a field the instrument has already band-limited, the added samples
are almost determined by their neighbours. Coding theory adds the
second half. Wavelet coefficient magnitudes persist across scales, and
zerotree coders~\cite{shapiro1993ezw,said1996spiht}, hidden Markov
trees~\cite{crouse1998hmt} and parent-conditioned
coding~\cite{buccigrossi1999joint} turn that persistence into bits
saved. An added octave that carries almost no variance is the case
those models handle best, which is why the oversampling penalty is
expected to be much smaller than the factor of~\eqref{eq:alpha}. The
native product shows only part of that octave. A median of $0.21\%$ of
each scene's variance lies above half the native Nyquist
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), while the octave level~20 adds
over level~19 lies mostly above the native Nyquist. On the \term{carrier}, the detail that level 20 adds
(the Haar coefficients of its finest level, which are not confined to
that octave) holds $3.47\%$ of the tile's variance
(Section~\ref{sec:level_choice}), two orders of magnitude more than a
spectral extrapolation of the $0.21\%$ suggests.

\subsection{A Direct Test of a Quadtree-Native Coder}
\label{app:quadtree}
A pyramid was built on the quadtree itself and coded with a
parent-conditioned context model in the spirit
of~\cite{buccigrossi1999joint}. Its ideal-entropy rate is $1.29\times$
the bytes the best generic codec spends at equal distortion on the same
40 tiles with the exact quadtree Haar, and $0.93\times$ with a
quadtree-native CDF~5/3 lifting.
Both are bounds that charge nothing for coding, and both lie within what
an implementation loses to its own bound. The link between scales is real
(conditioning on the parent saves $7.0\%$ of an order-0 rate, twice the
$3.2\%$ an intra-band neighbour saves) and small, and the generic codecs
already run a better wavelet on this very quadtree.

\subsection{What the Container Costs}
\label{app:container}
The coverage and datacube model behind a chunked archive sets what a
container must hold beyond the samples~\cite{baumann2025datacube}. One
level-20 chunk in a Zarr~v3 store~\cite{moore2023zarr} costs
\SI{737}{\byte} of group and array metadata, $0.018\%$ of the raw tile;
the cell identifiers of a contiguous NESTED block are implicit. A
single-tile deflate Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF~\cite{ogc_cog2023} of the
\term{equal-area crop} costs \SI{8461}{\byte} of header, tag directory and
near-empty payload, $0.52\%$ of its raw size. Container format therefore
costs at most about half a percent of the raw size on either side.
Against compressed files it weighs more: the GeoTIFF container is
$10\%$ of the $2\%$ shared budget and the Zarr metadata $0.9\%$.
The sizes compared in this paper are those of the codec streams, without
container, so at loose targets a fixed container cost would move
the ratio towards the lighter container. A major remaining
contribution to a layout overhead is granule geometry, whose sample-area
effect Section~\ref{sec:overhead} constrains.

\section{Supplementary Controls}
\label{app:controls}

\subsection{Same-Ground and Shifted-Crop Controls}
\label{app:support}
Both grids are restricted to the ground they share: the native pixels
whose centre lies in the tile and the cells whose centre lies in the
crop, $74$--$83\%$ of each raster. Outside it each raster is replaced by
the harmonic extension of its own values on the boundary, so the codecs
see rasters of the main comparison's shape, and the error is measured on
the shared ground only. The same ladders on the unmodified rasters
reproduce the per-region ratios of the main comparison to within
$5\%$. Against them, the same-ground ratio moves by $-0.6\%$ to $+2.1\%$
in median over the five \term{targets}: a median of per-region changes,
whereas the two rows of Table~\ref{tab:support_controls}, each over its
own regions, differ by $-2\%$ to $+5\%$. A constant fill, which leaves a step
along the boundary, changes no median by more than $0.01$, and scoring
the unmodified rasters on the shared ground alone moves the ratio by at
most $0.7\%$ in median. At $10^{-2}$ the same-ground median is $0.97$
over 32 regions ($95\%$ confidence interval $0.93$--$1.01$, $p=0.54$,
21 below parity), against $0.93$ over the full footprints. Over 40 regions, re-cutting the \term{UTM crop}
at eight offsets of up to 16 native pixels, with the HEALPix side fixed,
changes each region's content by about as much as the footprint mismatch
does. The median spread of the ratio across the nine crops is $0.012$ at
the tightest \term{target} and $0.057$ at the loosest, $1\%$ of the
effect at the tightest \term{target} and larger than the effect at the
loosest, where the median ratio of these crops is $0.97$.

The spectral fit of Section~\ref{sec:support_controls} trades slope
against response width over a band of this length: urban regions return
the flattest spectra and widest responses ($\beta\approx1.2$, FWHM
$\approx\SI{13.7}{m}$), forest regions the steepest and narrowest
($2.35$, \SI{10.1}{m}), so the control uses the joint median. Over the
calmest water the fitted spectrum is nearly flat ($\beta=-0.19$ over Lake
Victoria, with $23\%$ of its variance above half-Nyquist), dominated by
sensor noise.

\subsection{The Reading Operator on Single-Frequency Fields}
\label{app:operator}
Analytic single-frequency waves are placed on the level-20 cell centres
of three regions, read on the native UTM pixels and regressed against the
same wave at those pixels; nothing is resampled, so the residual is the
operator alone. The operator is registered to well under a cell (largest
fitted displacement \SI{2.1}{\milli\metre} over ten wavelengths and four
directions). Its amplitude response is $0.99$ and above beyond
\SI{100}{m}, $0.94$ at \SI{37}{m}, $0.82$ near the native Nyquist and
$0.53$ at the level-20 Nyquist, and directional, by up to $2.29\times$
between the $45^\circ$ and $135^\circ$ directions at the finest
wavelength. The residual that the amplitude-and-phase model does not
describe is almost the whole error, $95\%$ of the RMSE at \SI{21}{m} and
essentially $100\%$ above \SI{60}{m}: reading the containing cell acts as
a sub-pixel position jitter, reproducing a linear field with an RMS error
of $0.34$ cells of gradient (against $0.289$ for a uniform read within
$\pm$ half a cell), about \SI{2.1}{m} on a \SI{10}{m} native pixel. A
\SI{6.2}{m} cell need not contain any \SI{10}{m} pixel centre, so this
operator can drop a stored cell entirely. On a band-limited field with
the spectrum of the imagery ($0.19\%$ of its variance above half the
native Nyquist frequency, against the $0.21\%$ measured on the crops),
the \term{read} alone produces an RMSE of $14.0$--$14.4\%$ of the field's
standard deviation, against a floor of about $10\%$ on the real tiles,
where the Clough--Tocher smoothing removes the gradients a jitter turns
into error.

The kernel of that \term{read} has a cost in rate. With a uniform
dead-zone quantiser in an orthonormal wavelet domain as error source,
$68.8\%$ of the error energy lies in cells the product never reads. The
product error is no smaller for it: the RMS of the transmitted error is
$1.00$ times its RMS over all cells, so a coarser step gains nothing
($0.1\%$ of the rate). The kernel reveals allocation, bits spent on cells
nothing depends on, with a bound of $1/0.308\approx3.2\times$ that a
uniform quantiser cannot reach; $54.3\%$ of the $2\,800$ coded operating
points of the extended ladders, lossless ones included, reach a cell-space RMSE below the floor of
Table~\ref{tab:floors}. A \term{read} whose kernel attenuates coding
error, such as the forward operator of the PSF-aware \term{transfer},
would let a codec target the product's distortion instead of the
\term{carrier}'s, for a product consumed through that operator only. The rate such
a codec would save is not measured here.

\subsection{Codec Audit}
\label{app:codec_audit}
The JPEG~XL sweep extends beyond its quality-level ladder, which
saturates near RMSE $10^{-3}$ on Sentinel-2 B04, with Butteraugli-distance
operating points. Every operating point was written to a file, closed,
re-opened and decoded by a reader written independently of the encoder.
Over 424 audited points (two regions, both grids, both input scenarios)
the re-read bytes were identical to those written, and the decoded array
was identical to the encoder's to the last bit. Only ZFP writes a
self-contained file: the image codecs need a float32 minimum and span
carried out of band, eight bytes the reported sizes do not charge, at
most $0.17\%$ of the smallest size reported here, and the DWT baseline
charges four bytes for a scale it never writes. ``Lossless'' JPEG~2000
and JPEG~XL are bit-identical to the 16-bit renormalization they are
handed, which imposes a floor of $5.7\times10^{-6}$ in median on both
grids. Byte counts depend on the input type: the image codecs are
indifferent within $0.3\%$, lossless JPEG~XL is $34\%$ smaller on
integer digital numbers, and the two absolute-error codecs move by two
orders of magnitude; both grids are fed identically, so no comparison is
affected. Converting to integer DN costs the \term{native crop}
essentially nothing (its values already sit on the DN lattice) and costs
the HEALPix \term{carrier} $2.9\times10^{-5}$, the uniform quantisation
error of the $10^{-4}$ step, $29\%$ of the tightest \term{target}: an
archive that stores integer DN must keep the \term{carrier} in floating
point or accept a quantisation the native product never pays.

\subsection{What the Verdict Owes to Censored Sweep Points}
\label{app:censoring}
A \term{target} that a codec's coarsest setting already satisfies is met
without being measured, and the size of that setting is only a bound.
With the original ladders the oracle codec was censored on 16 of the 40
regions at the loosest \term{RMSE target}, so every ladder is extended at
its coarse end (Section~\ref{sec:codecs}) and the rule of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol} applies. Table~\ref{tab:censoring} reports
the verdict under three readings of the extended sweeps: every censored
pair kept as a bound; the qualifying pairs alone, the values reported in
the paper; and the interval that bounds the median of all 40 regions once
each censored value moves in the only direction it can. At every
reported \term{target} the three readings agree in sign, the two medians
differ by at most $0.01$, and the interval lies on one side of parity
except at $3\times10^{-3}$, where the paper reports no verdict. The
\emph{Sure} columns count the regions whose side of parity holds under any
reading; the sign test in the last column assigns every undetermined
region to the side least favourable to the verdict and separates the
grids at every reported \term{target} but $3\times10^{-3}$. The UTM side
carries $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.3$.

\subsection{Is Each Window Still the Product Its Tile Came From?}
\label{app:window_radiometry}
Three measurements re-run a \term{transfer} from the native window, which
must therefore still be the product its tile came from. Each stored tile
is compared with its own window sampled at the tile's cell centres by
nearest pixel. The difference between the two samplings is additive noise
on the regressor. If that noise is uncorrelated with the signal, it
attenuates the regression slope by exactly the squared correlation, so $\mathrm{slope}/\mathrm{corr}^2$ is one whatever
the noise and the contrast, while a different acquisition carries a gain
and an offset of its own. Across the 40 regions the ratio has median
$1.0032$, all in $[0.982, 1.011]$, over correlations from $0.910$ to
$0.999$. A second test, exact and free of that assumption, carries the argument: each tile's file holds the
equal-area \term{UTM crop} written in the same call, which must agree to
the bit with the window's pixels over the same ground, and all 198 stored
tiles do. The crop box is recomputed from the tile's centroid, which
survives any re-cutting of the window; in the deposited run no window was
re-acquired after its tile was built (0 of the 40 windows), and the
comparison at the recorded indices reports 0 mismatched tiles.

\subsection{The Two Level-19 Carriers}
\label{app:level19_direct}
A \term{carrier} built by Clough--Tocher at level 19 holds the
interpolant at the level-19 cell centres; the aggregate holds its mean at
the four level-20 centres inside each cell. The two coincide only for a
function affine over a cell, so their gap is the curvature of the
interpolant at the \SI{12.4}{m} scale. They differ by $6.3\%$ of a
scene's standard deviation in RMS, $0.27$ times the level-19 floor
itself, yet their floors against the observation are the same,
$8.59\times10^{-3}$ for the direct \term{carrier} against
$8.56\times10^{-3}$ for the aggregate, a median ratio of $1.002$, within
$5\%$ on 38 of the 40 regions, with the direct \term{carrier} lower on 15
of them and a widest departure of $1.059$. Against the level-20 floor the
two coarser \term{carriers} cost $2.25$ and $2.24$. Rebuilding from the
window makes this measurement depend on the window still holding the
product its tile was built from (Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry}).

\section{Reading a Target Against the Error the Product Already Carries}
\label{app:error_budget}\label{sec:error_budget}
Whether an \term{RMSE target} is an aggressive or a conservative place to
compress depends on how far the stored product already sits from what it
claims to represent. Clough--Tocher reproduces the \emph{observed} field,
the scene as the instrument's response left it; a PSF-aware
\term{transfer}~\cite{delouis2026resample} represents the \emph{latent} field,
the scene before the blur, and hands the codec more high-frequency
content to pay for. This appendix measures each \term{transfer} against the
\term{reference} it answers to, then re-expresses the results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} in units of that error.

The measurement is synthetic, because the latent field is not
observable. For each region an analytic random field is built from that
region's own fitted spectral slope and response width
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), convolved exactly with that
response and sampled at the native pixel positions to give the
observation. The truth at each \term{carrier} cell is the average of the field
over that cell's sixteen level-22 children, the cell-average estimand
the companion study reconstructs; in that study a reconstruction agrees
$2.36$ to $2.52$ times better with a cell-average \term{reference} than with a
point-sample one. Averaging four level-21 children instead moves the
blur by $2\%$ and the recovery fraction by less than a point. White
noise is added at the level Immerkaer's $3\times3$ estimator reads off
each region's own imagery, $6.3$--$13.4\%$ of the field's standard
deviation by class, because a deconvolving \term{transfer} amplifies noise as
it amplifies structure. The damping is $\lambda=10^{-3}$, that of the companion's synthetic
recovery experiments and of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}; the conversion chain of
Appendix~\ref{app:large_scale_trial} uses $\lambda=0.1$. In
Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}, \emph{Blur} is the RMSE between the
observed and latent fields; Clough--Tocher is scored against the observed
field and the PSF-aware \term{transfer} against the latent field; the last two
columns give the recovery fraction $1 - e_{\mathrm{psf}}/e_{\mathrm{blur}}$
at the measured noise level and in the noiseless control.

Clough--Tocher's error against the observed field is small, $7.5\%$ to
$14.8\%$ of a scene's standard deviation by class (median
$2.8\times10^{-3}$ in reflectance), and largely the noise it faithfully
\term{transfers}: the noiseless control puts the interpolation itself at
$3.9\%$ to $10.3\%$. Its error against the \emph{latent} field is
several times larger, $1.0\times10^{-2}$ in median, because the whole
instrument blur stays in place: $12.5\%$ of the latent standard
deviation over forest and $53.8\%$ over urban scenes. These are the two
denominators. The strict reading is the first: Clough--Tocher promised
the observed field and delivered it to $2.8\times10^{-3}$. The
operative reading for a DGGS
archive is often the second, because a reader who treats the HEALPix
cube as ``the data'' compares it to the scene and already carries the
blur.

Table~\ref{tab:alpha_budget} reports both readings. $\kappa$ is the
compression \term{target} as a fraction of the denominator, at the tightest
reported \term{target} where HEALPix is the smaller representation; the
inflation columns give the \emph{measured} growth of the product's total
error, with the orthogonal value $\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}-1$ in parentheses.
The choice of denominator moves $\kappa$ at the crossing by a factor of
three to four ($0.32$ against $0.88$ over water, $0.30$ against $1.07$
over agriculture). In general
$D_{\mathrm{total}}/D_0=\sqrt{1+\kappa^2+2\rho\kappa}$ with $\rho$ the
normalised inner product of the two errors, and
Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term} below finds $\rho$ neither zero nor of one
sign.
Against the latent scene, HEALPix becomes the smaller representation at
$\kappa=0.23$ over urban scenes, $0.30$ over agriculture, $0.32$ over
water and $0.76$ over forest, where the orthogonal formula puts the
inflation of the total error at $2.6\%$, $4.3\%$, $5.1\%$ and
$25.6\%$; the measured values, with the cross term of
Appendix~\ref{app:cross_term}, are $4.9\%$, $8.5\%$, $9.8\%$ and
$39.8\%$, about twice the orthogonal ones. Against the own estimand the
same crossings cost $\kappa=1.23$, $1.07$, $0.88$ and $1.57$: three of
the four lie above one, so on that yardstick the compression error at the
crossing exceeds the \term{transfer} error itself, and no crossing is cheap.
The choice between the two readings is a statement about the intended
use of the archive.

The deconvolving \term{transfer} is measured on the same scenes. Without
noise, the PSF-aware reconstruction recovers $+13.8\%$ of the blur over
agriculture, $+15.6\%$ over urban scenes and $+14.0\%$ over water
(class medians of the per-region fraction,
Table~\ref{tab:resampling_error}), at or just below the range the
companion study reports, $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$, under the same recovery
definition and damping. Over forest the median is $-3.5\%$. The split is not one of
land cover but of response width. The synthetic scene of each region
carries the width fitted to that region's imagery, and the fit returns
narrow responses over the smoothest scenes, down to \SI{7.4}{m} FWHM.
On the 29 regions whose fitted width is \SI{11}{m} or more, every
reconstruction recovers, by $8\%$ to $20\%$; on the 11 regions below
\SI{11}{m}, where the blur is a few $10^{-4}$ and the inversion's own
error exceeds it, the best recovers $+3\%$ and the rest lose
(Spearman $\rho=0.88$ between fitted width and recovery). The companion
fixes the width at the nominal \SI{12.5}{m} on every scene, which is
why its figure is uniform across classes and ours is not. With the
measured noise added, recovery is positive on 18 of the 40 regions
against 31 of the 40 without it, and the class medians fall to between
$-54.8\%$ over forest and $+3.5\%$ over urban scenes: a deconvolution
amplifies noise at the frequencies it restores, and the companion's
headline figure is a noise-free one. A stronger damping trades recovery
against that amplification; the $\lambda=0.1$ of the conversion chain
sits at another point of the trade, which is not measured here. On
matched synthetic scenes the companion finds the preferred damping
moving to $0.1$--$1$ as white noise grows, and recommends choosing it on
the \term{target} sensor~\cite{delouis2026resample}. Two differences with the companion's
protocol remain untested here, its \SI{10}{m} square detector aperture,
which the scalar Gaussian inverse does not model, and its high-resolution
image textures in place of a power-law field; at the nominal width and
without noise the two harnesses agree without them.

\subsection{Is the Compression Error Orthogonal to the Transfer Error?}
\label{app:cross_term}\label{sec:cross_term}
Writing $e_0 = x - \text{truth}$ for the \term{transfer} error on the
\term{carrier} cells and $e_c = \tilde{x} - x$ for the compression error,
the exact relation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{D_{\mathrm{total}}}{D_0} = \sqrt{1 + \kappa^2 + 2\rho\kappa},
\qquad \rho = \frac{\langle e_0, e_c\rangle}{D_0 D_c},
\label{eq:cross_term}
\end{equation}
of which quadrature is the $\rho = 0$ case. We measure $\rho$ on the
synthetic scene above, where both errors are available exactly, grouping
operating points (region, codec and setting) around each reported
$\kappa$ within a factor of $1.5$; the grouped values are in the data
deposit. The sign depends on the \term{reference}. Against the
\emph{observed} field, $\rho$ is negative and reaches $-0.33$ at
$\kappa=1$: the \term{transfer} error is dominated by the sensor noise the
\term{transfer} carried (what takes Clough--Tocher from $3.9$--$10.3\%$ of
a scene's standard deviation without noise to $7.5$--$14.8\%$ with it),
and a lossy coder discards part of that noise. Quadrature then
\emph{overstates} the damage, by $11$ percentage points at $\kappa=0.5$
and $24$ at $\kappa=1$. Against the \emph{latent} scene $\rho$ rises to
$+0.17$ at $\kappa=0.2$: the \term{transfer} error is a deficit of
high-frequency content, and a wavelet coder's error is another, so the
orthogonal formula \emph{understates} the inflation, by $2.5$ percentage
points at $\kappa=0.2$ and $6.6$ at $\kappa=0.5$. The wavelet coder
carries the entire effect ($\rho = -0.18$ against the observed field and
$+0.16$ to $+0.29$ against the latent scene at the crossing values of
$\kappa$), while ZFP, whose block-floating-point quantisation does not
sort by spatial frequency, stays orthogonal under both \term{references}
($|\rho| \le 0.03$); two codecs do not establish a rule for coders in
general.

\section{Detailed Limitations}
\label{app:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The verdict is conditional on the transfer, by about a factor of two.} Rebuilding the \term{carrier} with a PSF-aware or an inverse-distance \term{transfer} costs $1.2$ to $2.5$ times the bytes at the same coding RMSE (Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}), more than the margin by which HEALPix leads at the loosest \term{targets}. The argument does not extend to \term{transfers} we have not swept, and a non-deconvolving \term{transfer} as smooth as Clough--Tocher, which would isolate deconvolution from smoothness, has not been tested;
    \item \textbf{The equal-area UTM square matches the tile's nominal projected area}, neither its exact geodesic area (UTM is conformal, and $636.7$ pixels were rounded to $637$) nor its shape (Section~\ref{sec:utm_crop}); the fixed $2.58\times$ oversampling would not change under a footprint-exact crop;
    \item \textbf{The overhead is a parameter because only part of it is measured.} The container cost of each side is measured at about half a percent of the raw size or less and the overlap of the Sentinel-2 tiling at $21\%$ from its published geometry, while padding and metadata are assumed. $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ has not been measured from complete tiled product files, nor swept over $[1.0,1.5]$ for the \term{matched-budget} protocol, and for compressed storage the overhead is assumed to apply proportionally to the compressed byte count;
    \item \textbf{Neither reconstruction reference is the whole story.} The floor of Table~\ref{tab:floors} is an upper bound on what the representation costs, since the \term{read} alone can account for it (Section~\ref{sec:operator}). What stays open is the $3\times10^{-3}$ \term{target}, bracketed for $57\%$ of regions under the interpolating \term{read}. The PSF-aware \term{carrier} is not scored against the observation: read by the nearest cell or bilinearly it would be charged for the content it is built to restore, and read through its own forward operator it reproduces the pixels it was fitted to (Section~\ref{sec:operator}); a fair score needs pixels held out of the fit. A comparison of the two \term{transfers} at equal reconstruction error needs a synthetic scene with a known latent field, degraded by the instrument response, transferred by both, compressed, and scored against that field; it is the first experiment a follow-up should run;
    \item \textbf{The super-resolution surcharge is measured; the recovery it buys is reproduced only without noise.} In our synthetic harness the PSF-aware reconstruction recovers $8\%$ to $20\%$ of the blur on the regions whose fitted response width is at or above \SI{11}{m} (the companion reports $15.4\%$ to $19.6\%$), loses on the narrower ones, and keeps little of it once noise is added at the level measured on the imagery (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget}). The storage price is reportable; the quality it buys on real tiles is established only by the companion study;
    \item \textbf{The byte and type audit covers two regions.} Over 424 points it found no gap between reported and on-disk sizes and no disagreement with an independent decoder; it tests the adapters, which do not vary by region;
    \item \textbf{The footprints are compared through convex hulls.} The intersection fractions come from the convex hull of the cell centres, and geodesic areas have not been computed. The tile's raster axes run at about $45^\circ$ to the native ones, and what that orientation costs row-and-column codecs is not measured;
    \item \textbf{Codecs are swept over their own quality parameter}, so residual convention effects cannot be excluded;
    \item \textbf{Single sensor, one epoch.} Sentinel-2 B04 at \SI{10}{m} carries the comparison and B08 only the NDVI check of Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}, whose classes are thresholds rather than a trained classifier; lossy compression is documented to change downstream land-cover classification at ratios inside the range studied here~\cite{zabala2011lossy}. Gridded model output is a different problem (Section~\ref{sec:shared_grid});
    \item \textbf{The HEALPix side uses a deliberately simple resampling.} Clough--Tocher is PSF-agnostic, so the \term{carrier} holds no more than the product. Low power in the observed field bounds nothing about the latent one: the fitted spectra put $2.3\%$ (forest) to $31.1\%$ (urban) of the latent variance above the native Nyquist (Appendix~\ref{app:error_budget});
    \item \textbf{Forty regions, one tile each.} Independent replicate tiles within a region would be needed to separate scene-to-scene from tile-to-tile variability;
    \item \textbf{Ratios over near-constant scenes are fragile.} Over deep water at the loosest \term{targets}, compressed files fall below \SI{10}{kB} and individual ratios range from $0.07$ to $6.1$, though the class medians behave like the others;
    \item \textbf{Learned compression is not evaluated.} Comparing two grids with a neural codec~\cite{balle2017endtoend,valsesia2019onboard} needs models trained symmetrically on both, a study of its own;
    \item \textbf{Codec choice was evaluated on compression efficiency alone.} JPEG~2000 and JPEG~XL support partial, range-based reads of a chunk, ZFP at the block level in its fixed-rate mode only (the fixed-accuracy mode swept here writes blocks of variable size and would need an index), SZ3 and the DWT baseline neither; where partial access is required, the strongest compressor may be the wrong choice;
    \item \textbf{Global-sphere behaviour is not measured} (Section~\ref{sec:grid_property}).
\end{itemize}
