\subsection{One Illustrative Operating Point: a 30\% Granule Overhead}
\label{sec:rd_illustrative}

The tables that follow evaluate the comparison at
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$, the granule-overhead scenario of
Section~\ref{sec:overhead}. Every verdict in them is the curve of
Table~\ref{tab:gamma_continuous} read at one abscissa: HEALPix is the
smaller representation at a target exactly where $1.30$ exceeds the
$\gamma_\star$ of that target, which is why the sign changes between
$10^{-3}$ and $3\times10^{-3}$ and nowhere else. A reader whose product
carries a different overhead should read the curve at that value. The
scenario is easy to state in sizes: if the native product is stored with
$30\%$ of padding and metadata, the Clough--Tocher carrier is $1.59$
times its size at the tightest tolerance and $0.72$ times at the loosest,
so that HEALPix is the smaller archive only for users who accept an error
of a quarter of the scene contrast.

In Table~\ref{tab:storage_at_rmse} and in Table~\ref{tab:error_at_size}
below, each entry is a per-region HPX/UTM ratio with the oracle-best codec
chosen independently per region and per grid, the UTM side already carrying
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.3$, so that a value below one favours HEALPix.
The median is taken over the $n$ evaluable regions of 40, with the range in
brackets, and the reported $p$ is an exact or asymptotic two-sided Wilcoxon
test on the log ratio; $p^{\mathrm{Holm}}$ corrects across the whole family
of eight targets the two tables report together.

\input{tables/table_storage_at_constant_rmse}

At matched reconstruction RMSE (Table~\ref{tab:storage_at_rmse}), the
oracle-best per-region ratios show native UTM ahead at the three tightest
targets: HEALPix requires $1.23$--$1.59\times$ the size of the native
product under the illustrative overhead at RMSE targets from $10^{-3}$ to
$10^{-4}$, with Holm-adjusted $p\le0.016$. The ordering reverses at the
loosest target ($10^{-2}$), where HEALPix requires $0.72\times$ that size
over the 32 regions whose contrast admits the target (Holm-adjusted
$p<10^{-4}$). Between them, the $3\times10^{-3}$ target lies near parity
(median $0.92$, $n=37$) and is the only reported target that does not
survive correction ($p^{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.14$); it is reported as
inconclusive rather than as demonstrated equivalence, since no equivalence
margin has been defined. The excluded regions at the two loosest targets
are those listed in Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol}; they are not bounds in
disguise, and Appendix~\ref{app:censoring} shows that treating them as
such would not change any verdict.

There the budget is expressed as a fraction of the shared raw HEALPix tile
size, \SI{4194304}{\byte}, and the UTM side receives that budget divided by
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$; the ratio compared is of reconstruction errors,
so a value below one again favours HEALPix.

\input{tables/table_error_at_constant_size}

At matched storage budget (Table~\ref{tab:error_at_size}) the same regime-dependent pattern holds, and every target survives correction. At the two more generous reportable budgets ($10\%$ and $5\%$), the reconstruction RMSE of HEALPix is $4.01\times$ and $1.33\times$ that of the native product under the illustrative overhead, so that UTM is the better choice when storage is not tightly constrained. At the tightest budget ($2\%$) the ordering reverses and HEALPix achieves $0.83\times$ the UTM RMSE over all 40 regions (Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$).

Figure~\ref{fig:ratio_boxplots} shows this transition directly, together with the region-to-region dispersion behind each median. The spread is asymmetric across land-cover classes: urban regions are the most favourable to UTM at tight targets (median $1.67$ at $10^{-4}$) and, with agriculture, the most favourable to HEALPix at the loosest one ($0.70$ and $0.69$ at $10^{-2}$), while water regions produce nearly all the extreme ratios in either direction. These extremes are a property of the scenes. Over near-constant water the compressed files fall below \SI{10}{kB}, where container overhead is a large share of the total and any resampling artefact constitutes most of the remaining signal. Medians rather than means are reported for this reason, and individual water-region ratios are treated as uninformative at the loosest targets. Figure~\ref{fig:rd_comparison} shows the corresponding absolute-value curves, aggregated by median across scenes at each target. The two views are complementary but not interchangeable, since aggregation in absolute bytes mixes scenes of different intrinsic difficulty, which the per-scene-ratio protocol was designed to avoid.
