\subsection{The Compressed Size in One Table}
\label{sec:headline}

The rest of this section builds the comparison one factor at a time; its
outcome is in Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} and Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}. Both
start from one quantity: the compressed size of the HEALPix tile divided by
the compressed size of the area-matched native UTM square, at the same
reconstruction error, with no storage overhead charged to either side. A
value of $2$ means the HEALPix representation takes twice the bytes; $1$
means the two are the same size. The five targets run from a quarter of a
percent to a quarter of a typical scene's contrast, the median standard
deviation of the 40 tiles, $0.04$ in reflectance. The table gives, per
target, that ratio for the Clough--Tocher carrier the paper uses (the
column $\gamma_\star$, since the two coincide, Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}),
the share of the raw penalty that compression recovers, the verdict over
a range of native-layout overheads, and the verdict at the illustrative
$30\%$ overhead with its corrected $p$-value; $n$ is the number of regions
whose sweeps bracket the target on both grids. The figure adds the same
tile rebuilt with the PSF-aware and inverse-distance transfers of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}, on the regions all three sweeps bracket.
Under an operational overhead $\gamma$ on the
native product every ratio at $\gamma=1$ divides by $\gamma$.

Three things follow. First, the tolerated error sets the ratio.
Uncompressed, the tile is $2.58$ times the square. Compressed, the ratio
is $2.06$ when the reconstruction must stay within $10^{-4}$ of the stored
values (a quarter of a percent of a typical scene's contrast) and falls
steadily to $0.93$ at $10^{-2}$ (a quarter of the contrast), where the
HEALPix tile is the smaller one before any overhead is charged: the
oversampling penalty is more than recovered by the codec, though only at
an error that is large for a reflectance product. Second, the kind of
scene moves the ratio less than the tolerated error does: across the
four classes the medians span $2.01$--$2.17$ at the tightest target and
$0.89$--$1.10$ at the loosest, and no class changes the picture. Third,
the way the tile was filled matters as much as everything else combined.
The Clough--Tocher transfer adds no information that was not in the
native pixels; it does smooth them. The PSF-aware transfer of the
companion study~\cite{delouis2026resample} is designed to undo part of
the instrument blur and estimate a sharper latent field, whose
reconstruction gain that study establishes under its own conditions, and
a codec asked to preserve the content it places on the tile must spend
bytes on it: the tile compresses to $2.3$--$2.6$ times the UTM square at
every target, close to the uncompressed ratio, and no plausible overhead
on the native product closes that gap. The inverse-distance average,
which restores no detail at all, costs nearly as much ($2.1$--$2.5$), so
the extra bytes are consistent with the price of leaving the smooth
interpolant (Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}). With a Clough--Tocher
carrier, the storage question is therefore a question of the tolerated
error, and Sections~\ref{sec:rd_main}--\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} show
where the answer changes sign: at $10^{-2}$ at equal layout overhead,
more exactly for any relative overhead $\gamma\ge0.93$, and one target
earlier once a $30\%$ overhead is charged to the native product. A
reader whose product carries $30\%$ of padding and metadata reads the
Clough--Tocher ratio as $1.59$ down to $0.72$ (last block of
Table~\ref{tab:synthesis}), and the PSF-aware one as $1.8$ to $2.0$.
