\subsection{The Compressed Size in One Table}
\label{sec:headline}

The rest of this section establishes the comparison one factor at a time,
and a reader who wants only its outcome can find it in
Table~\ref{tab:headline} and Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}. Both report a single
quantity: the compressed size of the HEALPix tile divided by the compressed
size of the native UTM square of the same ground area, at the same
reconstruction error, with no storage overhead charged to either side. A
value of $2$ means that the HEALPix representation takes twice the bytes; a
value of $1$ means the two are the same size.

The table's second column expresses each target as a fraction of a typical
scene's contrast --- the median standard deviation of the 40 tiles, $0.04$
in reflectance --- so that $10^{-2}$ is an error of about a quarter of the
contrast and $10^{-4}$ a quarter of a percent. The C--T column is the
Clough--Tocher carrier the paper uses, with the range of the four
land-cover class medians in brackets; the PSF and IDW columns are the same
tile rebuilt with the PSF-aware and inverse-distance transfers of
Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}, on the $n$ regions all three sweeps
bracket. Under an operational overhead $\gamma$ charged to the native
product every entry divides by $\gamma$.

Three things are read directly from the table. First, the tolerated error
sets the ratio. Uncompressed, the tile is $2.58$ times the square. Once
compressed, the ratio is $2.06$ when the reconstruction is required to stay
within $10^{-4}$ of the stored values (an error of a quarter of a percent
of the contrast of a typical scene), and it falls steadily to $0.93$ at
$10^{-2}$ (a quarter of the contrast), where the HEALPix tile is the
smaller of the two before any overhead is charged to the native product.
The oversampling penalty is therefore not merely reduced by the codec but
more than recovered, though only when the user accepts an error that is
large by the standards of a reflectance product. Second, the kind of scene
moves the ratio less than the tolerated error does. Across the four
land-cover classes the medians span $2.01$--$2.17$ at the tightest target
and $0.89$--$1.10$ at the loosest; urban and agricultural scenes lie at the
top of the range at tight targets and at the bottom at the loosest one, and
no class changes the picture. Third, and this is the point a reader might
otherwise miss, the way the tile was filled from the native pixels matters
as much as everything else combined. The Clough--Tocher column is the
carrier used throughout the paper; the transfer passes a smooth surface
through the observed pixels and reads it at the cell centres, adding
nothing that was not in the image. The PSF-aware transfer of the companion
study~\cite{delouis2026resample} does more: it also undoes part of the blur
of the instrument, so that the tile contains sharper content than the
product it came from. That content is real, but it is content, and a codec
asked to preserve it to a given error must spend bytes on it. The result is
a tile that compresses to $2.2$--$2.7$ times the UTM square at every
target, essentially the uncompressed ratio, whereas the Clough--Tocher tile
falls below one. The inverse-distance average, a transfer that restores no
detail at all, costs nearly as much ($2.1$--$2.5$), so the extra bytes are
not the price of sharpness as such but of leaving the smooth interpolant
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost} takes this apart).

The columns are therefore not interchangeable. If the archive stores a
Clough--Tocher carrier, the storage question is a question of the
tolerated error, and Sections~\ref{sec:rd_main}--\ref{sec:rd_illustrative}
show where the answer changes sign, at $10^{-2}$ with no overhead at all
and one target earlier once a $30\%$ overhead is charged to the native
product. If it stores a PSF-aware carrier, the tile costs about the
uncompressed factor of $2.58$ at any error a reflectance user would accept,
and no plausible overhead on the native product closes that gap; what the
archive buys for it is a sharper product, and that is a different trade.
Every number in the table divides by the overhead $\gamma$ of the native
layout, so a reader whose product carries $30\%$ of padding and metadata
reads the Clough--Tocher column as $1.59$ down to $0.72$, and the PSF-aware
column as $1.7$ to $2.0$.
