\subsection{The Compressed Size in One Table}
\label{sec:headline}

The rest of this section builds the comparison one factor at a time; its
outcome is in Table~\ref{tab:synthesis} and Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}. Both
start from one quantity: the compressed size of the HEALPix tile divided
by that of the area-matched \term{native UTM square}, at the same
reconstruction error, with no overhead charged to either side. The five
\term{targets} run from a quarter of a percent to a quarter of a typical
scene's contrast, the median standard deviation of the 40 tiles, $0.04$
in reflectance. Per \term{target}, the table gives that ratio for the
Clough--Tocher \term{carrier}, which is also the \term{critical overhead}
$\gamma_\star$ (Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}); the share of the raw penalty
that compression recovers; the same \term{carrier} against a native
raster given the \term{carrier}'s smoothing (HPX/sm.,
Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}); and the verdict over a range of
overheads and at the illustrative $30\%$. $n$ counts the regions whose
sweeps bracket the \term{target} on both grids. Each grid is scored
against its own uncompressed raster; Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}
scores both against the observation. Under an overhead $\gamma$ on the
native product, every ratio at $\gamma=1$ divides by $\gamma$.

Three things follow. First, at equal content compression removes most of
the oversampling, not all of it. Uncompressed, the tile is $2.58$ times
the square. Against a native raster given the same smoothing, it
compresses to $2.22$ times at $10^{-4}$, a quarter of a percent of a
typical scene's contrast, and to $1.24$ times at $10^{-2}$, a quarter of the contrast. It never crosses parity, and an analytic field sampled on
both lattices gives the same curve within $7\%$
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}).

Second, against its own unsmoothed \term{native square} the Clough--Tocher tile
goes further: $2.07$ at the tightest \term{target} and $0.93$ at the
loosest, where it is the smaller one before any overhead is charged. The
difference between the two curves is the smoothing of the
\term{transfer}, which the tile pays for in error against the
observation (Section~\ref{sec:common_reference}); the crossing belongs
to the chain, not to the grid. The kind of scene moves the ratio less
than the tolerated error does: the class medians span $1.98$--$2.17$ at
the tightest \term{target} and $0.89$--$1.10$ at the loosest.

Third, the way the tile is filled matters as much as everything else
combined. The PSF-aware \term{transfer} of the companion
study~\cite{delouis2026resample}, designed to undo part of the
instrument blur, puts small-scale content on the tile, and a codec asked
to preserve it must spend bytes on it: the tile compresses to
$2.3$--$2.6$ times the \term{UTM square} at every \term{target}, close
to the uncompressed ratio. The inverse-distance average, which restores
no detail, costs nearly as much ($2.0$--$2.5$), so the extra bytes are
consistent with the price of leaving the smooth interpolant. Under an
illustrative $30\%$ layout overhead on the native product, the
Clough--Tocher ratio reads $1.59$ down to $0.72$ (last block of
Table~\ref{tab:synthesis}) and the PSF-aware one $1.8$ to $2.0$;
Sections~\ref{sec:rd_main}--\ref{sec:rd_illustrative} show where the
verdict changes sign.
