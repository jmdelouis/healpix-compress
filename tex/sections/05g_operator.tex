\subsection{What the Reading Operator Itself Contributes}
\label{sec:operator}

The floor of Table~\ref{tab:floors} is the error of two
operators together, the Clough--Tocher transfer that places the data on
the carrier and the nearest-cell read that retrieves them.
Appendix~\ref{app:operator} characterises the read alone, on analytic
single-frequency fields placed on the cell centres of three regions with
nothing resampled: it is registered to well under a cell (largest fitted
displacement \SI{2.1}{\milli\metre}), its amplitude response is $0.99$ or
higher beyond \SI{100}{m}, and almost all of its error ($95\%$ at
\SI{21}{m}, essentially $100\%$ above \SI{60}{m}) is a residual that no
amplitude-and-phase model describes. Reading the containing cell
therefore acts as a sub-pixel positional jitter of about \SI{2.1}{m} on a
\SI{10}{m} native pixel, and this jitter is most of what the floor is
made of. On a band-limited field with the spectrum of the imagery
($0.19\%$ of its variance above half the native Nyquist frequency,
against the $0.21\%$ measured on the crops), the read alone produces an
RMSE of $14.0$--$14.4\%$ of the field's standard deviation, against a
floor of about $10\%$ on the real tiles; the two agree, because a carrier
tile is a Clough--Tocher interpolation of the scene and the smoothing
removes the gradients that a jitter turns into error. The floor is thus
an upper bound on the cost of the representation, and a reconstructor
that interpolates within the carrier lattice could lower it.
The middle block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports three reads of the
same uncompressed carriers, scored on the native pixels of the
intersection against the observation: \emph{nearest} is the containing cell; \emph{bilinear}
interpolates within the carrier's own lattice at sub-cell position;
\emph{psf} is the companion study's forward operator, reported only on
the tiles it is matched to. Ratios are paired per region before the
median is taken.


The interpolating read places each native pixel in the carrier lattice at
sub-cell precision (the local index of a NESTED block is the Morton code
of the cell's integer position on its base face, so the tile is a
$1024\times1024$ lattice whose neighbours are spatial neighbours) and
takes the bilinear combination of the four bracketing centres. On the
same tiles, mask and observation, its floor is $0.582$ times that of the
nearest read (paired median over the 40 regions, lower on every region),
so replacing the nearest-cell read by the interpolating one removes
$66\%$ of the mean squared error of the transfer-and-read floor: the
floor drops from $10.0\%$ to $5.9\%$ of a scene's standard deviation when
the read changes and nothing else does. On the PSF-transferred tiles the
same read lies within $4\%$ of the companion study's forward operator
($0.957$). A substantial part of the floor is therefore caused by the
reading operator. This is a measured difference between two chains and no
variance decomposition: the transfer and read errors need not be
orthogonal, and what the residual $1.9\times10^{-3}$ owes to the
transfer, to the read and to their interaction is not separated here.

The read has a second consequence, on rate. A nearest-cell read of a
$1024^2$ tile by the $637^2$ crop touches only $30.8\%$ of the cells
(median over 40 regions, on the intersection mask); the other cells form
the kernel of the operator, and whatever a codec writes there never
reaches the product. With a uniform dead-zone quantiser in an orthonormal
wavelet domain as error source, whose error is close to white in cell
space, $68.9\%$ of the error energy lies in cells the product never
reads. The product error is no smaller for it: the RMS of the
transmitted error is $1.00$ times its RMS over all cells, because a
gather leaves the error per read pixel unchanged, so a coarser step gains
nothing ($0.1\%$ of the rate). What the kernel reveals is allocation,
bits spent on cells nothing depends on, with a bound of
$1/0.308\approx3.2\times$ that a uniform quantiser cannot reach. Seen from
the sweep, $54.0\%$ of the $2\,800$ lossy operating points of the
extended ladders reach a cell-space RMSE below the floor of
Table~\ref{tab:floors}. The matched case behaves
differently: on the PSF-transferred tiles read through the forward
operator they were built for (floor $0.558$ times that of the nearest
read, lower on all 40 regions), the operator attenuates a white coding
error to $0.67$ of its cell-space RMS, and setting the distortion target
on the product instead of the carrier reaches the same product distortion
with $20\%$ fewer bytes (median; $4.6\%$ at the tightest quartile of
targets, $36\%$ at the loosest). Three qualifications keep this from
being a recommendation: it is a property of the reader, since a gather
has a large kernel precisely because the carrier is oversampled relative
to it; white error is the most favourable case for attenuation; and the
gain holds only for a product consumed through that operator, since
noise shaped into its stop-band stays visible to anyone who reads the
carrier as an image. What the measurement establishes is that the
distortion a carrier-grid study should target is the one its reader
transmits, and on this carrier the two differ.
