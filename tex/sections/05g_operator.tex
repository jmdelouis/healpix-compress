\subsection{What the Reading Operator Itself Contributes}
\label{sec:operator}

The floor of Table~\ref{tab:floors} is the error of two operators
together, the Clough--Tocher transfer that places the data on the carrier
and the nearest-cell read that retrieves them. Appendix~\ref{app:operator}
characterises the read alone, on analytic single-frequency fields placed
on the cell centres with nothing resampled: it is registered to well
under a cell, its amplitude response is $0.99$ or higher beyond
\SI{100}{m}, and almost all of its error is a residual that no
amplitude-and-phase model describes. Reading the containing cell acts as
a sub-pixel positional jitter of about \SI{2.1}{m} on a \SI{10}{m} native
pixel, and that jitter is most of what the floor is made of. The floor is
thus an upper bound on the cost of the representation, and a read that
interpolates within the carrier lattice can lower it.

The middle block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports three reads of the
same uncompressed carriers, scored on the native pixels of the
intersection against the observation: \emph{nearest} is the containing
cell; \emph{bilinear} places each native pixel in the carrier lattice at
sub-cell precision and takes the bilinear combination of the four
bracketing centres; \emph{psf} is the companion study's forward operator,
reported only on the tiles it is matched to. Ratios are paired per region
before the median is taken. On the same tiles, mask and observation, the
bilinear floor is $0.582$ times that of the nearest read, lower on every
region, so changing the read alone removes $66\%$ of the mean squared
error of the transfer-and-read floor: it drops from $10.0\%$ to $5.9\%$
of a scene's standard deviation. On the PSF-transferred tiles the same
read lies within $4\%$ of the companion study's forward operator
($0.957$). A substantial part of the floor is therefore caused by the
reading operator. This is a measured difference between two chains and no
variance decomposition: the transfer and read errors need not be
orthogonal, and what the residual $1.9\times10^{-3}$ owes to each is not
separated here.

The read has a second consequence, on rate. A nearest-cell read of a
$1024^2$ tile by the $637^2$ crop touches only $30.8\%$ of the cells; the
rest form the kernel of the operator, and whatever a codec writes there
never reaches the product. That misallocation does not lower the product
error, and a coarser quantisation step does not exploit it
(Appendix~\ref{app:operator}). The matched case behaves differently: on
the PSF-transferred tiles read through the forward operator they were
built for, setting the distortion target on the product instead of the
carrier reaches the same product distortion with $20\%$ fewer bytes. That
gain belongs to the reader, not to the grid, and holds only for a product
consumed through that operator. The distortion a carrier-grid study
should target is the one its reader transmits, and on this carrier the
two differ.
