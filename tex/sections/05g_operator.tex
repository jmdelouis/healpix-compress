\subsection{What the Reading Operator Itself Contributes}
\label{sec:operator}

The floor of Table~\ref{tab:floors} is the error of two operators
together: the Clough--Tocher \term{transfer} that places the data on the \term{carrier}
and the nearest-cell read that retrieves them. Appendix~\ref{app:operator}
characterises the \term{read} alone, on analytic single-frequency fields placed
on the cell centres with nothing resampled. It is registered to well
under a cell. Its amplitude response is $0.99$ or higher beyond
\SI{100}{m}. Almost all of its error is a residual that no
amplitude-and-phase model describes: reading the containing cell acts as
a sub-pixel positional jitter of about \SI{2.1}{m} on a \SI{10}{m} native
pixel, and that jitter is most of what the floor is made of. The floor is
thus an upper bound on the cost of the representation, and a \term{read} that
interpolates within the \term{carrier} lattice can lower it.

The middle block of Table~\ref{tab:floors} reports two \term{reads} of the
same uncompressed Clough--Tocher \term{carriers}, scored on the native pixels of
the intersection against the observation. \emph{Nearest} is the
containing cell. \emph{Bilinear} places each native pixel in the \term{carrier}
lattice at sub-cell precision and takes the bilinear combination of the
four bracketing centres. Ratios are paired per region before the median
is taken. On the same tiles, mask and observation, the bilinear floor is
$0.582$ times that of the nearest \term{read}, lower on every region. Changing
the \term{read} alone therefore removes $66\%$ of the mean squared error of the
transfer-and-read floor: it drops from $10.0\%$ to $5.9\%$ of a scene's
standard deviation. A substantial part of the floor is caused by the
\term{read}ing operator. This is a measured difference between two chains and no
variance decomposition: the \term{transfer} and read errors need not be
orthogonal, and what the residual $1.9\times10^{-3}$ owes to each is not
separated here. The PSF-aware tiles are not scored in this table. Their
estimand is the latent field, so a floor against the observation would
measure their distance to a field they do not aim at
(Section~\ref{sec:transfer_cost}).

The \term{read} has a second consequence, on rate. A nearest-cell read of a
$1024^2$ tile by the $637^2$ crop touches only $30.8\%$ of the cells. The
rest form the kernel of the operator, and whatever a codec writes there
never reaches the product. That misallocation does not lower the product
error, and a coarser quantisation step does not exploit it
(Appendix~\ref{app:operator}). The distortion a carrier-grid study
should \term{target} is the one its reader transmits, and on this \term{carrier} the
two differ.
