\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
The evaluation uses 40 Sentinel-2 L2A \SI{10}{m} regions, ten in each of
four land-cover classes (urban, water, forest, agriculture), from
$43.5^\circ$S to $64.1^\circ$N (Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_scenes},
Fig.~\ref{fig:region_map}). The sites are those of the companion study of
PSF-aware resampling onto HEALPix~\cite{delouis2026resample}, whose
$256\times256$-pixel patches are archived under a public dataset DOI. A
level-20 carrier tile needs a much larger native window, so each region is
re-extracted here at the same anchor and, wherever the archive still serves
it, from the same product: one L2A B04 window of $1920\times1920$ native
pixels per region, so that the two studies share sites, acquisitions and
radiometric conventions.

The native window is centred on the HEALPix tile that contains the anchor.
The bounding box of the tile is \SI{9.0}{km} across and an anchor may lie
anywhere in it, so an anchor-centred window would have to be twice as
large, more than one granule reliably covers near its edge; centring on
the tile reduces the need to the bounding box plus a resampling halo,
about \SI{11}{km}, and no image content enters this choice. A region is
admitted only if a complete level-10 parent tile lies inside its window
with halo, if the native pixels under the footprint are valid, and if the
equal-area UTM square is complete. When invalid pixels exceed $0.1\%$ of
the footprint the region is rejected; below that, isolated invalid pixels
are removed from the triangulation on the HEALPix side and interpolated
from finite neighbours on the UTM side, symmetrically, and the per-region
counts are reported with the data. The red band (B04) is then resampled
once onto the level-20 tile by Clough--Tocher interpolation
(Section~\ref{sec:resampler}), and the native B04 raster is cropped to the
equal-area square of Section~\ref{sec:utm_crop}.

\subsection{Rate--Distortion Protocol}
\label{sec:rd_protocol}
For every (scene, codec, parameter) combination we record the encoded byte
count and the reconstruction RMSE against the raw tile (level-20 carrier
or native crop). Codecs are then compared at an \emph{achieved} error or
size, never at a nominal parameter value~\cite{lindstrom2014zfp,liang2022sz3},
by interpolating $\log(\text{size})$ against $\log(\text{RMSE})$, and the
inverse, on the sorted and de-duplicated sweep points. Interpolating
through a dominated configuration could produce a target no real
configuration achieves; restricting each of the 400 (region, codec) sweeps
to its Pareto frontier removes 44 dominated points, almost all at the
coarse end of the extended ladders, and changes no reported median (at the
$10^{-3}$ target it admits one more region and moves the adjusted
$p$-value from $0.016$ to $0.010$).

Three rules govern the targets. First, the curve is the constrained
problem $R(D)=\min\{S(q): d(q)\le D\}$: when a codec's coarsest setting is
already more accurate than the target, that setting is admissible and its
size is a bound on what the codec could achieve. Second, a (region,
target) pair enters the paired comparison only when the codec the oracle
selects brackets the target with two measured points on both grids, so
that every ratio tested is an interpolated estimate. This is stricter than
asking some codec to bracket the target, because the oracle takes the
smallest admissible size and a censored codec can be the smallest. Every
ladder was therefore extended at its coarse end
(Appendix~\ref{app:censoring}) until the only failing pairs were the
lowest-contrast scenes, where the target is a large fraction of the
scene's standard deviation or exceeds it: at $10^{-2}$ the eight excluded
regions are exactly those whose standard deviation is at most $1.22$ times
the target (six lakes and two smooth forests), every included region has
at least $1.36$ times it, and at tighter targets the one to three
exclusions are the smoothest lakes. A constant fill already meets such a
target, so the comparison is void there. Excluded pairs are counted, never
bounded, and the $n$ of every table is the number that remain. Third, a
target for which fewer than $75\%$ of the regions qualify is withheld; the
$40\%$ and $20\%$ budgets ($0\%$ and $65\%$) fall under this rule. Every
reported target rests on 32 to 40 regions, and Appendix~\ref{app:censoring}
shows how each verdict would change if censored pairs were kept as bounds.
No extrapolation beyond a codec's measured range is performed.

Target RMSE values run from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$ (normalised
reflectance). Storage budgets run from $2\%$ to $40\%$ of one shared
reference, the raw size of the HEALPix tile (\num{4194304}~B), for both
grids; the UTM side receives this budget divided by $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$
when an operational overhead is assumed (Section~\ref{sec:overhead}).
Codecs with a single operating point (the two lossless variants) are
reported at their achieved point only. All reported values are medians over
the regions that qualify.

\subsection{Operational UTM Storage}
\label{sec:overhead}
Sentinel-2 and comparable products are distributed as fixed geographic
tiles (MGRS granules with overlap bands), whose padding, overlap and
per-tile metadata add storage beyond the area needed \emph{under that
granule layout}. This overhead belongs to the product organisation; UTM
as a coordinate reference system carries none of it, and a native UTM COG
or Zarr archive need not either. We use $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$ as
an illustrative point on the decision curve of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_main}; it is not an estimate of the Sentinel-2 product
overhead, which we have not measured (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), and
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1$ is the overhead-free result.

For \emph{uncompressed} storage (Table~\ref{tab:storage_accounting}),
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ multiplies the raw equal-area UTM byte count
once. For \emph{compressed} storage, applying it to the raw size alone
would assume that the padded $30\%$ of native pixels compresses to zero
bytes, which is unsafe for a codec that encodes each tile independently.
We therefore apply $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ to the \emph{compressed} byte
count of the equal-area crop and compare absolute bytes, with the raw size
of the fixed HEALPix tile ($\num{4194304}$ bytes) as the single reference
for both grids; the tile and the crop have different pixel counts
($1024\times1024$ against $637\times637$), so only absolute bytes for the
same ground are comparable. For a target RMSE, the HEALPix value is its
interpolated compressed size divided by the reference, and the UTM value
its interpolated size multiplied by $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ and divided
by the same reference. For a budget given as a share of the reference, the
UTM crop receives the budget divided by $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$; the rest
is taken by the operational padding.
