\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
The evaluation uses 40 Sentinel-2 L2A \SI{10}{m} regions, ten in each of
four land-cover classes (urban, water, forest, agriculture), spanning
latitudes from $43.5^\circ$S to $64.1^\circ$N
(Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_scenes}, Fig.~\ref{fig:region_map}). The sites are
those of a companion study of PSF-aware resampling onto
HEALPix~\cite{delouis2026resample}, whose $256\times256$-pixel patches are
archived under a public dataset DOI. A level-20 carrier tile requires a much
larger native window, so each region is re-extracted here at the same anchor
and, wherever the archive still serves it, from the same Sentinel-2 product,
so that the two studies share sites, acquisitions and radiometric
conventions.

The native window is centred on the HEALPix tile that contains the anchor
rather than on the anchor itself. The bounding box of the tile is
\SI{9.0}{km} across and an anchor may lie anywhere within it, so that an
anchor-centred window would have to be twice as large, more than a single
granule reliably covers near a granule edge. Centring on the tile reduces
the requirement to the bounding box plus a resampling halo, about
\SI{11}{km}; no image content is consulted in this choice. A region is
admitted only if a complete level-10 parent tile lies inside its window with
halo, if the native pixels under the footprint are valid, and if the
equal-area UTM square is complete. It is rejected rather than repaired when
invalid pixels exceed $0.1\%$ of the footprint. Below this threshold,
isolated invalid pixels are removed from the triangulation on the HEALPix
side and interpolated from their finite neighbours on the UTM side,
symmetrically on both grids, and the per-region counts are reported with
the data. The red band (B04) is then resampled once onto the level-20
carrier tile by Clough--Tocher interpolation (Section~\ref{sec:resampler}),
and the native B04 raster of the same region is cropped to the equal-area
square of Section~\ref{sec:utm_crop}.

\subsection{Rate--Distortion Protocol}
\label{sec:rd_protocol}
For every (scene, codec, parameter) combination we measure the encoded byte
count and the reconstruction RMSE against the corresponding raw tile
(level-20 carrier or native crop). Two matched-quality comparisons are then
derived from the raw sweep, following the principle recommended for this
kind of evaluation~\cite{lindstrom2014zfp,liang2022sz3}: codecs are never
compared at a nominal parameter value, only at an \emph{achieved} error or
size.

The comparisons interpolate $\log(\text{size})$ against $\log(\text{RMSE})$,
and the inverse, directly on the sorted and de-duplicated sweep points.
Interpolating through a dominated configuration would produce a target that
no real configuration achieves. We verified that this does not occur:
restricting each of the 400 (region, codec) sweeps to its Pareto frontier
removes two dominated points in total, both on the HEALPix side, and changes
no reported median, range or $p$-value. Two further rules govern the
evaluation of targets. First, the curve is treated as the constrained
problem $R(D)=\min\{S(q): d(q)\le D\}$: when the coarsest setting of a codec
is already more accurate than the target, that setting is admissible and its
size is reported as a bound, rather than the region being dropped for lack
of a bracketing pair. An earlier version of the analysis required bracketing
and consequently discarded four to six regions at the two loosest RMSE
targets, which were precisely the smoothest scenes and the targets at which
the verdict changes sides. Second, the fraction of regions for which the
target is bracketed on both grids is reported per target, and any target
below $75\%$ is withheld: the $40\%$ and $20\%$ storage budgets ($0\%$ and
$18\%$ bracketed) are excluded on this criterion, as they would be sets of
bounds rather than estimates. Every target reported below is evaluated on
all 40 regions, with $85$--$100\%$ bracketing. No extrapolation beyond the
measured range of a codec is performed, and an interpolated point remains an
estimate between two measured configurations.

Target RMSE values range from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$ (normalised reflectance
units). Storage budgets range from $2\%$ to $40\%$ of a single shared
reference, the raw size of the HEALPix tile (\num{4194304}~B), for both
grids; the UTM side receives this budget divided by $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$
when an operational overhead is assumed (Section~\ref{sec:overhead}). Codecs
with a single operating point (the two lossless variants) are reported at
their achieved point only. All reported values are medians across the 40
regions.

\subsection{Operational UTM Storage}
\label{sec:overhead}
Sentinel-2 and comparable operational products are distributed as fixed
geographic tiles (MGRS $\SI{110}{km}\times\SI{110}{km}$ granules with a
$\SI{9.8}{km}$ overlap band), whose padding, overlap and per-tile metadata
add storage beyond the area actually needed \emph{under that granule
layout}. This overhead is a property of the Sentinel-2/MGRS product
organisation, not of UTM as a coordinate reference system; a native UTM COG
or Zarr archive need not carry it at all. We adopt a commonly used
illustrative value, $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$, as a stated modelling
assumption for one layout rather than a value re-measured from complete
product files (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1$ comparison is the grid-intrinsic result, and
$1.30$ is one scenario among others.

For \emph{uncompressed} storage (Table~\ref{tab:storage_accounting}),
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ is applied once to the raw equal-area UTM byte
count. For \emph{compressed} storage, applying it to the raw size alone
would assume that the padded $30\%$ of native pixels compresses to zero
additional bytes, which is not a safe assumption for a codec that encodes
each tile independently. We therefore apply $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ to the
\emph{compressed} byte count of the equal-area crop and compare in absolute
bytes, using the raw size of the fixed HEALPix tile ($\num{4194304}$ bytes)
as the single shared reference for both grids. The tile and the crop of the
same ground area have different pixel counts ($1024\times1024$ against
$637\times637$), so only absolute operational bytes for the same ground are
comparable. For a target RMSE, the reported HEALPix value is its
interpolated compressed size divided by the reference, and the reported UTM
value is its interpolated size multiplied by $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$ and
divided by the same reference. For a storage budget expressed as a share of
the reference, the UTM crop receives the budget divided by
$\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$, the remainder being assumed to be consumed by the
operational padding.
