\subsection{Which Codec to Use on a HEALPix Chunk}
\label{sec:codec_choice}

An operating point here is one (region, target) pair over the 40 regions
and the five RMSE plus five budget targets, four hundred in all, retained
when at least one codec is evaluable there. Wins sum to the number of
points by construction, since each point has exactly one best codec, while
\emph{evaluable} counts the points where a codec produced a value at all.

\input{tables/table_codec_ranking}

Table~\ref{tab:codec_ranking} aggregates every matched-quality and
matched-size operating point on the HEALPix side and ranks the codecs by how
often each is the best choice. The 400 operating points comprise the 40
regions at all ten swept targets, including the two storage budgets withheld
from Tables~\ref{tab:storage_at_rmse}--\ref{tab:error_at_size} for
insufficient bracketing, since a per-point ranking does not require the
target to be bracketed on both grids. JPEG~2000 is best at 196 points and
JPEG~XL at 131, against 35 for ZFP, 28 for SZ3 and 10 for the DWT+Zstandard
baseline; the mean ranks are $1.63$, $2.35$, $4.55$, $2.78$ and $3.66$.
Every codec is evaluable at almost every point, so the ordering is not an
artefact of sweep coverage. The robust conclusion is that JPEG~2000 and
JPEG~XL form the leading pair, rather than a strict ordering between the
two: the counts are descriptive, the points are not independent (the same
regions recur across targets), and no BD-rate summary, which would order the
pair properly, has been computed. This ranking is a statement about codecs,
not about grids: the same pair leads on the native UTM side, and
Section~\ref{sec:same_codec} reports what each codec says about the grid
comparison when it is held fixed on both sides.

The byte-level audit of every encoded point and the meaning of ``lossless''
after the 16-bit requantisation required by the image codecs are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:codec_audit}.
