\subsection{Raw, Uncompressed Storage Favours Native UTM}
\input{tables/table_storage_accounting}

Table~\ref{tab:storage_accounting} gives the raw storage accounting. The
HEALPix level-20 tile and the equal-area UTM crop of
Section~\ref{sec:utm_crop} are both fixed-size representations of the same
nominal ground area, so their raw byte counts are geometric constants, the
same for every scene. At $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1$ the HEALPix tile is
$2.584\times$ larger than the crop, exactly $1024^2/637^2$ on every region
(its range over the 40 regions is $2.584$ to $2.584$). It matches the
analytic factor $\alpha_{\mathrm{HPX/native}}\approx2.58$ of
\eqref{eq:alpha} because the crop side was derived from it. This is the
geometry-only result and the only exact one in the paper. Under the
illustrative $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}=1.30$ scenario the gap narrows and
stays open: the uncompressed tile is still $1.99\times$ the size of the crop
scaled by $\gamma_{\mathrm{tile}}$. A $30\%$ overhead cannot offset a
$2.58\times$ oversampling. The three rows compare float32 rasters, the
form the codecs sweep; the native product is distributed as 16-bit
integers, and stored in that form the crop is half the size again, so the
tile is $5.17\times$ the product as it ships (last row). Only
compression, by exploiting the redundancy that oversampling represents,
can make HEALPix competitive.
