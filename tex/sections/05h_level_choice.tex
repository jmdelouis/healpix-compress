\subsection{What Level 20 Buys Over Level 19}
\label{sec:level_choice}

Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} selected level 20 on a nominal argument and
deferred the measurement. The measurement is exact rather than modelled,
for a reason specific to this grid: HEALPix cells are equal-area, so the
value that a level-19 carrier would hold in a cell \emph{is} the mean of the
four level-20 values it contains, and a NESTED block stores these four as
adjacent elements. Aggregating a stored tile in groups of four therefore
yields the level-19 carrier that the same transfer would have produced, not
a model of it.

Two quantities are separated, because a coarser carrier is charged twice
and only one of the charges concerns information. The first is what the
aggregation discards: the intra-cell variance, i.e.\ the energy of the three
detail coefficients of the exact quadtree Haar transform at level 20. It
amounts to $3.07\%$ of the variance of the tile (median over 40 regions;
$2.2\%$ in forest to $7.2\%$ in urban scenes), an RMS of $17.5\%$ of the
standard deviation of the scene, which is $1.82$ times the level-20 floor
of Table~\ref{tab:representation_floor}. The second is the level-19 floor
itself, read in the same way against the same observation: $2.23$ times the
level-20 floor (paired median). The two separate cleanly. The level-19 floor
equals the level-20 floor and the discarded energy added in quadrature to
within $7\%$ (median ratio of measured to quadrature $1.07$), and the
discarded energy alone accounts for $84\%$ of the excess variance, so that
the additional error of a level-19 carrier is the information removed by
the aggregation rather than a coarser read. Against the sweep, $88.4\%$ of
the codec operating points lie below the level-19 floor. Level 19 is not a
drop-in carrier for this product.

The result is also a caution regarding the spectral argument that made the
question appear settled the other way. The observed field carries $0.21\%$
of its variance above half the native Nyquist frequency
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), and propagating the fitted spectrum
to the level-19 Nyquist frequency places about $0.03\%$ above it, two
orders of magnitude below what the aggregation discards. Aggregation is not
a brick-wall filter at the coarser Nyquist frequency: the residual of a
$2\times2$ mean has response $1-\cos^2(k_x d/2)\cos^2(k_y d/2)$, which grows
as $(kd)^4$ and draws mainly on the shoulder of the spectrum \emph{below}
the native Nyquist frequency, where the power lies. The same fitted spectra
predict the discarded fraction well only in the ensemble median (predicted
$3.4\%$ against $3.1\%$ measured), not scene by scene, which is a further
reason for reporting the spectral model of
Section~\ref{sec:support_controls} as an ensemble parameter only.
