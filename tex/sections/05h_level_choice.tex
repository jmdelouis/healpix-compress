\subsection{What Level 20 Buys Over Level 19}
\label{sec:level_choice}

Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} selected level 20 on a nominal argument and
deferred the measurement. The measurement made here is of a specific
object, and the object should be named before its result is read. A stored
level-20 tile is aggregated in groups of four, which in the NESTED ordering
are adjacent elements and are exactly the four children of one level-19
cell; because HEALPix cells are equal-area, this is the exact quadtree Haar
transform of the tile, and the aggregate is the cell-average of the carrier
over each level-19 cell. It is not the carrier that the same transfer
would have built at level 19. The carrier stores point evaluations of the
Clough--Tocher interpolant at cell centres, and for a general interpolant
the value at the parent centre is not the mean of the values at the four
child centres. What follows therefore measures the four-child (quadtree
Haar) aggregate, which is a level-19 carrier built by Clough--Tocher
followed by a cell average. Whether it also reproduces what a directly
evaluated level-19 carrier would cost is a question the aggregate cannot
answer about itself, so the other carrier was built and measured as well
(Section~\ref{sec:level19_direct}).

Two quantities are separated, because a coarser carrier is charged twice
and only one of the charges concerns information. The first is what the
four-child aggregation discards: the intra-cell variance, i.e.\ the energy of the three
detail coefficients of the exact quadtree Haar transform at level 20. It
amounts to $3.07\%$ of the variance of the tile (median over 40 regions;
$2.2\%$ in forest to $7.2\%$ in urban scenes), an RMS of $17.5\%$ of the
standard deviation of the scene, which is $1.82$ times the level-20 floor
of Table~\ref{tab:representation_floor}. The second is the floor of the
aggregate itself, read in the same way against the same observation: $2.23$
times the level-20 floor (paired median). The two separate cleanly. That floor
equals the level-20 floor and the discarded energy added in quadrature to
within $7\%$ (median ratio of measured to quadrature $1.07$), and the
discarded energy alone accounts for $84\%$ of the excess variance, so that
the additional error of the aggregate is the information removed by the
aggregation rather than a coarser read. Against the extended sweeps,
$66.8\%$ of the codec operating points lie below that floor (a share that
depends on how far the ladders reach, and is quoted for that sweep only).
Two thirds of the useful operating range of this study therefore lies below
the error that a cell-averaged level-19 carrier already carries, which is
the sense in which level 19 is not a drop-in replacement here. Whether a
directly evaluated level-19 carrier would do better is answered next.

The result is also a caution regarding the spectral argument that made the
question appear settled the other way. The observed field carries $0.21\%$
of its variance above half the native Nyquist frequency
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), and propagating the fitted spectrum
to the level-19 Nyquist frequency places about $0.03\%$ above it, two
orders of magnitude below what the aggregation discards. Aggregation is not
a brick-wall filter at the coarser Nyquist frequency: the residual of a
$2\times2$ mean has response $1-\cos^2(k_x d/2)\cos^2(k_y d/2)$, which grows
as $(kd)^4$ and draws mainly on the shoulder of the spectrum \emph{below}
the native Nyquist frequency, where the power lies. The same fitted spectra
predict the discarded fraction well only in the ensemble median (predicted
$3.4\%$ against $3.1\%$ measured), not scene by scene, which is a further
reason for reporting the spectral model of
Section~\ref{sec:support_controls} as an ensemble parameter only.
