\subsection{What Level 20 Buys Over Level 19}
\label{sec:level_choice}

Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} chose level 20 on a nominal argument. The
object measured here is a stored level-20 tile aggregated in groups of
four, which in the NESTED ordering are adjacent elements and exactly the
four children of one level-19 cell. Because the cells are equal-area,
this is the exact quadtree Haar transform of the tile, and the aggregate
is the cell-average of the carrier over each level-19 cell. It differs
from the carrier the same transfer would build at level 19, which stores
point evaluations at cell centres. Both were built and read identically
against the same observation, on the 39 regions the window audit of
Appendix~\ref{app:window_radiometry} admits: they are different arrays,
differing by $6.0\%$ of a scene's standard deviation, yet their floors
agree to a median ratio of $1.002$ and cost $2.25$ and $2.24$ times the
level-20 floor, so what follows holds for either reading of ``the
level-19 carrier'' (Appendix~\ref{app:level19_direct} gives the
comparison and the one region it excludes). The figure quoted below is
the aggregate's.

Two quantities are separated, because a coarser carrier is charged twice
and only one charge concerns information. The first is what the
four-child aggregation discards: the intra-cell variance, the energy of
the three detail coefficients of the quadtree Haar transform at level 20.
It is $3.07\%$ of the variance of the tile (median over 40 regions;
$2.2\%$ in forest to $7.2\%$ in urban scenes), an RMS of $17.5\%$ of the
scene's standard deviation, which is $1.82$ times the level-20 floor of
Table~\ref{tab:floors}. The second is the floor of the
aggregate itself, read the same way against the same observation: $2.23$
times the level-20 floor (paired median over the 40 regions; $2.24$ over
the 39 of Table~\ref{tab:floors}, which excludes one region). The two separate cleanly: that
floor equals the level-20 floor and the discarded energy added in
quadrature to within $7\%$ (median ratio of measured to quadrature
$1.07$), and the discarded energy alone accounts for $84\%$ of the excess
variance, so the extra error of the aggregate is the information the
aggregation removed. $66.8\%$ of the sampled operating points in the
extended codec sweeps lie below that floor, a share that depends on how
far the ladders reach; a cell-averaged level-19 carrier already carries
more error than most of the settings this study swept, and in that sense
level 19 cannot replace level 20 here.

The result also cautions against the spectral argument that pointed the
other way. The observed field carries $0.21\%$
of its variance above half the native Nyquist frequency
(Section~\ref{sec:support_controls}), and propagating the fitted spectrum
to the level-19 Nyquist frequency places about $0.03\%$ above it, two
orders of magnitude below what the aggregation discards. Aggregation is no
brick-wall filter at the coarser Nyquist frequency: the residual of a
$2\times2$ mean has response $1-\cos^2(k_x d/2)\cos^2(k_y d/2)$, which
grows as $(kd)^4$ and draws mainly on the shoulder of the spectrum
\emph{below} the native Nyquist frequency, where the power lies. The same
fitted spectra predict the discarded fraction well only in the ensemble
median (predicted $3.4\%$ against $3.1\%$ measured), one more reason to
report the spectral model as an ensemble parameter only.

What the coarser carrier costs to store is measured as well
(Table~\ref{tab:level19_rd}): the direct level-19 carrier and its
cell-averaged twin are run through the codec sweep of
Section~\ref{sec:rd_protocol} and compared with the native crop and the
level-20 tile under the paper's rules. Raw, the level-19 carrier is
$0.65$ times the native crop. Compressed to equal error it stays smaller
than the crop at every target, on $85\%$ to $100\%$ of the regions, and
its ratio rises from $0.67$ at the tightest target to $0.78$ at the
loosest: compression recovers nothing for a carrier that has already
discarded samples, and at loose targets it erodes the raw advantage
instead. Against the level-20 tile the same carrier costs $0.32$ of the
bytes at the tightest target and $0.88$ at the loosest, so the
level-20 tile costs $1.13$ times the level-19 carrier at $10^{-2}$ and
$3.1$ times at $10^{-4}$. The cost of the finer carrier is the cost of its density, largest where
the codec keeps every sample and nearly gone where it keeps few. The cell-averaged carrier is $9\%$ cheaper than the
direct one at equal error (median over $195$ region--target pairs), the
price of the sharper point evaluations the transfer keeps, and it is
cheaper on every region at the four tightest targets. Its advantage grows
with the target, from $0.95$ to $0.83$ of the direct carrier's size,
which is why its column against the native crop is flat where the direct
carrier's rises: at the loosest target the aggregate compresses to
$0.9\%$ of its own raw size against $0.8\%$ for the native crop and
$1.1\%$ for the direct carrier, so the aggregate and the crop gain
compressibility at nearly the same rate as the target loosens. A $2\times2$
mean of a Clough--Tocher tile behaves, per stored value, much like the
native raster it came from; the directly evaluated carrier keeps the
sharper point values and pays for them.
