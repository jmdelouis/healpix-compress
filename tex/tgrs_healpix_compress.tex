\begin{abstract}
Storing Sentinel-2 imagery on HEALPix forces a choice between level 19,
whose \SI{12.4}{m} cells are coarser than the \SI{10}{m} product, and
level 20, whose \SI{6.2}{m} cells hold $2.58$ times as many samples per
unit area. We measure what the finer choice costs after
compression. Level-20 tiles are reshaped into rasters and
compared with equal-area native UTM squares at equal reconstruction
error, over 40 Sentinel-2 B04 regions and
five general-purpose codecs. The result is the layout overhead a native
product must carry, at each error, for the HEALPix copy to be
smaller. Coded losslessly after 16-bit requantisation, the tile takes $3.4$ times the
native bytes. As the tolerated RMSE grows from $10^{-4}$ to
$10^{-2}$, an analytic field sampled on both lattices falls from $2.17$
to $1.16$ times the native size: lossy coding removes most of the
oversampling only at loose tolerances, and never all of it.
The Clough--Tocher tile falls from $2.07$ to $0.93$ against its own
native square, at an error where coding alone changes the NDVI class
of $5$--$6\%$ of samples. The crossing is marginal: $0.97$ ($p=0.54$) on the ground both grids
share, $1.10$--$1.24$ against a native raster read through the same
interpolant. A PSF-aware transfer costs $2.3$--$2.6$ times the native
square at every tolerance. Scored against the native observation,
native UTM leads at all matched-storage budgets;
the tile keeps a size advantage only at the loosest tolerance, under an
interpolating read and a $30\%$ native overhead. Level 20
competes only at aggressive compression and with a smoothing
transfer.
\end{abstract}
