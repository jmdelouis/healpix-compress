\begin{abstract}
Storing Sentinel-2 imagery on HEALPix forces a choice between level 19,
whose \SI{12.4}{m} cells are coarser than the \SI{10}{m} product, and
level 20, whose \SI{6.2}{m} cells hold $2.58$ times as many samples per
unit area. This paper measures what the finer choice costs once both
representations are compressed. Five established codecs are applied to
level-20 tiles reshaped into ordinary rasters and to native UTM squares
of equal nominal area, over 40 Sentinel-2 L2A B04 regions in four
land-cover classes, and compressed sizes are compared at equal
reconstruction error. The result is a distortion-dependent critical
overhead $\gamma_\star(e)$: the layout overhead a native product must
carry for the carrier to be the smaller representation at error $e$. A
Clough--Tocher tile falls from $2.06$ to $0.93$ times the native square
as the tolerated RMSE grows from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$, while a
PSF-aware or inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.5$ times those
bytes and never reaches parity; of the five codecs, ZFP alone shows no
crossover. Fixed-phase smoothing controls remove the crossover,
implicating transfer-induced smoothing as a major contributor. Against the native
observation the carrier starts from a floor of $4.0\times10^{-3}$ and
native UTM leads at all three reportable matched-storage budgets. The carrier becomes
competitive only at aggressive compression; among the transfers tested
this happens only with Clough--Tocher, and where the crossover falls
depends on whether the transfer-and-read error is charged to the carrier.
\end{abstract}
