\begin{abstract}
Storing Sentinel-2 imagery on a HEALPix grid forces a choice between level
19, whose \SI{12.4}{m} cells are coarser than the \SI{10}{m} product, and
level 20, whose \SI{6.2}{m} cells hold about $2.58$ times as many samples
per unit area. This paper measures what the finer choice costs in storage
once both representations are compressed. On 40 Sentinel-2 L2A B04 regions
spanning four land-cover classes and both HEALPix cell-shape regimes, five
established general-purpose codecs (ZFP, SZ3, JPEG~2000, JPEG~XL and a
wavelet baseline) are applied to level-20 tiles reshaped into ordinary
rasters and to native UTM squares of equal ground area, and sizes are
compared at equal reconstruction error. The result is one ratio: the
compressed size of the HEALPix tile divided by that of the UTM square, with
no storage overhead charged to either side. Uncompressed it is $2.58$;
compressed to an RMSE of $10^{-4}$ in reflectance, a quarter of a percent
of the contrast of a typical scene, it is $2.06$ in median, and it falls to
$0.93$ at $10^{-2}$, a quarter of that contrast, with land-cover class
moving it within $2.01$--$2.17$ and $0.89$--$1.10$. Read as a
distortion-dependent critical overhead $\gamma_\star(e)$, the
native-product overhead above which the carrier is the smaller
representation, this is a decision curve rather than a verdict, and the
illustrative $30\%$ granule-overhead scenario is one abscissa on it. Three
conditions qualify the curve, each measured here. The transfer matters
most: a PSF-aware or an inverse-distance transfer gives a tile that stays
at $2.1$--$2.7$ over the whole error range, $1.2$ to $2.4$ times the bytes
of the Clough--Tocher tile at the same coding RMSE. The codec matters next:
four of the five reproduce the crossover and ZFP does not. And the
reference matters: scored against the native observation, the transfer and
the read already cost an RMSE of $4.0\times10^{-3}$ before any compression,
and native UTM leads at every storage budget under either read. The
contribution is a quantified decision framework rather than a claim that
either grid compresses better.
\end{abstract}
