\begin{abstract}
Storing Sentinel-2 imagery on a HEALPix grid means choosing between level
19, whose \SI{12.4}{m} cells are coarser than the \SI{10}{m} product, and
level 20, whose \SI{6.2}{m} cells hold about $2.58$ times as many samples
per unit area as the native raster. This paper measures what the finer
choice costs in storage once both representations are compressed. On 40
Sentinel-2 L2A B04 regions spanning four land-cover classes and both
HEALPix cell-shape regimes, five established general-purpose codecs (ZFP,
SZ3, JPEG~2000, JPEG~XL and a wavelet baseline) are applied to level-20
tiles reshaped into ordinary rasters and to native UTM squares of equal
ground area, and sizes are compared at equal reconstruction error. The
result is one ratio: the compressed size of the HEALPix tile divided by
that of the UTM square. Uncompressed, it is $2.58$. Compressed to a
tolerated RMSE of $10^{-4}$ in reflectance, a quarter of a percent of the
contrast of a typical scene, it is $2.06$ in median, and it falls steadily
to $1.06$ at $10^{-2}$, a quarter of the contrast; land-cover class moves
it within $2.01$--$2.17$ at the tightest tolerance and $0.91$--$1.24$ at
the loosest. These figures hold for a tile filled by Clough--Tocher
interpolation, a smooth surface passed through the observed pixels. A
PSF-aware transfer, which also undoes part of the instrument blur, or a
plain inverse-distance average, gives a tile that compresses far less: its
ratio stays at $2.3$--$2.7$ over the whole error range, which is $1.2$ to
$2.2$ times the bytes of the Clough--Tocher tile at the same accuracy.
Whether the finer grid ever costs less than the native product therefore
depends on the tolerated error, on the transfer, and on the overhead that
the native tiling layout carries: under an illustrative $30\%$ granule
overhead the Clough--Tocher carrier is smaller only at the loosest
tolerance ($0.81$ of the operational UTM size) and larger at the tighter
ones ($1.23$--$1.59$). It also depends on the reference. Scored against
the native observation itself, the transfer and the read of the carrier
already cost an RMSE of $4.0\times10^{-3}$ before any compression, and
native UTM leads at every reportable target; an interpolating read halves
that floor and narrows the lead without reversing it. All of this is
expressed as a distortion-dependent critical overhead $\gamma_\star(e)$,
the native-product overhead above which the carrier becomes the smaller
representation, and offered as a decision framework rather than as a
verdict for either grid.
\end{abstract}
