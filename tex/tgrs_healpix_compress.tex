\begin{abstract}
Storing Sentinel-2 imagery on a HEALPix grid forces a choice between level
19, whose \SI{12.4}{m} cells are coarser than the \SI{10}{m} product, and
level 20, whose \SI{6.2}{m} cells hold about $2.58$ times as many samples
per unit area. This paper asks what the finer choice costs in storage once
both representations are compressed, and answers it as a decision framework
rather than a verdict for either grid. On 40 Sentinel-2 L2A B04 regions,
four land-cover classes and both HEALPix cell-shape regimes, five
established codecs (ZFP, SZ3, JPEG~2000, JPEG~XL and a wavelet baseline)
are applied to level-20 tiles reshaped into ordinary rasters and to native
UTM squares of equal nominal projected area, and their compressed sizes are
compared at equal reconstruction error. The result is expressed as a
distortion-dependent critical overhead $\gamma_\star(e)$: the layout
overhead a native product must carry for the carrier to be the smaller
representation at error $e$. This separates the measurement from any assumed
overhead, and the illustrative $30\%$ scenario is one point on the curve.

Three measured conditions govern that curve, and the paper's central
finding is how strongly they do. The transfer that fills the carrier
matters most: a smooth Clough--Tocher interpolant gives a tile that falls
from $2.06$ to $0.93$ times the native square as the tolerated RMSE grows
from $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$, while a PSF-aware or inverse-distance transfer
keeps it at $2.1$--$2.6$ throughout, $1.2$ to $2.4$ times the
Clough--Tocher bytes at equal coding RMSE. The codec matters next: four of
the five reproduce the crossover and ZFP does not. The reconstruction
reference decides the sign of the verdict: scored within each
representation the carrier can reach parity at the loosest target, but
scored against the native observation --- which charges the carrier the
transfer and the read it was given for free --- native UTM leads at every
storage budget, the carrier starting from a floor of $4.0\times10^{-3}$
before any compression. The carrier is competitive only at aggressive
compression, only for a smooth transfer, and, against the native
observation, only at the loosest target under an interpolating read; the
contribution is the framework that makes those conditions explicit.
\end{abstract}
