\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical equal-area pixelizations such as HEALPix are well suited to
global Earth observation, but their dyadic refinement restricts resolution to
powers-of-two steps. The \SI{10}{m} bands of Sentinel-2 fall between level 19
(\SI{12.4}{m}) and level 20 (\SI{6.2}{m}), so that a level-20 carrier grid
holds about $2.58$ times as many samples per unit area as the native raster.
We ask whether this redundancy remains expensive once compression and the
storage overhead of native tiling are both taken into account. On 40
Sentinel-2 L2A B04 regions spanning four land-cover classes and both HEALPix
cell-shape regimes, five established general-purpose codecs are applied to
level-20 chunks reshaped by Morton de-interleaving and to a native UTM square
of equal nominal projected area. Three findings follow in order. With no
overhead assigned to either product, native UTM is intrinsically the more
compressible representation at every tested distortion, but the penalty
decreases monotonically as the tolerated distortion grows; expressed as a
distortion-dependent \emph{critical overhead} $\gamma_\star(e)$, the
native-product storage overhead above which the carrier becomes the smaller
representation, it falls from $2.06$ at an RMSE of $10^{-4}$ to $1.06$ at
$10^{-2}$. Under an illustrative $30\%$ granule-overhead scenario, HEALPix is
therefore competitive only at aggressive compression ($0.81$ of the
operational UTM size at the loosest target, $0.83$ of its error at the
tightest budget), and native UTM leads at high fidelity (median ratios
$1.23$--$1.59$). This crossover is not a property of the grid alone. It
depends on the transfer that populates the carrier: rebuilding the tiles with
a PSF-aware or an inverse-distance transfer costs $1.2$ to $2.2$ times the
bytes at the same accuracy and leaves HEALPix ahead at no target, and the
evidence points to energy compaction rather than added content as the
mechanism. It also does not survive a common native-observation reference:
scored end-to-end against the same Sentinel-2 observation, the uncompressed
carrier already differs from it by $4.0\times10^{-3}$ in median, about $10\%$
of a scene's standard deviation, and native UTM leads at every reportable
target. Two thirds of the variance of that floor is due to the nearest-cell
read itself, a \SI{2.1}{m} positional jitter; re-scoring the sweep through
an interpolating read halves the floor and narrows the UTM lead at the
tightest budget from $1.44$ to $1.12$, with the two grids at parity at the
loosest RMSE target, but does not reverse it. The contribution is a
quantified decision framework, conditioned on distortion, product layout,
transfer and reconstruction reference, rather than a claim that either grid
compresses better.
\end{abstract}
